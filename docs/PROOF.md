# Proof

*Scripts > FreeLab > Proof* makes a PDF proof of the current font, drawn from the font's own outlines in FontLab: nothing is exported or installed, and the font is not changed. FontLab 9 has a *Notepad* panel for specimens and PDF proofs; FontLab 7 has none.

## What a proof holds

The proof opens on a [summary](#the-summary) of the font: what it is and what it holds. The languages it covers close the proof. The font's info follows (on the next page when it does not fit): its family, style, and full names, version, designer, manufacturer, vendor ID, copyright, trademark, license (with its URL), description, the designer's and the manufacturer's URLs, the dates it was created and last modified, its units per em, and its masters, each as FontLab's font info gives it, empty ones left out, and a long one (a license's whole text) cut at 1,200 characters. Both come first because a printed proof is passed around and compared: the reader checks which font and which version they hold before judging a glyph. Then, for the current master or for each master in turn (each starting on a new page, after a page of the masters side by side), the sections you check:

- **Character set**: every glyph in a grid of ten columns, in the font's order, each with its name and code point. A glyph wider than its cell is drawn smaller; a glyph with no layer in the master says so.
- **Waterfall**: a line of sample text at each size typed (8 to 72 points by default), cut at the page's edge.
- **Text**: a few lines of sample text in each script the font covers, at each text size typed (9, 11, and 14 points by default). See [Sample text](#sample-text).
- **Kerning strings**: every kerned pair (or only the pairs common in text), grouped by first glyph, each between control letters: two straight letters of the first glyph's kind before the pair, a straight and a round one of the second glyph's kind twice after it (*HHTAHOHO*, *HHTonono*, *nnr,nono*). Punctuation takes its partner's kind. The first glyph is the left one, except in a right-to-left pair (Hebrew, Arabic, Thaana, and the other right-to-left scripts), whose string is set right to left and reads the same way from the right; see [Right to left](#right-to-left).
- **Spacing strings**: each encoded letter and figure of the Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, Armenian, Georgian, Hebrew, and kana scripts as *HHxHOxOO* or *nnxnoxoo*, with the control letters of its script and case, set without kerning: the sidebearings alone. Modifier letters (superscript and subscript letters) are left out.
- **Draw colliding glyphs in red** (unticked at first, kept from one session to the next; grayed while neither strings section is ticked): in the kerning and spacing strings, each glyph whose ink overlaps the next glyph's is drawn in red, in the preview and the PDF (image 348). It is off at first because a script font's letters may join on purpose. Overlap means their filled outlines meet, kerning applied in the kerning strings and not in the spacing strings; each pair is measured once per proof. The red is the log's on dark paper, a deeper one on white.
- **Diacritics**: the letters with diacritics, each group after the letter it is built on (*Á* after *A*, *ł* after *l*, by Unicode's decomposition or name), then the combining marks (on the dotted circle when the font has one) and the spacing accents.
- **Figures and punctuation**: the figures, their alternates (*zero.osf*, *one.tf*, and so on, a row per suffix), the figures between *H*s and between *n*s, fractions and superiors, the punctuation alone and between letters, and the symbols.

Each page has a header (the font's name, the master, the date) and a footer (its sections, what is applied, and *Page n of m*).

The control letters are a straight and a round letter per script and case: *H* and *O*, *n* and *o* for Latin; Eta and Omicron, nu and omicron for Greek; En and O for Cyrillic; Ho and Oh, now and oh for Armenian; in and on for Georgian; he and samekh for Hebrew; ro and no for kana; *H* and zero for figures. Where the font lacks a script's controls, Latin's stand in, and where it lacks those too, each letter stands alone.

## The summary

Page 1 is listed as *Summary*. It is set in the labels' typeface, under the family's name in the font itself (30 points, smaller for a name wider than the page; in the labels' typeface when the font lacks a character of its own name). From top to bottom:

- **The font and the proof**: the family and style names, the master proofed and the others the font has (or every master, when all are proofed), the version and the dates the font records, its units per em, its glyphs and its encoded characters counted, what the proof is limited to (*Proof*), the day of the proof, what is applied, and the scripts left without text.
- **Vertical metrics**: a table of the ascender, descender, and line gap of the master, of the typo and hhea values, and of Windows' winAscent and winDescent; then the x-height, the cap height, and whether *Use Typo Metrics* is set. A value the font does not set says *not set*. Font Check reads them (`read_metrics`).
- **OpenType**: the features the font's code defines, each tag with OpenType's name and, for a stylistic set or a character variant, the name its own code gives it (*ss01 (Stylistic Set 1: Round a)*); and the master's kerning in one line: its kerned pairs of glyphs, classes resolved, with the entries and classes they come from.

The font's info follows, then the glyphs. The summary's language coverage closes the proof, after every section (listed as *Language coverage*), so that its start keeps to the font's technical data and info:

- **Language coverage**: the languages covered and within reach, counted; each script the font has, in Font Check's own words (*965 of 2084 languages (411 within reach)*); the character sets it covers whole, of those Font Check counts (the five *Legacy* sets, wholly optional and so always covered, do not count toward the stats: a Latin font would otherwise cover *CJK Compatibility Ideographs*); and, in small print, how the languages are counted, how many of the covered ones are inferred from text, and where the data comes from (each source with its version and the languages it gives, and the day the data files were last changed). A coverage claim on a proof is a claim: the small print says what it rests on.
- **The languages**, under their scripts, A to Z, in five columns of 6.5-point type, read down each column: the covered ones, then (when asked) those within reach, each with what it lacks (*2 missing*, or *12 auxiliary missing* for a language that lacks auxiliary characters alone). A language whose characters were counted from a text is marked *(inferred)*, as in Font Check's list, with a line that says what that means. Names are English: an autonym needs glyphs the labels' typeface may lack.

The coverage is Font Check's own computation, called with the settings its window starts with: every language, the inferred ones too; within reach at three base characters or marks; letters the font's anchors compose counted. A language is covered there when the font has all its base and auxiliary characters and its marks, which is stricter than the *Proof* dropdown's and the text section's notion (every base character: what setting its text needs). It is computed once each time the font is read, never as a page is drawn. Without Font Check or its data files, the page leaves the coverage (and, without Font Check, the vertical metrics) out, and the log says why once.

Some 435 names fill a page (87 rows of five), and about 85 fit under the rest on page 1. A list that runs past the page goes on over the following pages, never cut: a font with Latin-1 and Latin Extended-A covers 965 languages (four pages), and one with every Latin, Cyrillic, and Greek character the data lists covers 1,915 (six pages). *Summary lists* shortens it to the counts alone (one page). The character sets are counted as Font Check's top line counts them, by the same function (`set_tally`): *Character sets: 33 of 140 covered (the 5 Legacy sets, wholly optional, not counted)*, and Unicode's scripts on a line of their own, *Scripts (Unicode): 0 of 172 covered whole*. A choice in *Summary lists* shows the first *Language coverage* page once the proof is laid out again, since the lists it changes close the proof. A name too long for its column ends in an ellipsis (the longest names only: nine in ten have 25 characters or fewer, *(inferred)* included).

With the *Summary* check box off, a few lines about the proof take the page's place: the font, its glyph and master counts, the kerned pairs in each master, and what is applied.

## What is applied

- **Kerning: yes**, as FontLab applies it, classes resolved (FontLab's own resolution, which Kerning Check reads too). Where FontLab does not resolve the classes, only pairs of glyphs are applied, and the log says so.
- **OpenType features: no.** No ligatures, no contextual or localized forms, no mark positioning, no small caps or figure styles: each character shows its own glyph, as the font maps it. Alternates still show in the character set and among the figures by their names.
- **Scripts that need shaping get no text**: Arabic, the Indic scripts, Thai, and the like need joining, reordering, or mark positioning. The first page, the text section, and the window's top line name the ones left out. Their glyphs still show in the character set.
- **Right to left**: Hebrew text, the kerning strings of every right-to-left script, and the spacing strings of Hebrew, are set right to left, flush right, each pair kerned by the font's key in text order. See below.

## Right to left

FontLab 7 keeps a right-to-left pair's kerning in text order: typed yod then bet, the pair FontLab kerns is (yod, bet), though bet stands at the left on screen; (bet, yod) is another pair, usually empty (read live on Noto Sans Hebrew, 2026-11-14; a compiled font kerns such a pair the same way, in the order its glyphs are typed). Read as it is seen, left glyph then right glyph, a Hebrew pair would be looked up as (bet, yod) and drawn without its kerning.

Proof treats every line as a text in reading order and every kerning key as a pair in that order:

- **Lines**: a line holds glyphs in reading order. Set right to left, it is reversed for drawing, its first glyph at the right, flush right; each pair is still kerned by (the glyph typed first, the glyph typed second), which on screen is (right glyph, left glyph), and the value goes between them, as a text engine applies a right-to-left `kern` value. Lines are broken with the same kerning, so the measure and the drawing agree.
- **Kerning strings**: each key is listed under its first glyph, in key order, between the control letters of its kinds, the whole string in text order: *he he yod bet he samekh he samekh* for (yod, bet). A string whose pair is right to left is set right to left, so it reads *HH?HOHO* from the right, as a Hebrew reader proofs, with yod at the right of bet and their kerning between them. A group whose first glyph kerns with both directions (a period beside Latin and Hebrew letters) is set in runs, each in its own direction.
- **Direction**: a pair is right to left when either glyph's character is of a right-to-left script: the blocks Unicode gives a right-to-left bidirectional class by default (Hebrew, Arabic, Syriac, Thaana, N'Ko, Samaritan, Mandaic, their presentation forms, and plane 1's right-to-left scripts, from Phoenician and Old South Arabian to Old Hungarian, Hanifi Rohingya, and Adlam), without the numbers in them (Arabic-Indic digits, the Rumi numerals), which a right-to-left text sets left to right. Proof holds these ranges as a table rather than asking the interpreter's Unicode data: FontLab's Python 2.7 knows Unicode 5.2 only, and its narrow build cannot read a character past U+FFFF, so Adlam or Old Hungarian would have passed for left to right in FontLab and not in the tests. An unencoded glyph goes by its base glyph's character (*yod.alt* by *yod*), and a mark of punctuation or an unencoded glyph with no such base takes its partner's direction. This is the rule Auto Kerning and Kerning Check use to read a key as it stands on screen (`screen_of_key`), widened from Hebrew and Thaana to every right-to-left script, since a font kerns them all in text order. Arabic gets no text here (it needs joining), and its kerning strings, set right to left too, show each glyph unjoined; FontLab's text order for Arabic keys is assumed from Hebrew's, not read live.
- **Spacing strings** of a right-to-left script are set right to left too, so that they read *HHxHOxOO* from the right; they have no kerning.
- **Show in Glyph window** hands FontLab each line in text order (a right-to-left line from its right end). FontLab 7's Glyph window sets typed Hebrew left to right, in that order, and kerns it by the same keys: the kerning shows, the glyphs read reversed there. Handed as drawn instead, the line would read right but lose its kerning, which is what the window is opened to check. Auto Kerning opens its Hebrew pairs in the same order for the same reason.

Mirrored characters (a parenthesis, which a right-to-left run sets with the glyph of its mirror) are not mirrored in the text section: no sample text holds one. A kerning string shows the glyphs the key names, so a Hebrew key with the *parenright* glyph shows that glyph, as a right-to-left run would draw a typed opening parenthesis.

## Other writing directions

Proof sets every line horizontally: left to right, or right to left for the scripts above. What it reads of a font is horizontal only: each glyph's advance width (`layer.advanceWidth`) and the master's horizontal kerning (`package.kerning(master)`, through Auto Kerning's `FontKerning`). It reads no advance heights, vertical origins, vhea or vmtx values, and no `vkrn` or `vpal` code; the features are named in the summary but never applied, `vert` and `vrt2` included.

- **Top to bottom, Mongolian and Phags-pa**: Unicode encodes both as left-to-right scripts whose text runs in vertical columns, read left to right; a horizontal line of them is the vertical text turned a quarter turn. Proof sets them horizontally, left to right, as encoded, which is how a font editor shows them, and unjoined (Mongolian needs joining and its variation selectors, which Proof does not do). Neither is in Font Check's language data, so they get no text section, only the character set, kerning strings, and the waterfall when the font has no other letters. The summary (or the page in its place) says so: *Set horizontally: Mongolian (written top to bottom: set here horizontally, left to right, as encoded; vertical setting is not done)*, for each such script the font has letters of.
- **Top to bottom, CJK**: Japanese, Chinese, and Korean are set horizontally only. A vertical proof would need each glyph's advance height and vertical origin, the `vert`/`vrt2` substitutions (CJK Vertical builds them), `vpal`, and `vkrn`, none of which Proof reads today.
- **Bottom to top, Ogham and Hanunoo**: Unicode encodes both left to right, and their fonts are made for horizontal text; Ogham runs bottom to top only along the edge of a stone, and Hanunoo bottom to top on bamboo. Proof sets them left to right and says nothing; Hanunoo gets no text (its vowel signs need mark positioning), and Ogham, absent from the language data, none either. Old Hungarian, whose inscriptions also run in other directions, is set right to left, as Unicode encodes it.
- **Boustrophedon** (lines alternating direction, the alternate lines mirrored: early Greek, Old South Arabian, Rongorongo) is a property of a text's layout, not of its characters: Unicode has no control for it, and a font that supports it does so with mirrored glyph alternates. Proof sets each line in its script's direction.
- **Mixed directions in one line**: no bidirectional algorithm is run. Each sample text is of one script, and the Hebrew sample holds no digits or Latin words; a kerning group whose first glyph kerns with both directions is set in runs, one direction each.

## Sample text

The text section reads Font Check's language data (*language_coverage.json*, from [Hyperglot](https://github.com/rosettatype/hyperglot)): a language counts as covered when the font has every one of its base characters. For each covered language with a sample below whose every character the font has, its sample is used, the most spoken six per script first; a script with no sample that fits shows the letters of the language it covers best instead (up to 120 of them; the CJK ideographs once, though Japanese, Chinese, and Korean list them each).

The samples are pangrams written for FreeLab, GPL-3 with the code, for Spanish, Portuguese, German, French, Italian, Turkish, Polish, Dutch, Romanian, Hungarian, Czech, Swedish, Icelandic, Danish, Croatian, Catalan, Lithuanian, Russian, Ukrainian, Greek, Armenian, Hebrew, and Korean, and three texts in the public domain: the English pangram (in print since 1885), the opening line of Nguyễn Du's *Truyện Kiều* (1820) for Vietnamese, and the Iroha (11th century) for Japanese, in hiragana and in katakana. The other languages' traditional pangrams are not used: their origins are unclear. Each of FreeLab's pangrams is a sentence a reader of the language finds plausible and holds every base letter Hyperglot lists for its language, accented ones included; letters a language writes only in foreign words come in names and loanwords (*Quentin*, *whisky*, *Wi-Fi*). *tests/proof_tests.py* checks each one and lists any letter missing, with two exceptions: Hyperglot lists the Russian *э* for Ukrainian, which never writes it, and Hebrew's pointed letters (with a dagesh or a shin dot) count by their letter, since the proof does not position marks. Korean's is checked for its 14 basic consonants and 10 basic vowels. Only letters of the language and common punctuation appear, so a font that covers the language and has the punctuation sets it. The waterfall sets the first script's text.

## The window

The top line names the font and the master in bold (as every window's top line: *Libertinus Serif, master Regular*, then *(of 3 masters)* for a font with several), the page count and size, the pages per section (each section's name in italics, its pages in parentheses, the number bold alone as every count with no noun of its own), the kerned pairs (*no kerned pairs* at none, never *0 kerned pairs*), and what is applied. With no font open it reads, on one line, *No font is open. This window hooks to the next font you open, and lays out its proof at once.* Under it, the header's line (shown with no font too), then the settings, each caption a noun and a colon before its control, its items capitalized: *Masters:* (the current master, or all of them), *Page:* (A4 or US Letter, portrait), *Kerning strings:* (*Every kerned pair*, or *Common pairs only*: the pairs common in text that Auto Kerning's *Common pairs* lists), *Proof:* (below), *Colors:* (*As the window*: the PDF in the window's text and background colors, light on dark in dark mode; then the others the window is not in already, tied to dark mode's *Shade* setting: beside a light window, *Dark (light gray on off-black)* (#ebebeb on #1e1e1e, named for what they are) and *Medium (light gray on medium gray)* (#e0e0e0 on #3c3c40, the text and base colors dark mode's medium grays give a window); beside a dark window in the dark grays, *Print (black on white)* and *Medium*; beside one in the medium grays, *Print* and *Dark*. The shade is the one dark mode says it applied, or, while dark mode is off, the one the window's background matches. The item chosen keeps its place as dark mode is switched, and the second is always the other of light and dark), the *Summary* check box and a check box per section, *Summary lists:* (*Covered languages*, *Covered languages and those within reach*, or *No languages (counts only)*), and *Waterfall sizes:* and *Text sizes:* (typed in points, separated by spaces or commas). A caption shows its control's help on a right-click and grays with it; with no font every control is grayed, *Close* and the tools aside, *Masters* shows a grayed *n/a* rather than an empty dropdown or the closed font's masters, and *Proof* is back at *Every script* (the choice made returns with the next font that has it). The proof is laid out again as any of them changes, and after an undo, a redo, or an edit in FontLab. The *Summary* check box, *Summary lists*, and *Draw colliding glyphs in red.* are kept from one FontLab session to the next (*proof.json* in FreeLab's settings folder); the other settings start as they are each time, and *Restore defaults* puts all of them back.

*Proof* limits the proof: *Every script*; one script the font has letters of, with the figures, punctuation, and symbols; or, after the line, one language the font covers (every base character Hyperglot lists for it), with its base and auxiliary letters, whose sample the text section then shows alone. Every covered language is listed: the 20 most spoken first, then, after a second line, the others, A to Z (the dropdown opens as a list of 24 rows, scrolled, and typing a name's first letters goes to it). A font that covers 24 languages or fewer lists them in one block. Languages within reach are not offered: a language's text is set only when the font has all its base characters, the script entries proof whatever letters are there, and Font Check lists what such a language lacks. The dropdown's help gives the counts. A glyph's kind (letter, figure, mark, punctuation, symbol) and a letter's script come from Unicode 17.0's character database (the general categories, and the first word of a letter's name, kept in *character_sets.json*), not from the running Python's, so FontLab's Python 2.7, which knows Unicode 5.2, classes the letters encoded since (Adlam, Georgian's Mtavruli, Tai Yo) as the tests do, and a language's characters past U+FFFF are read as code points, never as their two UTF-16 units; a glyph whose code point Unicode does not assign is left out of a script's or a language's proof rather than kept as a symbol. The figures, marks, punctuation, and symbols a script's or a language's proof keeps are those Unicode's Script property (*Scripts.txt*, kept in the same file) gives to no one script, such as the ASCII digits, the period, and the combining accents, and those of the proof's own script: Adlam's digits and initial question and exclamation marks are Adlam's, and a Latin-only proof leaves them out.

The list on the left shows the pages; the selected page is drawn beside it as the PDF will be, in the colors *Colors* says, the whole page as large as the room allows (drag the line beside the list, or make the window larger) and drawn at the display's own resolution, so it stays sharp on a scaled display. *Show in Glyph window* (or a double-click on a page) opens the page's lines of glyphs in a FontLab Glyph window. *Save PDF…* writes every page to a file you name, in *Documents* at first and then where you saved last, named after the font, the master, and the date. Where a file of that name is there already, FreeLab asks before replacing it (*Cardo Regular proof 2026-11-24.pdf already exists. Replace it with a new proof?*), in its own box, which follows dark mode, rather than in Windows' *Confirm Save As* box, which stays light (the save dialog is asked not to confirm; where FontLab's PythonQt cannot ask it that, Windows' box asks as before and FreeLab does not ask again); *Cancel* leaves the file as it was, and the log says *The operation was interrupted. Nothing was saved or overwritten.* The PDF is written to a staged file beside its own (*…proof 2026-11-24.freelab-proof-k2x9_q4m.part.pdf*) and takes its place only once whole, so *Cancel* or *Esc* while it is written (or *Close*, the tools menu, an arrow) stops it between two pages, and a PDF stopped or failed leaves the file there as it was and no partial file; the log says the same line, or why the PDF could not be written. A save also removes the staged files an earlier save of the same PDF left behind: only files whose names carry FreeLab's mark (*freelab-proof-*), so a file of your own named alike stays. Otherwise the log says how many pages were saved, in which colors and how large (*Saved 12 pages (black on white, 840 KB) in …*), and where: the folder is a link that opens it in a new Explorer window with the PDF selected (the Finder on macOS; image 375). *Open PDF* then opens it in the application the system opens PDF files with; once the file is moved or deleted, the log says it is not there anymore and the button grays out (looked for again as the window is activated).

## How it works

The proof is laid out in points by pure code (*proof/freelab_proof.py*, above its Qt line, tested by *tests/proof_tests.py* under Python 3 and 2.7): glyph names with their advance widths and kerning, lines broken to the page's width (at spaces, or between any two characters in Japanese and in a script's letters), pages filled in turn, and headers and footers added once the page count is known. Each page is a list of items: lines of glyphs, text, rules, and cell frames. A renderer draws them with a QPainter, each glyph's outline as a vector path (from FontLab's layers, through Auto Kerning's `layer_path`), into a `QPdfWriter` at 600 dots per inch (each page filled with the background color first, unless it is white) or into the preview's pixmap. The renderer takes Qt's modules as arguments, so the same code draws in FontLab (PythonQt) and offline (PySide6, with fontTools pens and HarfBuzz for the kerning), which is how its sample PDFs were checked.

## Limits

- A font with tens of thousands of class-kerned pairs makes a long PDF: each glyph is drawn as its own path. *Common pairs only* keeps it short.
- Line metrics come from the current master's ascender and descender, for every master.
- Combining marks are not positioned (no mark feature): a mark sits where its outline is drawn, after the glyph before it.
- The preview draws one page at a time; the PDF has them all. *Zoom* shows it whole (*Fit page*) or at 50% to 400% of its printed size (100%: a point is 96/72 pixels), scrolled; *Ctrl* with the mouse wheel over the page, and *Ctrl+plus*, *Ctrl+minus*, and *Ctrl+0* anywhere in the window while a page is shown, change it. Those keys are FontLab's own *View* zoom shortcuts too (*Zoom In*, *Zoom Out*, *Fit*), set application-wide, so Qt runs them before any window sees the key, and each *Ctrl+plus* would zoom FontLab's view: while Proof is the active window, those actions give the keys up, and they get them back as soon as Proof is no longer active, hides, or closes (a later Proof gives back keys a window left taken). FontLab's own zoom keys work everywhere else. A zoomed page is drawn at most 36 million device pixels large, coarser past that rather than larger.
- The dropdowns' lists open as windows of their own, which dark mode would take for FontLab's lists and map (light text with ClearType's colored fringes): they ask dark mode to leave them alone, and Qt draws them from the dark palette as it draws the boxes.
- The summary's date for the language data is the day its files were last changed on this computer, not a release date: the data files carry their sources' versions, which the page prints, and no date of their own.
- The summary's version and dates come from the font info below, with the same caveat.
- The font info is read by the names FontLab's font info is expected to use (UFO's: *familyName*, *versionMajor*, *openTypeNameDesigner*, and so on), each tried in turn; a field FontLab names otherwise is left out, as an empty one is.
