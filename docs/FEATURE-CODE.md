# Feature code

*Scripts > FreeLab > Feature Code* writes the OpenType code for the font's ligatures, for alternates that take turns, and for the forms its glyph names call for (case-sensitive forms, localized forms, fractions and figures, small capitals, stylistic sets). The code is shown, and checked by fontTools' feature parser (FontLab 7 ships it), before anything is written.

The window has three tabs over its lists, *Ligatures and alternates*, *Forms*, and *Stylistic alternates*, and *Code preview* under them: it shows everything the *Write the code* button would write, whichever tab is open. The code shown is not in the font until *Write the code* writes it, hence the heading's name. *Code preview* is in colors (keywords, the tags and lookup names after them, glyph classes, glyph lists, numbers, strings, and comments), chosen to read on the dark palette and on the light one and taken again when dark mode switches; code of more than 2,000 lines (a CJK font's classes) is shown plain, since Qt colors every line again at each click. Where FontLab's PythonQt cannot make the highlighter, *Code preview* is plain text.

The window is laid out as every FreeLab window ([WINDOWS.md](WINDOWS.md#layout)): under the top line, the status lines (the ligatures, the glyphs taking turns, the form features ticked, and what keeps the code from being written), then a line, then the tabs, so the bar a long read shows stays clear of the tab bar; the top line names the font even after a read was stopped. The window's bottom row holds the buttons of the tab shown: at the left its *Tick* buttons, then *Restore suggestions*; at the right *Compile after writing*, then *Write the code* (or *Update the code*) right beside it, the tick being about that button, then the tab's builds (*Build ticked figure forms*; *Make copies to redraw* and *Build N variants*), *Cancel*, and *Close*. No button stands between a list and its splitter handle, and each tab's lists share a splitter, the third tab's too (image 259). Every splitter of the window reads the same way: the frame above, the handle right against it, then the line, then the next heading (owner's images 288, 300, 318). A heading that follows a list or previews on the same tab (*Alternates that take turns*, *Figure forms to build*, *Variants that take turns*, *Code preview*) has that line over it, the first thing of its part under the handle, so it does not read as the caption of the list above (image 234). Like every separator but the header's, the shared module hides it while the window has no font. The status lines and the notes set their counts in bold as the log does (**155 ligatures** (**28** standard, **127** discretionary, **0** off), **397 glyphs**, **794 variants** to build; owner's images 297 and 301).

With no font open, the window says so and takes the next font opened (as every FreeLab window does); meanwhile its lists and *Code preview* are empty, and no control that acts on a font takes a click: the look back and the turns' *Tick* buttons, the build settings and *Build ticked figure forms*, *Write the code* and *Restore suggestions*, and the whole third tab, whose field for typed characters says *n/a*.

## Ligatures

The list holds every ligature of the font: the simple rules of its `liga` and `dlig` features (`sub f f t by f_f_t;`, `sub parenleft c parenright by copyright;`), and each glyph whose name joins glyphs of the font with `_` (*l_l*, *f_f_t*) that no rule makes yet. Each is set to:

- **Standard** (`liga`): on unless the user turns it off. In InDesign and Illustrator, the *Ligatures* option; on the web, `font-variant-ligatures: no-common-ligatures`.
- **Discretionary** (`dlig`): off unless the user turns it on (*Discretionary Ligatures*; `font-variant-ligatures: discretionary-ligatures`).
- **Off**: in neither.

The user can turn off every ligature, except in apps that offer no switch. *Off* takes it out of the font's code altogether, for the rules FreeLab writes (its own, and FontLab's automatic ones); a rule the font's own code writes stays where it is (below, *How FreeLab reads and writes a feature's code*). A ligature's rule starts in the feature it is in now; a glyph no rule makes starts as Discretionary, or Standard when its name ends in `.liga`.

The rules are written longest first, in a block between FreeLab's marker lines; every other line of the two features stays as it was (in the owner's test font, the hand-written rules that set a standalone *I*). FontLab rewrites the code between its `#> feature` and `#< feature` lines when it updates its automatic features, which it does on compiling and exporting with *Update on Compile* on. A feature written here has that first line turned into `#/> feature`, FontLab's documented way of handing a feature back to you, so FontLab no longer rewrites it; its code stays as it was.

## Alternates that take turns

For a handwriting font: a letter, a figure, or a punctuation mark with alternates (named *X.2*, *X.3*; *X.alt*, *X.alt1*; or *X.calt1*) takes its next form when its nearest earlier form, within a few glyphs (2 by default), is the one before it: *l*, *l.2*, *l*, *l.2*. Doubled and nearby glyphs vary as handwriting does, and the same text always comes out the same. Stylistic sets (*X.ss01*) are the *Forms* tab's; a variant the third tab built from one (*X.ss01.alt2*, while the font has *X.ss01*) is in *X*'s turn.

Each kind looks back among its own: only letters stand between two forms of a letter, only figures between a figure's, only punctuation marks between a mark's. So *1001* varies its second *1* (two figures between) and its second *0*, *99* its second *9*, and three periods in a row, *!!*, *--*, and *?!?* their marks; and for a letter, a space, a hyphen, an apostrophe, or a figure between two letters ends the look back, so each word starts afresh (*a.a*, *a-a*, and *1.1* stay plain). One class of every kind would have made *l'l* and *e-e* vary where they did not. The kinds are Unicode's general categories: letters (L), figures (N: superior figures and fractions too), and punctuation (P: the period, comma, hyphen and dashes, quotation marks, brackets, slash, ampersand, asterisk, and the like). Symbols (*+*, *=*, *~*, *$*, the fraction bar) and combining marks take no turns. Which character is which is read from a table in *features/alternates_model.json* (*features/train/kinds.py*: the three categories to U+FFFF, Unicode 16.0), not asked of the Python that runs: FontLab's Python 2.7 knows Unicode 5.2, where *§* and *¶* are symbols and letters encoded since are unassigned.

The code is a `calt` lookup (contextual alternates, on unless the user turns it off) between FreeLab's marker lines; the rest of `calt` stays. An alternate the font's code already uses for something else is listed but left unticked, with a note: taking turns would undo its job. In the owner's test font, *I.2* is the standalone *I*, set in `liga`, so *I* stays out, and *N*, *i*, *j*, and *l* take turns. `aalt` and `salt`, which list every alternate as a menu, do not count as a job.

`calt` comes after `liga` in the font, so ligatures form first: where `liga` makes *l_l* of *ll*, the alternates take turns around it.

A font with no alternates to take turns gets a line under the look back that says so and where to make them: on the *Stylistic alternates* tab (a link that opens it), under *Variants that take turns*, or drawn and named as above. The look back and the *Tick* buttons are grayed out meanwhile.

The list puts the glyphs that double most in text first (*ee*, *tt*, *ll*, *ss*, *oo*, *ff*, *00*): alternates matter most where a glyph stands beside itself. Its *Doubled in text* column says *Often* or *Seldom*, with the count in the cell's tooltip. The counts are the `doubles` of *features/alternates_model.json*: for each letter, figure, or punctuation mark, how often it stands doubled among 300,000 pairs of characters in text, in the language that doubles it most (Finnish *aa* counts as often), from the totals of [andre-fuchs/kerning-pairs](https://github.com/andre-fuchs/kerning-pairs) for 24 languages (MIT License, Copyright 2019 André Fuchs; its notice is in *kerning/common_pairs.txt*). *features/train/doubles.py* makes them from that project's *count/total/list.json*, pinned by commit and SHA-256: 70 letters, 10 figures, and 19 punctuation marks, 33 of them counted 100 times or more, which is what *Often* means (*ff* 300, *jj* 141, *00* 419, and *99* 112, against *ww* 20 and *11* 72). A capital counts as its small letter. A letter whose pair is among Auto Kerning's common pairs (*kerning/common_pairs.txt* and *kerning/script_pairs.txt*, which add kana, Armenian, and Georgian) is *Often* too; those lists alone could not rank the doubles, since they hold the pairs fonts kern and so lack *ll*, *ss*, *ee*, and *oo*.

Punctuation takes another rule, because the counts cannot serve it. The text counted is an encyclopedia's, with its markup: the mark it doubles most is *}}* (85, the end of a template), then *..* (72), *--* (47), *,,* (32), and *//* (24, addresses); *!!* and *??* count under 1 in 300,000, fewer than *]]*. No mark reaches 100, and the common pairs' doubled marks are markup too (*}}*, *{{*, *))*, *//*, and two backslashes). So a punctuation mark is *Often* when it is counted 100 times or more (none is), or when it is doubled by convention, the `doubles_typed` of the model: the marks whose double or triple Unicode encodes as a character of its own (a compatibility decomposition into one punctuation mark repeated: *…* and *‥* are periods, *‼* exclamation marks, *⁇* question marks, *″* primes, *‶* reversed primes), and the two TeX reads doubled as one character (*The TeXbook*, chapter 2: *--* is a dash, *''* a double quotation mark). That makes seven: the period, the exclamation mark, the question mark, the hyphen, the apostrophe, the prime, and the reversed prime. The counts still rank the marks, and the tooltip of a *Seldom* mark gives its count.

*Tick all*, *Untick all*, and *Tick often doubled*, in the bottom row while this tab is shown, tick the glyphs at once. *Look back:* sets how many glyphs of its kind may stand between a glyph and its earlier form, at most. Every glyph whose alternates have no other job starts ticked while they all fit rules by glyph (below); where the font has more (a whole alphabet of built variants), only the often doubled ones start ticked. The glyphs FreeLab's `calt` block already cycles start ticked in any case, and the ticks are kept when the font is read again (after a build, a write, or an undo). A row's forms are drawn for the first 120 glyphs; click a later row to draw its forms.

The line under the look back says which of two ways the ticked glyphs take turns:

- **By glyph**, while that takes 800 rules at most: each glyph looks back for its own earlier form, as described above. A glyph with *n* alternates and a look back of *b* takes (*n* + 1) × (*b* + 1) − 1 rules: 11 with three alternates and a look back of 2, so about 70 such glyphs. One class per kind (`@FreeLab_letters`, `@FreeLab_figures`, `@FreeLab_punctuation`) holds the font's glyphs of that kind for every rule; a glyph's rules are written nearest first, and after its last alternate an `ignore` rule stops the look back there.
- **By position**, past that: each glyph takes the form after the form of the glyph of its kind before it, whichever that is. After a plain letter comes a first alternate, after a first alternate a second, and after a form with no next the plain letter again (*hello*: *h*, *e.alt1*, *l.alt2*, *l.alt3*, *o*). A doubled glyph still never repeats its form, and the same word always comes out the same, but the look back does not apply, and a word's first letter is always the plain one, after an opening quotation mark as after a space (as a number's first figure is, and the first mark after a word). The code is one rule per kind and step of the turn, on classes, whatever the number of glyphs.

Letters alone, the code is what it was before figures and punctuation took turns, class names included, so a font written then shows no change.

By glyph, the font needs a table per rule, and a lookup's tables must sit within the 65,535 bytes its 16-bit offsets reach. 1,100 letters with three variants would take 12,100 rules, and FontLab's compiler stops at `coverage section too large` on such code (tried with the font's letters named in a class per letter, 56 MB of text), so they take turns by position: 62 KB of code, 3 rules, and a `GSUB` table of 11 KB (fontTools). With 40 figures and 60 punctuation marks beside them, three variants each (1,200 glyphs, 4,800 with their variants): 68 KB of code, 9 rules, and a `GSUB` table of 12 KB, or 17 KB with the glyphs in an order that gives the compiler no runs to shorten its lists with; the window counts 28,800 bytes for them, of the 48,000 it allows. By glyph, in that font, 72 glyphs with three variants (792 rules: 50 letters, 10 figures, and 12 marks) compile to 22 KB, 72 letters to 21 KB, and 10 figures with 60 marks (770 rules) to 19 KB, each in one lookup of chaining rules and three of substitutions, none an extension. AFDKO's *makeotf* 3.6.1, tried once on 2026-11-12 with letters only, compiled the code by glyph up to 2,200 rules and stopped at 3,300 (an offset overflow), and compiled the code by position for 1,100 letters; the limit of 800 keeps a margin. By position, the window also counts the room the classes need (8 bytes per glyph and step) and, past 48,000 bytes, says to untick some glyphs instead of writing code that cannot compile.

## Forms

The *Forms* tab lists one row per feature the font's glyph names call for, with its substitutions under it (click the row's arrow; their pictures are drawn as a feature opens). A ticked feature is written with its ticked rows. Each feature's code sits in a FreeLab block between marker lines, in named lookups (*FreeLab_case*, *FreeLab_frac_numerators*, and so on). A glyph's form is found by its suffix wherever the suffix stands among its others: *a.sc.ss01* is the small capital of *a.ss01* and the `ss01` form of *a.sc*.

The check boxes of the lists (the alternates, the forms, and the figure forms to build) are drawn in the window's own colors, a gray frame, or the highlight color with a check mark when ticked, so they show in dark mode too, which leaves these lists' pictures as they are drawn.

Every row of the forms list has one height, a picture's (40 pixels: the 36 of a picture and the few pixels a list puts around it), whether it has a picture or not: a feature's row and a row without one (`aalt`'s *its alternates (Superscript)*) are as high as the rows with pictures. Its first column is wide enough for an open feature's rows (their indent, check box, and glyph name, up to 220 pixels), even while the features are closed. A note stays on its row's one line, cut at its end where the column is too narrow; the cell's tooltip has it whole. The window's tooltips are rich text, which Qt wraps, and one whose text would pass 520 pixels is laid out that wide (Font Check's manner).

- **case** (case-sensitive forms, for text in capitals): every *X.case* of a glyph *X*: hyphens and dashes, brackets, guillemets, *at*, and marks (an *acutecomb.case* raised for capitals), whatever the font has.
- **locl** (localized forms, for text tagged with a language): one row per language and substitution.
  - Romanian and Moldavian (`ROM`, `MOL`): *S* and *T* with a cedilla (U+015E, U+015F, U+0162, U+0163) become the comma-below letters (U+0218 to U+021B). The code points decide, not the names: Adobe's *Tcommaaccent* is U+0162, the *T* with a cedilla. Where one glyph holds both code points (an old Romanian encoding), the other direction: the languages that write a cedilla (Turkish, Azerbaijani, Crimean Tatar, Gagauz, Kazakh, Tatar, Turkmen, Kurdish; Gagauz for *T*) take the font's unencoded *Scedilla* (or *uni015E*).
  - Catalan (`CAT`): between two *l*'s, the middle dot becomes *periodcentered.loclCAT* (between two *L*'s its *.case* form, where the font has one); a font without it but with *ldot* (U+0140) and *Ldot* sets *l·l* as *ŀl*.
  - Dutch (`NLD`): after *í*, *j* takes an acute (*jacute*, *uni006A0301*, or *j_acutecomb*), and *J* after *Í*; a ligature named for the language (*iacute_j.loclNLD*) replaces the pair.
  - Turkic languages (`TRK`, `AZE`, `CRT`, `KAZ`, `TAT`, `BSH`, `GAG`): *i* becomes *i.loclTRK* (or *idotaccent*), so it keeps its dot in capitals and small capitals; `smcp` then takes *i.sc.loclTRK* (or *Idotaccent.sc*). The row says when the font has no small capital *İ*.
  - Any other glyph named *X.loclXXX*: the language from the suffix (*loclVIET*, Glyphs' name, is `VIT`; *loclMARNEP* is Marathi and Nepali), the script from *X*'s character (`cyrl` for *uni0434.loclBGR*; `dev2` and `deva` for Devanagari; for a glyph of no script, such as a middle dot, the language's usual one). A ligature named so (*Iacute_J.loclNLD*) replaces its parts. A language tag FreeLab does not know is written as named, with a note to check it. The font's own *X.loclXXX* wins over a convention for the same glyph and language.

  The rows are grouped by script and language (`script latn; language ROM;`), a lookup per kind of rule. Each language needs a `languagesystem` line in the prefix, or the font's other features would be missing for text in it: FreeLab adds the ones the prefix lacks in a block of its own after the prefix's last `languagesystem` line (with `DFLT dflt` first where the prefix has none, since declaring one language would otherwise drop the default), and takes it out again when no locl row needs it. A feature sets a language once (FontLab's compiler stops at a second `language CAT;` under the same script: *language-specific behavior already specified*), so where the font's own locl code sets a language already (Bellefair's sets `CAT`, `MOL`, and `ROM`), that language's rows are shown in amber, never written, with a note to add the rule there; FreeLab's block sets only the languages the font's code does not. FontLab's automatic code does not count: FreeLab's block replaces it.
- **frac**, **numr**, **dnom**: a figure's numerator is its *.numr* form (or *.numerator*), its denominator its *.dnom* form (or *.denominator*); where a figure has a denominator but no numerator, `frac` takes its superior. `frac` is the usual three lookups for the text a user selects as a fraction: the slash becomes the fraction bar (U+2044; without one, the slash divides), every figure a numerator, and each numerator after the bar or a denominator a denominator (*1/2*, *12/25*). A figure's other forms (*one.tf*, *one.osf*) take the same numerator and denominator.
- **sups**, **subs**, **sinf**: *.sups* (or *.superior*); *.subs* (or *.inferior*, *.inf*, *.sinf*); *.sinf* (or *.inferior*, *.inf*, *.subs*). Where a glyph has no such form, the font's Unicode superscript or subscript character of it (*twosuperior*, *uni2082*) does, but not the ordinal indicators.
- **ordn**: after a figure, and after a figure and a period (Spanish *1.º*), *a* and *A* become *ª* (or *a.ordn*), *o* and *O* *º* (or *o.ordn*), and letters with an *.ordn* form take it, one after another (*1er*, *2nd*).
- **smcp**, **c2sc**: from *.sc*, *.smcp*, and *.c2sc*. A lowercase letter takes its own form in `smcp` (*a.sc*, or *a.smcp*), else its capital's (*A.sc*), else the small capital of its capital's lowercase letter (dotless *ı*: *i.sc*); a capital takes its own form in `c2sc` (*A.c2sc*, *A.sc*), else its lowercase letter's (*a.sc*). A glyph of no case (*ampersand.sc*, *one.sc*) is in both.
- **lnum**, **onum**, **pnum**, **tnum**: the suffixes say each set's spacing and style as Glyphs names them (*.tf* tabular lining, *.lf* proportional lining, *.osf* proportional oldstyle, *.tosf* tabular oldstyle), or one of the two (*.tnum*, *.pnum*, *.lnum*, *.onum*: the other is read from the set's own widths and ink, else is the default figures'). The default figures' style is read from their widths (most of them one width: tabular), from their ink (a zero lower than the eight: oldstyle), else from the suffixes (a font with *.tf* and *.osf* has proportional lining defaults); the *Forms* tab and each figure feature say which. Each feature maps every set to its counterpart (`tnum` takes *one* to *one.tf* and *one.osf* to *one.tosf*), so features combine; where the font has oldstyle figures at one spacing only, `onum` takes them anyway (tabular lining defaults with proportional *.onum* figures, as in Crimson). The figures of a tabular set must share one advance width: those that do not are amber warning rows under `tnum`, never ticked and never changed.
- **ss01** to **ss20**, **cv01** to **cv99**: one feature per suffix, its name written as `featureNames { name "..."; };` (a character variant's as `cvParameters`), first in the feature. The name is suggested from what the set changes and can be edited in the feature's row: *Single-story a* where the outlines tell (an *a* whose counter reaches near its top is single-story, a *g* of three contours double-story; read from the contours' boxes in the current master), else *Alternate a and g*, *Alternate figures*, *Alternate capitals*, or *Alternate A, E, F, and others*. A name the font's own code gives the set is kept and shown, not edited.
- **aalt** (Access All Alternates): what InDesign and Illustrator offer as a selected character's alternates, in place and in their *Glyphs* panel. It refers to every feature with one-for-one alternates (`feature case;`, `feature ss01;`, and the like: case, small caps, figures, superiors and inferiors, salt, swash, titling, the sets), only while it has code, and offers each ASCII character the font draws a look-alike spacing accent for (~ and ˜, ^ and ˆ, ` and ˋ, " and ˝, ' and ʹ) as the other's alternate: the character typed stays, so a search still finds it. Written, aalt is moved first among the font's features (the compiler takes it only there; a feature FontLab adds goes last).

### Figure forms to build

Under the forms, *Figure forms to build* lists what the font lacks for those features, in sections (click a section's arrow for its glyphs, each drawn beside its figure as it would be built):

- **Numerators** and **denominators**: each figure without a *.numr* (or *.numerator*) or *.dnom* (or *.denominator*) form.
- **Superiors** and **inferiors**: each figure without a *.sups* or *.subs* form and without the Unicode character `sups` or `subs` takes instead.
- **Superscript figures** (U+2070, U+00B9, U+00B2, U+00B3, U+2074 to U+2079) and **subscript figures** (U+2080 to U+2089) the font does not encode.
- **Ordinal indicators**: *ª* and *º*, where the font has *a* or *o* but not them.

Names follow the font's: the suffix most of its figures use for the set (*one.numerator* in the font: *two.numerator*), and its names for the Unicode figures (*onesuperior*: *foursuperior*, and *fourinferior*); else *.numr*, *.dnom*, *.sups*, *.subs*, and *uniXXXX*, and *ordfeminine* and *ordmasculine*. A name a glyph without the code point already has is not built beside it.

Each is made one of three ways:

- **Scaled**: the figure (its lining form where the default figures are oldstyle; *a* or *o* for an ordinal indicator), scaled, raised or lowered to its set's baseline, and centered in a width a little wider than the scaled figure's.
- **Copied**: a Unicode superscript or subscript figure is its set's form of the figure (*uni00B2* is *two.sups*), where the font has it or it is ticked to be built too.
- **Moved**: where the font has a whole drawn set (all ten figures), a set it lacks is that set moved, unscaled, to its own baseline: drawn small figures beat scaled ones.

The settings row (*Set:*, *Scale:*, *Baseline:*, *Width:*, and *Strokes:*, each label naming its box, as in every tool: owner's image 271) shows and changes one set's scale (of the figure's height), baseline (where the scaled figure's baseline goes, in font units), and width (of the scaled figure's advance); clicking a row picks its set, and *Suggested* puts the set's settings back, grayed out while they are as suggested (its tooltip and its What's This say where the set's suggestion comes from: *as the font's 5 drawn forms*). *Strokes*, on the same row, is one value for every set, which its What's This says; *Suggested*, at the row's end, puts it back at 107% too, and is clickable while it or the picked set's three settings differ from the suggestion. *Restore suggestions* puts everything back. Changing the scale of the numerators or of the ordinal indicators keeps their top where it is. The first settings are the font's own where it has drawn forms of the set (the medians of their scale, baseline, and width); a set it has none of takes the size of its other small figures, else the corpus's; and is placed as the corpus places it: a numerator's top at the figures' top, a denominator on the baseline, a superior's baseline at 0.51 of the cap height, an inferior's at −0.17, an ordinal indicator's top at 1.01 of the cap height (or at the font's other indicator's top). Cap height is the current master's.

*Build ticked figure forms* (*Build 12 ticked figure forms*: the count of the ticked rows that can be built), in the window's bottom row while the *Forms* tab is shown, adds them at the end of the font in every master, as one step of FontLab's History: *Edit > Undo* takes them back. Each glyph's code point is set once its layers are made. Placements come from the current master's measures; widths from each master's own figures. The font is then read again, and the forms above take the new glyphs (a feature that was not there, such as `sups`, now has its row): tick it and write the code.

Each set's row says, in its own columns, how many of its glyphs are ticked and, in short, what its rows say (*10 scaled from their figures*; *10 copies of their set's glyphs; U+00B2 to U+2079*; *3 built here before*), folded or not. What a build made stays listed, with the note *built here before*: build again with other settings (a scale, a baseline, a width, *Strokes*) and it replaces those glyphs, each emptied and made again in place (the glyph, its code point, and the glyphs made of it stay), as one step of the History; the log names them (*Replaced 10 glyphs built here before, with the settings shown*). A form whose settings did not change is left as it is. The build reads every setting from its box as it starts, a value typed and not yet entered included. Only what this window built is replaced, and only while it is as a build left it: a glyph drawn over, given another width, or changed any other way since is the user's, no longer listed, and never touched (what each build left is noted, every master, so an undo that brings back an earlier build's glyph is still known). The notes last as long as the window: a glyph built in an earlier session is the font's own. The button grays out when a build would make or change nothing (nothing ticked, or every ticked figure form as built here with these very settings), and its tooltip and What's This then say why; the settings row grays out while the list is empty.

Copied and moved forms are made of components. Scaling thins strokes: a figure at 59% has stems 59% as thick as the figure's, where the corpus's drawn small figures have ink about 7% wider than scaling gives. So *Strokes* (107% at first) keeps some weight: above 100%, a scaled form is made of its figure's outlines, scaled, then offset outward evenly on both sides of each stroke until its strokes are that share of the scaled ones (the stroke measured across the middle of the font's zero, else its one). A form is outlines in every master or components in every master: where a master's figure is made of components or its stroke cannot be measured, the form is components everywhere, and a warning says so and why (*Strokes at 130% could not thicken 10 glyphs: … They are made of components, scaled (their strokes thinned), because the figures' stroke could not be measured.*). At 100%, every form is components. A built form is a start to draw over.

The offset on each side is the figure's stroke, times the scale, times half of what *Strokes* adds: a stroke of 80 units scaled to 59% is 47.2 units, and is written 50.5 units wide at 107% (1.7 units a side) and 61.4 at 130% (7.1 a side). The tests measure those widths on the nodes written, on a fake figure of one stem. The log gives the offset (*strokes at 130% of the scaled ones (each side moved out 7.1 units)*; a range where the sets' scales differ), so the result can be measured in a Glyph window. The pictures of the forms to build show the strokes too, drawn again as *Strokes* changes.

A thickened form is written into a new shape: each of the figure's contours is cloned, the clone's nodes are put at their offset places while it belongs to no shape, and the clones are given at once to a new shape (`flShape()`, then `addContours`, the call Font Check's master fixes proved to redraw), which is then updated. Nodes moved inside a shape the glyph has already do not move what FontLab draws: FontLab keeps a node's `x` and `y` set from a script, and the contour's path follows, but the shape's own outline, what FontLab draws and boxes, does not (seen live on 2026-11-13), and the nodes read back where they were put. Where FontLab makes no shape from a script, or a clone's nodes do not take their places, the form is written node by node into a copy of the figure's shape, which is updated after. Either way the form is read back twice: its nodes, and the box FontLab draws it in. A form whose nodes are not where they were put, or that FontLab draws as it was, is not reported as thickened, and a warning names it with the counts that say where the build stopped (*40 nodes in 10 contours, 40 on-curve, 40 given a new place, 40 found there, 10 shapes drawn as before*).

### What starts ticked

Every feature starts ticked, but:

- one the font's code has by hand (code outside FontLab's automatic block and FreeLab's, or in a block handed back from FontLab) starts unticked, with a note: ticked, FreeLab's block goes in beside it and the rest is kept;
- one whose only code is FontLab's automatic code, when that code already makes every ticked substitution and FreeLab's would add nothing else (no context, no name), starts unticked and is left to FontLab.

When a feature with FontLab's automatic code is written, that code is replaced by FreeLab's block (the `#> feature` line handed back as `#/> feature`, what the block held dropped): two versions of the same feature would otherwise both run. A feature whose FreeLab block is in the font but whose glyphs are gone is listed with no rows; writing takes the block out. An unticked feature's block is taken out as well.

### The checks on real fonts

*features/train/forms_check.py* makes every form feature of an open-licensed font as Feature Code would (on a font with no features), compiles it into a copy of the font with its own `GSUB` taken out (fontTools' feaLib), and shapes test strings with HarfBuzz. On Montserrat Regular, Big Shoulders Display Black, Crimson Roman, and Alegreya SC Regular, 67 of 67 checks pass: *1/2* and *12/25* under `frac`; *(H)* and *A-B* under `case`, in Romanian text too; *a* and *A* under `smcp` and `c2sc`; Romanian *ş* as *ș* (and not in Turkish), Turkish and Azerbaijani *i*, Turkish *i* in small capitals as *i.sc.loclTRK*, Catalan *l·l* (and not in Spanish), Dutch *íj*, Bulgarian *д* (and not in Russian); `ss01`, `tnum`, `onum`, `onum` with `tnum` (*one.tosf*), `sups`, `sinf`, and `ordn` (*1a*, *1.o*, and a lone *a* left alone). ## How FreeLab reads and writes a feature's code

Every writer (Feature Code's liga, dlig, calt, and form features, CJK Spacing's palt and halt, CJK Vertical's vert, vrt2, vpal, and vkrn) reads the font's code as tokens, never as lines: comments, quoted strings, braces, statement ends, and words, each with its place in the text. The CJK tools load Feature Code's script for this, so the three read alike.

- **The feature's braces** are found by statements, comments and strings skipped: a feature written on one line (`feature locl { script latn; language CAT; sub l by ldot; } locl;`), a brace after the last rule, or a keyword, tag, and brace on lines of their own get FreeLab's block inside the braces, on lines of its own, the font's code kept as typed.
- **FreeLab's own block** is found by its two markers as comments, wherever they stand: after code on its line too (`sub b by b.case; # <<< FreeLab forms`). A block is taken out from its opening marker (the code before it on its line kept) to its end marker. Markers that do not pair (one without the other, or a block opened inside another) make the feature unreadable: nothing is written to it, even to take a block out.
- **FontLab's automatic code** (`#> feature` to `#<`) is found the same way: handed back as `#/> feature` when FreeLab writes the feature, what it held dropped for the form features (FreeLab's block makes the same feature).
- **A text without the feature**: no code at all, the feature is made; another feature's code only (`feature kern { … } kern;` given for this tag), this feature is made after it, whole, never as statements at the top level, where they would belong to no feature; code with no feature statement at all is taken for the feature's body, the block after it, but only where its braces balance. Anything else is refused.
- **Refused** means nothing of FreeLab's goes in that feature, and the window says so in its top line and its log (*FreeLab leaves locl as it is: it cannot find where that feature's code begins and ends…*); the rest is written. For liga and dlig, neither is written then, since a ligature moved out of one and not into the other would be lost.
- **The ligatures' rules: FreeLab never moves or takes out a rule the font wrote.** It manages only the rules of its own block and those of FontLab's automatic code (`#> feature` to `#<`) in that feature, right in it: FontLab writes that code again from the glyph names, and FreeLab's block replaces it. A ligature whose rule the font's own code has (anywhere: compact on one line, in a lookup, under a script, language, or lookupflag, or in another feature given under this tag) is shown where it is, never written a second time; so is a ligature whose glyph the font's own code makes in any other way (a class in the rule's input, `sub f [i i.alt] by f_i;`, a named class, escaped names, a context), even where FreeLab does not read that input; set to another feature or to *Off*, the window says that FreeLab does not move it (*FreeLab does not move f_i: the font's own code has its rule…*), and it can be moved in FontLab's *Features* panel. Rules are read as statements wherever they stand, each with the feature it stands in, so another feature's rule is never taken for this one's (Codex review 7). This is the simplest policy that is safe by construction: moving the font's own rules can write a rule a second time, put it under a later `lookupflag`, or take out another feature's rule. What it costs: Feature Code does not move a ligature the designer placed by hand from liga to dlig or turn it off; that is done in the *Features* panel.
- **Where FreeLab's ligature block goes:** where the feature's default scope ends, before its first `script`, `language`, `lookupflag`, or `lookup` statement (a lookup, referred to or defined there, may carry its own lookupflag on: Codex review 8), else before its closing brace, so its rules apply as plain ligatures (after a `lookupflag`, they would take it). An older block left after such a statement is written again in the right place.
- **Taking a block out** (a feature with nothing of FreeLab's left) checks the code's braces before and after, as writing one does: an unclosed `lookup Own {` around FreeLab's block is refused, not written back without the block (Codex review 7).
- **The ligatures' choices last** while the window is open on the same font: a write, a build, or an undo reads the font again, and each ligature FreeLab writes keeps the choice it had (*Off* too: a ligature whose rule *Off* took out is not offered again as its name suggests). A new window starts from the suggestions again.
- **CJK Spacing and CJK Vertical** read and write through Feature Code's script. Where it is missing, does not load, or is an older one without what they call now (an incomplete update: Codex review 8), they write nothing, gray out *Write*, and say in the log to reinstall FreeLab.

## Limits

- Only simple ligature rules (glyph names in, one glyph out) are listed; contextual rules and rules with classes stay as they are, where they are.
- The look back counts glyphs of the glyph's own kind: for a letter, letters, their alternates, ligatures, and accented letters of the font; for a figure, figures and their forms (*one.tf*, *one.numr*); for a punctuation mark, punctuation marks and their forms (*hyphen.case*). A glyph of another kind, a space, a symbol, or a combining mark between ends it, so the periods of *U.S.A.* and the figures of *1.1* and *10:10* do not see each other. A symbol's alternates (*plus.alt1*) are not cycled, and a glyph whose name joins two kinds (*f_period*) is of none.
- Which punctuation marks are *Often* doubled is a convention's list, not a count (the counts are an encyclopedia's): a font for another kind of text may double other marks, and they are a tick away.
- The code for letters taking turns was compiled by fontTools and by *makeotf* 3.6.1, and the code with figures and punctuation by fontTools only; neither yet by FontLab's own compiler. Fonts the size of CJK ones were tried on fakes only, with fontTools (*What one click builds*, below): the turns compile where the font's letters follow one another in its glyph order, and the window counts the room of the glyphs that may stand before a glyph taking turns in that order; whether FontLab exports the glyphs in the order its scripts read them was not checked.
- A variant built of a mark that joins its neighbors (an underscore, a dash drawn to the edges of its width) no longer joins them where it is shifted or turned.
- The other features do not grow that way: on a font of 1,100 letters, each with *.sc*, *.ss01*, and *.case* forms, every form feature together compiles to a `GSUB` table of 22 KB (fontTools; 2.3 KB for the largest lookup), and 1,000 ligatures to 12 KB.
- Writing goes straight into the open font, as one step of FontLab's History: *Edit > Undo* takes it back. With *Compile after writing* (on by default) the features are compiled right after, by the *Features* panel's own *Compile* button (FontLab has no call for it; the panel must have been opened once in the session); off, compile them in the *Features* panel to try them. After the compile, the log says what FontLab's *Output* panel shows once it stops changing: the errors, in red, or *Compiled: FontLab reported no errors.* (with the warnings, where there are some). FontLab clears that panel at each compile, so a panel still empty 16 s after the compile means no message. A panel that holds text and does not change within 3.2 s is not taken for the compile's own (neither its old error nor its silence): the log says it still shows what it showed before, and points to the *Features* panel.
- A ligature that neither the font's code nor its name's suffix (*.liga*, *.dlig*) places starts where most of the open-licensed fonts FreeLab learned from put it: *features/train/ligatures.py* counts, per character sequence, the families that make it in liga or clig and in dlig or hlig, into *features/ligature_model.json* (409 families, 252 with ligatures: *f_i* in liga in 214 of 224, *f_f_i* in 124 of 127, *c_t* and unit squares in dlig). One the corpus does not have starts discretionary, to be safe. The feature's tooltip says why.
- The code is made again as the choices change. Pick a ligature's feature in its box (the list scrolls as usual with the boxes in it); drag between the lists and *Code preview* to give one more room; the *Cycle* column shows the forms a glyph takes in turn. Double-click a ligature, a glyph, or a form to see it in a Glyph window. *Write the code* becomes *Update the code* once FreeLab's code is in the font, and grays out while the font has the code shown. *Restore suggestions* puts the choices, ticks, and set names back as they first came; the log says what was written. A glyph a log line names (a built figure form, a copy to redraw, a variant) is drawn there from the font's own outlines; click it to open it in a Glyph tab. Code is compared with the font's by its tokens, comments and FreeLab's markers included, whatever the spaces and line breaks: FontLab may hand code back spaced otherwise than it was written. A write leaves out the features whose code is the same, and the log names only those it wrote.
- *Code preview* colors the code (keywords, tags, glyph names, classes, comments) while it has 2,000 lines at most. Longer code is shown plain, and the heading says so (*Code preview (shown plain: more than 2,000 lines)*): the code is made again at each click, Qt asks for every line's colors each time, and thousands of lines held up every click (a font of 2,043 ligatures, or a CJK font's classes). What is written to the font is the same either way.
- A list's cells show plain text: a note's markup is taken out before it goes in a cell.
- Every button, check box, dropdown, number box, field, and list of the three tabs has a What's This of its own (the *?* button, then a click; or a right-click, then *What's This?*), and a setting's label has its box's. A widget with none answers with its nearest container's, shown in that container's middle, so the hint of the part's dividers is on the dividers themselves, and a blank space answers with the window's help.
- A button's state is set whatever its container's: Auto Kerning's helper sets a state only when it differs from the one the widget reports, and a widget reports its container's too, so a button grayed out while the window is busy keeps its own state once the work is over.
- The way *Strokes* above 100% writes a form, a new shape given contours placed beforehand (above), ran live on 2026-11-27 in the owner's session: 20 figure forms built as outlines, strokes at 107% of the scaled ones (each side moved out 3.6 units), read back as thickened. `resetInkBox` is called, then `update`, each guarded; the read-back, not either call, is what tells a thickened form. The build says when one is not thickened.
- While the font is read, variants are built, or the code is written, the window's top line says what is under way, and the lists and the buttons under them take no clicks until it is over.
- Features run in the order FontLab's *Features* panel lists them. A new feature goes where FontLab puts it (written in the order `locl`, `case`, `frac`, and so on to `ss01`); `locl` should come before `smcp` for the Turkish *i* to find its small capital: move it up in the panel if the font's other features come first.
- FreeLab's block goes at the end of a feature (first, for a set's name). Where the feature's own code sets a language (`language TRK;`) before it, the block would apply to that language only: the row says so.
- `frac` turns every figure of the selected text into a numerator until the bar: in *1 1/2*, select only *1/2*.
- `aalt` is not updated with the new forms.
- Built figure forms are scaled outlines, not drawings: *Strokes* evens their weight as a whole, not stroke by stroke (thin and thick strokes grow by the same amount). Placements are measured in the current master and used in every master. Superscript and subscript signs (⁺, ₋) and letters (ⁿ, ₐ) are built by Glyph Compose's *Glyph generation* tab, on these figures' numbers; the bar under some ordinal indicators is not built.
- Where FontLab cannot write the prefix from a script, `locl` is not written (its languages would lose the font's other features), and the log gives the lines to add to the prefix by hand. Writing the prefix uses `set_prefix`, proven live on 2026-11-24 ([FAMILY-AUDIT.md](FAMILY-AUDIT.md#fontlab-calls-proven-live-and-not-yet)).

## Stylistic alternates

The third tab holds two jobs, apart from each other: neither is a step of the other. Its list, *Glyphs for alternates*, has a group for each (owner's image 302), laid out as the window's other grouped lists: a bold row with its count in parentheses (*Suggestions (12)*, *Selected glyphs (73)*), whose arrow closes and opens it. The list's columns are the same for both; a cell that means nothing for a row shows *–*.

*Suggestions* lists the glyphs fonts most often give a stylistic alternate, counted in the open-licensed fonts of FreeLab's corpus (*features/train/alternates.py*: in how many families an `ssXX`, `cvXX`, or `salt` feature varies each character; **128** of **412 open-licensed families** have alternates, and *a*, *g*, *Q*, *G*, *R*, *A*, *l*, *I*, *J*, *t*, *y* lead), those the font has: the character, the glyph (drawn), *Fonts with alternates* (the count), and *Current alternates* (those the font has already, drawn). *Make copies to redraw* gives each ticked suggestion a copy named for the next free stylistic set (*a.ss01*), its outlines as they are, to redraw by hand; the *Forms* tab writes the set. No row starts ticked, so the list has no suggested ticks to restore; its ticks are kept when the font is read again after a build, a copy, or an undo. It offers no *Tick high-value glyphs* (owner's image 227): the suite's high-value glyphs, Font Check's and Glyph Compose's, are glyphs a font lacks that would complete languages within reach, and this list holds glyphs the font has. Nor does the list of figure forms to build (image 230): no language of Font Check's data needs a superior, inferior, or ordinal indicator, so none of them is ever among those glyphs. The note over the list keeps to two lines in a window of the size it opens at. The two previews under the settings show their captions and the line between them only while a glyph is drawn, and the handle under them, between the tabs and the code, is drawn as nothing while this tab is shown, since its dots would meet that line (image 11); it is still dragged there.

*Selected glyphs* lists the glyphs *Variants that take turns* builds: those its *Glyphs* dropdown picks, all ticked. A new pick replaces the last; untick a glyph there to leave it out of the build and of the previews. With *The ticked suggestions* (the first choice), it lists the ticked suggestions, and follows them as they are ticked or unticked. The rows have the same columns as the suggestions': *Fonts with alternates* counts a glyph the model counts, and shows *–* for the others (most of a script's glyphs). A pick of more than 6,000 glyphs is counted in the group's row but not listed, and the line says why: one click builds 6,000 variants at most, so it could never be built in one click, and a whole CJK font's rows would take seconds at each pick. Past the first 120 rows, a row's pictures are drawn when it is clicked.

*Tick all* and *Untick all* are in the bottom row while this tab is shown, with *Make copies to redraw* and *Build N variants* at its right; they tick or untick both groups (with *The ticked suggestions* picked, the suggestions, which *Selected glyphs* follows), and the log says how many glyphs each ticked or unticked, and how many of those were in a closed group. *Make copies to redraw* acts on the ticked suggestions, *Build N variants* on the ticked selected glyphs: each button's count and graying follow its own group.

*Variants that take turns* computes copies of each glyph ticked in *Selected glyphs* (a letter's, a figure's, or a punctuation mark's: what the first tab's turns take), changed a little as a whole. It needs no copy made first. *Glyphs* picks the glyphs, the narrowest first:

- *The ticked suggestions*: the glyphs of letters, figures, and punctuation marks ticked in *Suggestions* (the first choice, so nothing is built that was not picked).
- *Often doubled*: those the first tab calls *Often* (letters, *0* and *9*, and the marks doubled by convention), under the name of that tab's button.
- *These glyphs*: their characters typed in the box beside it, one after another, with spaces or without, listed as they are typed. While this choice is picked, the empty box says *Type characters…* (a field's words end with an ellipsis, as every FreeLab field's: owner's image 309); with any other, it is grayed out and empty; with no font, it says *n/a*. Its What's This gives an example (*a g 0 !*).
- *Latin glyphs*, *Cyrillic glyphs*, *Han glyphs*, and so on: a choice per script the font has, Latin first, then the script with the most glyphs. A script's glyphs are its letters (and the few marks Unicode gives one script); figures and shared punctuation belong to none.
- *Lowercase letters*, *Capitals*, and *Capitals and lowercase*, of every script.
- *Everything*: every letter, figure, and punctuation mark.

A choice's name is the name alone; what it holds is in the box's What's This.

A character's script and case are read from tables in *features/alternates_model.json* (*features/train/scripts.py*: Unicode's Script property to U+FFFF, from fontTools' copy of *Scripts.txt*, Unicode 17.0; *features/train/kinds.py*: the categories Ll, and Lu and Lt, Unicode 16.0), not asked of the Python that runs, as the kinds are.

The settings read *Glyphs:*, *Variants each:*, *Strength:*, *Seed:*, and *Baseline: ±* (0 to 500 units, 0 to start with): how far each variant moves up or down besides the strength's own shift. The variants take turns, one up and the next down, so each differs from the one before, each moving between a third of that reach and all of it; the first one's way comes from the seed and the glyph, so glyphs side by side do not all start the same way. A large move shows clipped in the preview, a warning that the variant may reach past the font's line and be clipped in use too. The button says how many variants a click builds (*Build 6 variants*), and the line over the settings, with the paragraph that opens the part, names the glyphs and counts the variants the font has already, which are left as they are. A build of 200 variants or more adds *A build this large may take a bit.* to that line, and nothing more; the window's top line counts its glyphs meanwhile. *Variants each*, *Strength*, and *Seed* are grayed out while no glyph is to build (none ticked in *Selected glyphs*), as the button is; with no font, so is *Glyphs*. A font with none of the suggested glyphs says so above the list. Under the settings, two previews stand side by side for the selected glyph (else the first ticked in *Selected glyphs*; none while no glyph is, as while *These glyphs* is picked and nothing typed): at the left the glyph with the alternates the font has of it (*u.01*, *u.02*, and variants built before), at the right the variants a click would build, a line between them; their captions (*In the font: a*, *To build (Subtle, seed 1): a.alt1, a.alt2*) are drawn smaller and in the quiet color, as every preview's caption (image 246). With nothing to draw, the previews are empty, never hidden: they take the part's spare height, and the part ends with them, right over the splitter's handle: hidden, they would leave that height to the heading and the paragraph over the settings, which Qt spreads into blank bands. *Baseline: ±* (0 at first) lets each variant move up or down by up to that many units besides the strength's own shift, drawn from the seed like the other changes; at 0 it adds nothing, and a seed builds the variants it built without the field.

### Building from the font's alternates

With *Also build variants from existing alternates.* ticked, a glyph's variants are spread over its designs in turn: the glyph, then the alternates it has, named with one suffix (a set or a character variant, *.salt*, *.alt*, *.calt1*, a number, or a variant built before: *W.ss01*, *W.2*, *W.alt1*). With two designs and four variants, variants 1 and 3 are changed copies of *W*, and 2 and 4 of *W.ss01*; each is a copy of one design, never a blend of two. A variant of an alternate is named after it (*W.ss01.alt2*), so its origin reads from its name, and a number is given once per glyph whatever the design (the font never has both *W.alt2* and *W.ss01.alt2*). The two previews show it: *In the font: W, W.ss01* at the left, *To build: W.alt1, W.ss01.alt2* at the right, each drawn from the design it copies; the line counts the variants that come from alternates, as the log does. While no glyph in scope has an alternate to build from, the option is grayed out and counts as unticked, its tooltip and its What's This say why, and its tick is kept for glyphs that have some. Variants built before count as alternates.

The first tab cycles them all in the glyph's turn (*W*, *W.alt1*, *W.ss01.alt2*), so a repeated glyph changes design as well as detail: that is what the option is for, in a handwriting font. It reads a name of that form only while the font has the alternate named in it. The alternate itself keeps its own role: *W.2* and *W.alt* take turns, and *W.ss01* stays a stylistic set, the *Forms* tab's (whose `ss01` pairs *W* with *W.ss01* alone: no *W.alt2* exists for it to pair *W.ss01.alt2* with). A variant built before (*X.alt1*) is a design to build from; a variant of an alternate (*X.ss01.alt2*) is not, which would drift twice from the drawing.

### Building again with another seed

Variants this tab built, and nobody changed since, are its own: each one's outlines in every master are noted as the build left them, with the strength and seed it was built with, for as long as the window is open (as the figure forms are). Change *Strength* or *Seed*, and they are planned again: the button counts them (*Build 3 variants*), the line says they replace variants built here with other settings, the right preview draws them as they would be, and a click empties each and makes it again in place (its name, and the code that uses it, stay), new variants beside them, as one step of FontLab's History: *Edit > Undo* takes it all back. With the strength and seed they were built with, they are left as they are (*Nothing to build of a: the font has these variants already (see left)*). A variant the font had before the window was opened, or one drawn over, given another width, or changed any other way since, is the user's, and never replaced.

The other way was to keep each design's variants to itself (*W.ss01.alt1* taking turns only with *W.ss01*, for text set with the stylistic set on). It was left aside: the turns are written on the glyph typed, and a set's feature usually runs before `calt`, so that needs a second cycle per design and an order of features the tool does not set.

### What one click builds

A build is refused before anything is made, in the line over the settings, with the button grayed out:

- when the font would pass 65,535 glyphs, the most a font holds (*the font has 59,057 glyphs, this build would add 50,336 glyphs, and a font holds 65,535 glyphs at most*);
- past 6,000 variants in one click: what the first tab's turns can serve (8 bytes each, of the 48,000 it allows), so more could not all take turns;
- when the pick holds more than 6,000 glyphs (one variant each would pass that already): *Selected glyphs* then counts them without listing them;
- when the build would take more than 10 minutes at the pace of the session's last build of 20 variants or more. FontLab's pace is not known before a first build, so the first one is limited by the count alone.

### The variants

A figure or a mark is varied by the recipe a letter is. Every change is made about the glyph's own ink (the center of its box), none against the x-height or the baseline, so a small mark is changed in proportion: at *Subtle*, a period 100 units wide and high grows or shrinks by 1 unit a side at most (2%), and its rotation (0.8°) and slant (1°) each move its corners under 1 unit, a seventh of what they move a 700-unit letter's; the three together move a corner about 3 units at most (6 at *Moderate*, 12 at *Bold*). The baseline shift is a share of the em, not of the glyph (5, 10, or 20 units of 1,000), so it is the change that shows on a period or a comma, as a dot set a little high or low; a hyphen 300 units long also shows its rotation (its ends 2 units up and down at *Subtle*, 8 at *Bold*). A variant keeps its glyph's advance width, so tabular figures stay tabular. The changes are a slight rotation, uneven scaling, a slant, and a baseline shift, each drawn within the chosen strength's reach (*Subtle*: 0.8°, 2%, 1°, 0.5% of the em; *Moderate*: 1.5°, 4%, 2°, 1%; *Bold*: 3°, 7%, 4°, 2%) from a seed, so the same seed makes the same variants. They are named *X.alt1*, *X.alt2*… (*X.ss01.alt2* for a variant of an alternate), which *Alternates that take turns* on the first tab cycles. Nodes are never moved one by one: tools that jitter every point break smooth curves and read as noise rather than a hand; whole-glyph changes keep the drawing. Copies to redraw and variants are made in every master that draws the glyph (each variant changed around that master's own center); a glyph the current master does not draw is skipped. A font with several masters or drawn alternates has better material for variety; these variants are for the many fonts that have neither.
