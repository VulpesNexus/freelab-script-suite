# History

How each tool came to work as it does: what it did before, what it does now, and why it changed, moved here from the tools' pages, which describe only the present. Dates are given where the pages gave them; image and report numbers are the owner's.

## Dark Mode

- 2026-11-14: Switching dark mode back and forth left parts of open FreeLab windows stale until they were opened again (the owner's screenshots of *Feature Code*). In light mode, the ligatures list kept dark cells, and its labels and first tab kept pale text; in dark mode, those labels looked ghosted, the *Often doubled*, *All*, and *None* buttons looked struck, and glyph pictures stayed black. Dark mode now tells FreeLab once it has switched (`sys._freelab_theme_hook`), and each window polishes its widgets again and redraws what it drew in the old colors.
- 2026-11-14: Three crashes of FontLab came from PythonQt freeing a wrapper twice, at the end of tab docking's walk over every widget (some 5,000 wrapped each second). Dark mode's worker timer also stopped itself when its queue drained and dropped its last reference while it emitted. Since then FreeLab hands a closed tool window to Qt, tab docking finds the document areas from FontLab's windows, and dark mode's timers have FontLab's application as their parent and are kept.
- 2026-11-15: The widget sweep took 24 to 33 ms every 0.75 s, with about 2,200 PythonQt wrappers made and freed each time. Since it holds every widget's wrapper, it takes 9 to 10 ms every 0.5 s and makes none, about half the time spent per second. In the same live measurement, FontLab panels opened for the first time were looked at after 508 and 841 ms before the change and 203, 443, and 664 ms after; a FreeLab window (*Font Compare*) after 1.30 to 1.44 s before and 1.26 to 1.39 s after.
- 2026-11-19: From November 7 to November 19, 2026, *Dark Mode Settings* offered a second, experimental engine for the islands: a library of hooks (*darkmode/native/freelab_hooks.c*, built into a *freelab_hooks* DLL) that patched FontLab's in-memory import tables, so that the colors FontLab handed to Qt were mapped before anything was drawn, with a start guard (`native_pending`) that fell back to the script engine after a crash. The owner removed it on November 19. The script engine stayed as it was, and the pixel helper (*freelab_pixels.c*) was never part of the native engine; the engine's description, status, and comparison are in the Git history (*docs/NATIVE.md*, last in the commit before its removal).
- 2026-11-23: A dark menu's tick (the switches of *Scripts > FreeLab*, FontLab's *View* entries) was Fusion's filled box, whose outline, a pixel wider and taller than dark mode's rounded frame, showed as dark pixels around it on the highlighted row (image 13). At the owner's request it is now drawn as FontLab's light look draws it: the mark alone, in the text's color.
- 2026-11-23: Two parts of FreeLab's windows still kept the theme before a switch (the review of November 23, images 2 to 4). A dropdown's open list, a window of its own that the walk over a window's widgets stopped at, opened dark on a light window and light on a dark one; it is now walked with its window (`popup_windows`). The grayed fields and spin boxes of a light window (`light_fields`) had one background set on a copy of their own palette, which kept the old theme's other states; all three states are now set from the application's current palette.
- The color mapping once kept HLS saturation, which turned FontLab's #fffffa metrics background khaki and bluish whites navy. It now works in OKLab and mutes chroma toward white, so tinted whites and pastels turn gray.
- The ink effect replaced dark mode 0.2's selective effect, which mapped only near-white pixels and left the smoothing around them dark.
- Native helper 106 gave a list's rows a variant of ink. Under plain ink, FontLab's dark gray glyph icons in the font window's sidebar (*Number*'s 2, *Mark*'s tilde) passed for dark surfaces, and its translucent gray sub-items (*None*, *uppercase*, *Regular*) fell just short of dim ink, so both stayed dark on the dark panel and looked hollow.
- Native helper 108 thinned the smoothing of light ink drawn on nothing, at the edges only. Light ink there had looked heavier than the dark ink it came from, and the counters of small bold text had filled in.
- Native helper 110 kept the counters of light text on a row FontLab paints dark itself (the selected row of *Export Profiles*) as the panel's. Mapped as glyph icons, those counters had filled the letters with light gray, and the text looked struck twice.
- Native helper 111 stopped *Preview Features*' one light row from bending the colors of its dark rows. Its light gray text had passed for smoothing between that row and a yellowish fringe, and its dark gray rings for smoothing between the dark base and a pale fringe; dropped as blends, both were redrawn dark on dark. Since then, such colors stay as painted, and text in the dark interface's text color stays as drawn on the row once it turns dark.
- Native helper 114 kept light text on a gray badge light (*Anchors & Pins*' coordinates). Before it, the digits were mapped with the list's white and came out dark on the badge, their light fringes kept around them; a badge with such text that came out too light is now drawn darker, at the *Classes* panel's badge gray.
- Native helper 115 fixed the compiler's dark red errors in FontLab's *Output* panel. Their ClearType fringes (#340c00) had been mapped alone as dark ink and came out light pink (#eac2b6), light dots inside every letter (owner's report 155); an earlier fix, helper 109, had covered the bright red of Python's tracebacks, whose fringes are smoothing of lighter text. Each such fringe is now drawn again channel by channel from the paper and the text. On the 215 captures and screenshots kept, every mode then mapped exactly as helper 114 did, and on 85 raw captures of FontLab's widgets, plain ink mapped exactly as before.
- Before only colored pixels went through the pair search, a font window full of smoothed glyphs took 215 ms to map.
- 2026-11-24: The glyph window's bar of glyphs showed a white band beside its cells when a font had fewer glyphs than the bar had room for. A cell chart paints its cells only, and the light background of the widget behind it showed through. A cell chart now fills itself, in a color its effect maps to the bar's dark one.
- 2026-11-24: Font Builder's *Ink darker than:* caption looked blurrier than *Make:* beside it. Dark mode had taken the label for fixed dark ink and given it the ink effect, and text drawn through an effect loses ClearType. Labels of FreeLab's own windows are drawn from the palette and are no longer looked at for ink.
- 2026-11-24: Dragging a window over the main window stalled for two or three seconds as FontLab's blue highlight came up, and again at the merge; with dark mode off, it did not. A profile of the owner's own drag named the cause: the highlight grows step by step, and dark mode rendered it and counted its pixels at each new size, 4.6 of the 7.4 seconds profiled. It is no longer looked at. Two earlier guesses went to the repaints under it: a cache keyed on Qt's source pixmap, which never hit (Qt keeps none for a widget), then large widgets' pictures held for the length of a drag, which stays (111 ms a repaint down to 1).
- 2026-11-24: A font window merged back into the main window came out light under its cells: the fill color a cell chart had been given that morning, through its palette, did not outlast the repolish a move between windows makes. It is now set in the chart's stylesheet.

## Auto Kerning and Kerning Check

- 2026-11-14: The FreeLab method's model trained on the widened corpus replaced the one shipped before, which scored 8.6 over all pairs and 21.1 on kerned pairs on the corpus as it was then. That scoring did not yet merge families that kern alike into one design or pool each script's pairs across folds.
- Until November 2026, Auto Kerning's list was a table, and in dark mode its header came out washed out. It is now laid out as the other tools' lists, which ask dark mode to leave them alone, so the header is drawn from the dark palette.
- Kerning Check's three tabs had settings rows of different heights: the *Masters* row (*Compare masters* alone) was a few pixels shorter than *Cleanup*'s, whose spin box is taller (owner's images 285 and 286), so the list moved as tabs were switched. Each row now stands in a widget as tall as the tallest row (`level_rows`).
- An earlier version of the check on the owner's test font read only the font's glyph pairs and missed its class kerning, so it favored the FreeLab method wrongly. The check now reads the kerning as HarfBuzz applies it, classes included, and contour distance comes closer on the kerned pairs.
- Before every tool read each kerning key's direction from its own glyphs, Kerning Check measured an Arabic key's ink gap on the wrong sides, and Font Compare drew a Hebrew pair the wrong way round. Kerning Check, Font Compare, and Proof now read every font's keys by `RTL_RANGES` and `direction_char`, an Arabic font's included.
- 2026-11-24: Kerning Classes (*Suggest classes…*) told users to close the font without saving to take a write back. Tried live on a font opened afresh, one *Edit > Undo* takes a created class back. Its two writes now record the font in FontLab's History first, as Auto Kerning's write does, and its status line says *Edit > Undo* takes them back.

## Auto Spacing

- 2026-11-18: *l* joined *H*, *O*, *n*, *o*, and zero as a reference Auto Spacing suggests for a font not spaced yet, measured in the 457 families at hand then (0.10 of the x-height on its left, 0.07 on its right). Every glyph is measured against *l*, *n*, and *o*, and *n* and *o* spaced without *l* had left *ll* far from *nn* and *oo*, so nothing could be spaced after (image 335).
- Spacing the ticked glyphs took several clicks, each listing fewer glyphs (24, then 13, then 4 on the owner's font): a glyph spaced once asked for more when measured again. Offline, on open-licensed fonts unspaced at random by up to 40 units a side, it took three or four clicks (Noto Sans listed 39, 7, then 0 glyphs; Arimo 27, 11, 1, then 0; EB Garamond 33, 7, 1, then 0), and without halfkern's half strength a second click still listed one or two glyphs. The analysis now measures each change again with it made until it settles, so one click is enough.
