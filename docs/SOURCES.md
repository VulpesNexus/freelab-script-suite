# Sources: in use, weighed, and to watch

What FreeLab's data and fonts are made from, what was looked at and left out, and what to look at again when the data is updated. The data itself is described in [FONT-CHECK.md](FONT-CHECK.md#the-data); this page is the register of sources, so that the next update starts from what is known rather than from a search. Dates are the days a page was read. A source is shipped only when it can be pinned (a release, a commit, a checksum) and its license, quoted from the source itself, allows redistribution and changes in a GPL-3.0-or-later repository.

## Open: Tai Yo

Since 2026-11-14 FreeLang draws Tai Yo's 55 characters from GNU Unifont, as pixels: no outline font under a license FreeLang can take has them. The only outline font known, "Noto Serif Tai Yo" 1.030 (made with FontCreator on 2023-09-04; distributed by Language Tools since September 2023 and bundled by [Unicodia](https://github.com/Mercury13/unicodia)), carries no copyright, license, or designer record, and no license file accompanies it: a repository's license covers what its own licensor can license, not a third party's font placed in it. It is not a Noto font; [notofonts.github.io issue 81](https://github.com/notofonts/notofonts.github.io/issues/81), which offers a file of that name to Noto, is open and its sources were never supplied. The script's proposal is L2/22-289R, "Final Proposal to encode the Tai Yo Script", by Viet Khoi Nguyen, Cong Danh Sam, and Frank van de Kasteelen (2022-12-16), whose form names Viet Khoi Nguyen as the one who would provide a font and leaves the license line blank. Who to ask for a license: Viet Khoi Nguyen first, then Craig Cornelius (who can say where the file came from), then the proposal's co-authors.

## Not supplied by any font

Until 2026-11-14 these had no source: Tai Yo, Syriac Supplement (U+0860 to U+086A: the one outline font found forbids commercial use), Bopomofo Extended U+31BC to U+31BF, 14 pictographs of Unicode 16 and 17, two CJK strokes of Unicode 16 (U+31E4, U+31E5), the squared katakana *PAATU* (U+332C), and the six symbols of Chinese folk religion (U+1F260 to U+1F265). Plangothic now draws the CJK ones (Jigmo until 2026-11-14), Tai Yo, and Syriac Supplement, and GNU Unifont the emoji, as pixels. Since every script but Han is drawn whole, one group has no source at all: the non-core signs of Egyptian Hieroglyphs Extended-A, which NewGardiner leaves out as unverified; Tangut's additions of Unicode 16 and 17 (U+187F8 to U+187FF, U+18D09 to U+18D1E, U+18D80 to U+18DF2) had none until Plangothic P2, which also draws Tai Yo and the Syriac and Arabic characters Unifont drew (2026-11-14); [COVERAGE-GAPS.md](COVERAGE-GAPS.md) counts them. Look again at each update: Noto, then a font designed for the script, then a broad-coverage font, and Unifont's pixels last.
