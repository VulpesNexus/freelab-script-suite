# Family Audit

*Scripts > FreeLab > Family Audit* compares the fonts of a family with one another, before they become masters of one font: the glyphs each lacks and the code points that differ, each glyph's structure across them, their font info side by side, their kerning, and their feature code. It changes nothing in any of them. Its one action, *Assemble as masters*, makes a new font with a master per source, and leaves the sources as they were.

A survey of 4,986 fonts (the owner's and the corpus's) found that only about 7% of static Regular and Bold pairs are compatible for interpolation as shipped, that within families of several files the glyph sets differ in 39%, the versions in 13%, and the feature code in 16%, and that the owner's own FontLab projects mostly hold one master each. So the audit comes first: it says what differs and where, which is what tidying a family takes; assembling the masters is a second step, which reports compatibility and never promises interpolation.

## The window

The window keeps every FreeLab window's layout ([WINDOWS.md](WINDOWS.md)). The top line names the window's font and how many sources are ticked, with the reference: *Sans: 3 of 5 sources ticked (reference Regular).* (the counts with their noun in bold); under it, the status line of the tab shown, then the header's line. The options follow, in one row: *Add files…*, *Reference:* [a dropdown], and *Compare* at their end. Under them, the sources' list over the results, in a splitter: the list's frame, the handle right under it, then a line, the tabs, and the tab's list. The log follows, then the bottom row: *Tick all*, *Untick all*, and *Show in Glyph window* at the left, *Assemble as masters*, *Cancel*, and *Close* at the right.

With no font open, the top line reads *No font is open. This window hooks to the next font you open, or click Add files… to open a family's fonts.*, on one line. Every control is grayed then but *Add files…*, which opens fonts, and the *Reference* dropdown reads a grayed *n/a*.

## Sources

The sources' list holds the fonts open in FontLab, a row for each master of a font of several masters (a variable font, a designspace, a Glyphs file, a FontLab project with masters). Its columns: the source's name in the window, its file, the file's format (*VFC*, *UFO*, *OTF*, *unsaved*…), its font's masters, its glyphs, and its version. A source is named by its style, or by its master in a font of several; two sources of one name take their font's name in parentheses (*Regular (Sans)*), then their file's (*Bold (Sans, old.otf)*), then a number.

To start with, the window's own font is ticked with every open font of its family (the same family name, FontLab's `tfn`: a variable font's `fgPackage.info.familyName` is its name ID 1, *Noto Sans JP Thin* for *NotoSansJP[wght].ttf*, live); with no font of its own, the family most open fonts belong to. The list is read again each time the window becomes the active one: the ticks are kept, and a new font of the family is ticked. A font closed leaves the list.

*Add files…* opens font files in FontLab, several at once, in any format FontLab 7 opens: FontLab projects (*.vfc*, *.vfj*, *.vfb*), UFO (*.ufo* is a folder, which a file dialog cannot pick: pick any file inside it, *metainfo.plist* for one) and *.ufoz*, Glyphs, designspace, OpenType and TrueType (*.otf*, *.ttf*, *.otc*, *.ttc*), WOFF and WOFF2, and Type 1 (*.pfb*, *.pfa*). FontLab opens a font in the background: the window looks for each font twice a second, for up to a minute, matches it with its file by path (a font that gives no path is taken for the first file still awaited, once the fonts with paths are matched), ticks it, and says so in the log: *Opened Sans-Bold.otf in FontLab: ticked as Bold.* A font of another family than the window's (its own font's, else the one the ticked sources belong to) is left unticked, and the log says so: tick it to compare it all the same. A file open already is ticked at once. A file that never comes in is said, as FontLab may be waiting for an answer in a dialog of its own (*Coordinates Rounding*, for some fonts).

### Fonts FontLab 7.2 crashes on

FontLab 7.2 can crash as it opens a font whose `GPOS` holds a single adjustment subtable (lookup type 1, or an extension of one) with no values: of format 2 with a `ValueCount` of 0 (*NotoSansJP[wght].ttf* and *NotoSansKR[wght].ttf* among the test fonts, and the variable Noto Sans SC, TC, and HK), or of format 1 over no glyph (a static instance of Noto Sans JP at weight 700 keeps the two subtables with no glyph left; its copy fixed to format 1 crashed FontLab on 2026-11-27, in the same function). Not every time: it depends on what FontLab's memory held. A crash loses the unsaved work in every open font. FreeLab MCP's *docs/FONTLAB7-API.md* has the bisect that proved it.

So *Add files…* reads each file picked before FontLab is asked to open it: its table directory, then its `GPOS` lookups, in the file's own bytes (no fontTools; the shared `crash_checked_paths` in *kerning/freelab_auto_kerning.py*). OpenType and TrueType files, every font of a collection, and WOFF are checked; WOFF2 needs brotli 1.2 or later (which stops inflating at the length the file's tables say), and FontLab's Python has none, so a WOFF2 is opened unchecked and the log says so, as for a file whose tables cannot be read. A part that cannot be read loses nothing found elsewhere: a damaged lookup, a subtable whose coverage cannot be read, or a collection's font whose tables cannot be listed, is skipped and the rest read, a subtable found anywhere is asked about as below, and the log says what was not checked. The reading stops after 200,000 subtables, coverage ranges, and table directory entries in the whole file: lookups may share their data, so a small `GPOS` could list billions of them and hold FontLab for an hour or more, where a CJK font lists some thousands, and a collection may list one font or one `GPOS` over and over (a `GPOS` its fonts share is read once). FontLab and source formats (*.vfc*, *.vfj*, *.ufo*, *.glyphs*, and the rest) hold no binary `GPOS` and are not checked.

When some files hold such a subtable, one question (*A font FontLab crashes on*) names them, says why, and offers *Open a fixed copy* (the default) or *Skip it* (*Open fixed copies* and *Skip them* for several). When part of a file could not be checked, the question says so before it offers the copy (the copy fixes what was found, and FontLab may still crash on the rest, so save the work in the open fonts first), and *Skip* is then the default. A fixed copy is written to *FreeLab\safe-copies* in the system's temporary folder, in a folder named after the original's full path, under the original's file name, and opened instead; each such subtable becomes a format 1 subtable with no values over the same coverage, which positions nothing either and which FontLab opens, or, where it covers no glyph (or its coverage cannot be read), is taken out of its lookup (the lookup's later subtable offsets moved up in place: the copy of the static instance above then opened); nothing else in the file changes but its checksums. The copy as written is checked again: one that still holds such a subtable is not kept. The file picked is only read. The log says, for each file, what was done: the copy's folder, or that it was skipped, or that no copy could be made (a WOFF2 is never copied: convert it to TTF or OTF first). A file whose copy is open already is not asked about again: the copy is ticked, not written again; nor is a file picked again while it, or its copy, is still being opened (the log says it is being opened already, and it is ticked once it is in). A file declaring lengths past its own end is refused before they are read, and a WOFF table is inflated no further than its directory says (256 MB at most). A write that fails leaves no *.tmp* behind; the copies themselves are kept, since a font opened from one may be saved beside it. Font Compare's *Open a font…* makes the same check, and FreeLab MCP's `open_font` makes it too, with its own copy of the code.

*Reference:* is the source the others are compared with, and whose glyph order, feature code, classes, and font info a new font assembled from them takes. To start with, it is the upright source whose weight is nearest Regular's 400 (a style named Regular, Book, Roman, Normal, or Plain first), of the normal width: its weight class from its font info, else from its style's name (*SemiBold* 600, *Black* 900); a font of several masters goes by its masters' names, since its font info is the font's, not each master's. A choice stays while its source is ticked.

*Compare* reads the ticked sources (at least two) and fills the tabs. It shows its steps in the window's top row and can be stopped there. A tab counts what differs in parentheses: *Compatibility (40)*. Ticking another source or choosing another reference afterward leaves the tabs as they were, says so in the status line, and grays *Assemble as masters* until *Compare* is clicked again. A tab with nothing that differs says *(alike)* rather than *(0)* (images 327 and 328). The audit is kept for the FontLab session: a Family Audit window opened again (after another tool, or closed) shows it again, its sources ticked, as long as every source is still open and none changed since (each font's place in its History), and the log says so; otherwise it shows nothing until *Compare* (image 330).

## Glyph sets

The glyphs each source lacks that another has, a group a source (*Missing from Bold (12 glyphs)*), each row saying which sources have it. A master lacks a glyph that has no layer in it. Then the code points that differ: a glyph name with other code points in another source (*U+0061 in Regular; none in Bold*), and a code point given to glyphs of other names (*U+00E9: eacute in Regular; uni00E9 in Bold*). Glyphs are matched by name across a family, since a family's members should share their names; a renamed glyph shows both as missing and under its code point.

## Compatibility

Each glyph in at least two sources is compared, each source with the reference (or, where the reference lacks the glyph, with the first source that has it), in this order: the number of contours, the components (names, in order), the number of nodes of each contour, the node types of each contour (on-curve, curve, off-curve), the direction of each contour, and the anchors (by name). The first difference found is the glyph's: *contour 2: 12 nodes in Regular, 14 in Bold*, *contour 1 runs the other way in Black*, *components in another order in Bold*. The glyphs are grouped by that first difference, then *Compatible*, closed.

A glyph whose structure matches in every source has what interpolation needs, but no more: start points, contour order (contours of the same structure in another order), and the shapes themselves are not compared, and FontLab may still find two layers incompatible. The status line says how many glyphs match and that it promises nothing more: *312 of 340 glyphs match in structure (contours, nodes, components, and anchors). That is needed for them to interpolate, but does not promise it.* Sources of other outline formats (a TrueType file and a CFF one) differ in nearly every glyph, as they should: their curves are made of other points.

*Show in Glyph window* (or a double-click on a row) opens the selected row's glyphs in a *Glyph* window of the window's font, as every FreeLab list does.

## Metrics and info

The font info a column per source: family and style names, version, units per em, ascender, descender, x-height, cap height, line gap, the OpenType typo, hhea, and win values, italic angle, and weight and width classes. Three groups: *Should match, but differ* (what a family shares: names, version, units per em, the line metrics and the OS/2 and hhea values that set line spacing), *Differ by style* (style name, x-height, cap height, italic angle, weight and width classes, which may differ on purpose), and *Alike*, closed. A field no source gives is left out.

FontLab gives a script the line metrics (ascender, descender, x-height, cap height, line gap) of a font's current master only, and the tool never switches a source's master: another master's are left blank.

## Kerning

As Kerning Check's *Masters* tab compares a font's masters: the kerning classes whose members differ between sources (against the reference's: *Bold lacks oacute; Bold adds odieresis*), or that only some sources have; the entries only some sources kern (*not kerned in Black*); and the entries that tighten in one source and loosen in another by more than 10 units, where an instance between them may collide. A source with no kerning at all is said once, rather than against its every entry; a source whose kerning cannot be read is left out, and the status line says so.

## Features

The prefix, each feature, and the Classes panel's classes, as Font Compare splits them, each source's code compared with the reference's line by line: the features some sources lack, then those whose code differs, then those alike, closed. A row under each feature for each source that differs (*3 lines only in Regular, 2 lines only in Bold*); selecting it shows its lines beside the list, those only in the reference on red after a minus, those only in that source on green after a plus, as Font Compare shows them.

## Assemble as masters

After an audit, *Assemble 3 sources as masters* (its count follows the sources) makes a new font in FontLab, which opens in its own window, and never changes the sources:

- a master per audited source, named by the source's name in the window, the reference's first (the default master);
- every glyph of every source, in the reference's glyph order, then the glyphs only other sources have, in the order they come; each glyph's layer in a master copied by name from that master's source: its outlines (the contours copied, nothing shared with the source), its components (made once the glyphs they draw are drawn, as FontLab builds a component from its glyph as it is then), its anchors, and its width; a glyph a source lacks has an empty layer in its master, and the log lists those glyphs master by master;
- the code points of the reference's glyphs (else of the first source that has the glyph); where sources give a glyph other code points, the reference's are kept, and the log says which;
- each master's kerning from its source: its classes, then its entries;
- the reference's feature code, its prefix with the Classes panel's feature classes written first as code (FontLab offers a script no way to fill the Classes panel), and its font info (family name, version, the OpenType line values, copyright, designer, manufacturer, license, vendor);
- the reference's Unicode ranges and code pages, updated for what the new font covers as the tools update them after a write ([WINDOWS.md](WINDOWS.md#the-font-info)): a range or code page the other sources' glyphs complete is set, and the reference's own choices stay; and the largest Win ascent and descent of the sources (each source's covers its own glyphs; the font's are one for every master), the log saying so where it is not the reference's;
- each master's line metrics from its source, where they could be read (see *Metrics and info*);
- a *Weight* axis (*wght*) where the sources' weight classes differ, a *Width* axis (*wdth*) where their width classes differ, each master placed at its source's classes; no axis where they are alike. Sources that would share a location (a Regular and an Italic of one weight) are said: an italic needs an axis of its own, which the tool does not set.

The units per em are the reference's; sources with other units per em are copied unscaled, and the log says so. The new font is marked modified and not saved: there is no step to undo in its History, since it did not exist before; close it without saving to take it back. The log says what was made (*Made a new font with 3 masters (Regular, Light, and Bold) and 412 glyphs; 9 layers are left empty where a source lacks their glyphs.*), how many glyphs matched in structure at the audit (that is needed to interpolate, not a promise of it), and, as warnings, whatever could not be carried over: a line a kind, its glyphs listed in short, with FontLab's own error, so that a call FontLab refuses for every glyph is one line, not thousands. When FontLab makes no font, or not its masters, nothing is placed in it: the log says the font was left unfinished, to close without saving.

The sources are read again as *Assemble* starts, as they are then. Making the new font cannot be stopped halfway: its steps show in the top row until it is done.

## FontLab calls proven live, and not yet

Every FontLab call of the tool is a method of `FontLabCalls`, each with a docstring saying what it assumes. Proven before (FreeLab MCP's *FONTLAB7-API.md*, Font Compare, Feature Code, Glyph Compose): the open fonts and their numbers, `masters`, `master`, `tfn`, `tsn`, `path`, `versionMajor` and `versionMinor`, `fgPackage.glyphs` and their `unicodes`, `findName`, `getLayerByName` (with `True` to make a layer), a layer's shapes, contours, nodes and their types, components and anchors, `kerning(master)` read through FontKerning and written through it, `groups[name] = (members, side)`, `get_feature`, `get_prefix`, `set_feature`, `getAllGroups`, `requestLoadingFont`, `flPackage()` for a new font, `flGlyph()` and `addGlyph`, `flShape()` with `addContours` and `flContour.clone()`, `GlyphComponent` with `setGlyphComponents`, `flPinPoint` with `addAnchor`, `advanceWidth`, `fgGlyph.unicodes`, `flGlyph.update()`, `rebuildMaps()`, `notifyPackageContentUpdated`, and `setFlag(2, True)`. Proven on 2026-11-24, by assembling Sarabun Regular and Bold (two static fonts of 777 glyphs, opened from their files) in FontLab 7.2 and reading the new font back:

- `renameMaster`, `addMaster(name, False, package, "", False, False)`, and `setMaster(name)`: the masters read *Regular* and *Bold*, and every glyph has a layer in both.
- A component's `transform` copied to the new shape: *Aacute*'s acute sits at 298 in Regular and 321 in Bold, as in the sources.
- `set_prefix`: the prefix reads back (7,332 characters), with 13 features.
- `setUpm(units, False)`, `versionMajor` and `versionMinor` (set and read back), and the font info's text fields and OS/2 and hhea values through `setattr` (family name, copyright, designer, typo ascender and descender, win ascent, hhea ascender read back).
- The line metrics (`ascender_value` and the others) are settable and are the current master's: `xHeight_value` set to 510 with Bold current read 500 with Regular current.
- Where the export takes the OpenType line values (live, 2026-11-27, see [FONT-CHECK.md](FONT-CHECK.md#fontlab-calls-not-yet-proven-live)): the typo values are each master's *Ascender*, *Descender*, and *Line gap*, the Win values its *Safe top* and *Safe bottom*, and the hhea values the typo ones unless *Custom hhea linespacing* is on; values set in `fgPackage.info` alone were exported as the master's. So the new font's typo, hhea, and Win values are written into every master (`flPackage.info`, each master made current in turn), and a source's are read from its master's own: the largest Win ascent and descent of the sources reach the exported font.
- `flAxis` with `minimal`, `default`, and `maximum`, `addAxis`, and `setLocation(flLocation, master)`: the font has a *Weight* axis (`wght`, 400 to 700), and the masters' locations read `<flLocation weight=400>` and `<flLocation weight=700>`.
- `notifyPackageAdded`: the new font's Font window opens as a tab of the main window (titled after the family, its names set before the window opens).
- `italicAngle`, `openTypeOS2WeightClass` (400 and 700), `openTypeOS2WidthClass` (5), and `styleName` read as *Font Info* gives them; a font opened from a TrueType file gives that file as its `path`.
- Each master kept its source's kerning (2,394 entries and 50 classes each).

- `setGlyphComponents` on a layer that also gets contours (live, 2026-11-26, on scratch layers of a test font): it replaces every shape of the layer, so contours added before it are lost (*O*'s two contours, then an *acute* component: the acute alone), while contours added after it stay (the acute and the two contours). The assembly sets a glyph's components first and copies its contours after (`set_components`, then `copy`), so a glyph of both keeps both.

- The other formats through `requestLoadingFont` (live, 2026-11-26, each opened in the background within 8 s and closed unchanged): a UFO (1,374 glyphs, written by `fgPackage.save`), a VFB (written by `fgPackage.save(path, True)`), a WOFF (Amatic SC, 850 glyphs, made with fontTools), and an OpenType CFF font (Oliver, 28 glyphs), each giving its file as `path`, with its masters and family name read as from a TrueType file.

- Type 1 and *.vfc* through `requestLoadingFont` (live, 2026-11-27): a Type 1 font made from Sarabun with fontTools (*.pfb*, 374 glyphs) opened as *Sarabun T1*, one master, its *.pfb* as its `path`; a copy of a *.vfc* FontLab wrote (its sketchboard) opened as *Untitled*, its path given with forward slashes (*C:/…/SketchCopy.vfc*), which `same_path` matches.
- How long it takes (live, 2026-11-27, timed inside FontLab): four static instances of Noto Sans (4,515 glyphs each), compared in 4.0 s and assembled in 14.4 s (four masters, every glyph matched); two of Noto Sans JP (17,936 glyphs each, Regular and Bold), compared in 10.1 s and assembled in 57.3 s (17,886 glyphs matched in structure), FontLab holding 5.1 GB after; Sarabun's two styles (777 glyphs), compared in 0.4 s and assembled in 2.3 s.
- The new font's names (live, 2026-11-27): each master has its own style, full, and PostScript names (`tsn`, `ffn`, and `psn` read the current master's). Only the first master was named, so the second kept *Unnamed Regular* and *Unnamed-Regular*, and a master named after its source's label put parentheses and commas in a PostScript name. Each master is now named with it current, by its source's style (*Sarabun Bold*, *Sarabun-Bold*), and the PostScript name keeps only what PostScript allows. FontLab then opened the new font's window on its last master: the reference's master is made current again once the window is made (300 ms after), and was.

Not yet proven: nothing of the tool's own calls.

- *NotoSansJP[wght].ttf* and *Bellefair-Regular.ttf* added: one question; *Skip it* opened only Bellefair (left unticked, of another family), and the log said the other was skipped.
- Added again, *Open a fixed copy*: the copy opened from *FreeLab\safe-copies* in the temporary folder, matched by its path, without a crash. It was left unticked at first, as of another family (*Noto Sans JP Thin*, its `familyName`): the family is now FontLab's `tfn` (*Noto Sans JP*), and the copy was ticked as *Thin* and *Black*.
- Added a third time: the question was asked again, and the log said a copy was opened, while the copy open already was ticked. A file whose copy is open is now not asked about; live, it was ticked at once (*is open already: ticked*), with no question.
- The question's text fits its box at 100 % (*such subtables position nothing, and each is made…*: it said *such a subtable positions nothing* of NotoSansJP's two, counting files instead of subtables; fixed).
- It fits its box at 150 % display scaling too (2026-11-21, a Windows 10 test machine at 1920 × 1080, FontLab in its trial mode): the question as Qt drew it and as the screen showed it, both lines of text whole, the two buttons unclipped.

## Limits

- Glyphs are matched by name only (a family's members should share them); Font Compare pairs two fonts' glyphs by code point too.
- Compatibility compares structure, not shapes: Font Compare and Font Check's *Masters* tab look at the outlines themselves.
- The new font's feature code and font info are the reference's alone: the other sources' code that differs is not merged, and the log says so.
- No italic or optical axis is set, nor instances; a family whose sources share a weight and width (a Regular and an Italic) is said, and left on one location.
- The line metrics of a master of a font of several masters other than its current one are not read.
- The crash check looks for one known FontLab 7.2 crash only ([above](#fonts-fontlab-72-crashes-on)), not for every font FontLab cannot open; a WOFF2 is not checked inside FontLab, and never copied.
