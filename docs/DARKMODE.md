# Dark mode

FontLab 7 has no theme to switch: it colors its interface in several independent ways, and *darkmode/freelab_dark_mode.py* handles each. Everything below was found live in FontLab 7.2.0 (Qt 5.14.2) on 2026-11-06, through the listener of FreeLab MCP (the separate `freelab-mcp` repository).

## How FontLab 7 colors itself

- **The application palette and style.** Qt's default Windows style and palette. Dark mode switches to the Fusion style (compiled into *Qt5Widgets.dll*) with a dark palette. FontLab's application stylesheet is empty.
- **Widget stylesheets.** About a thousand of FontLab's 4,700 widgets carry their own stylesheet (490 different ones) with fixed light colors, such as `background:#edeeef` or `color:black`. A widget's own stylesheet beats the palette, so these stay light under a dark palette, and the palettes Qt derives from them are light too.
- **Fixed colors in FontLab's code.** Some widgets paint light colors no setting reaches: the Sketchboard and glyph-window canvases (black outlines and fills on the workspace color), the font window's cells and the *Font Map* (black glyphs on the font-cell color), the rulers, the tool bar, tab strips, search-field frames, the *Preview* panel, list headers, the *History* list's selected row, the feature editor's margin, panel title strips, the background of every panel (`YPanel`, #edeeef, normally hidden under its content), and the 1-px frames the dock draws between panels (`YDock`, #cacbcc).
- **Editors.** The *Source* and *Scripting* editors (QScintilla) paint black text and a white margin on a paper that is already dark.
- **Item delegates.** About 40 classes paint list rows themselves, with colors set in code (`ProfilesDelegate`, `UnicodeDelegate`, `CodepagesDelegate`, `ActionsListDelegate`, and others), and item views carry fixed colors in their items' data.
- **Canvas preferences.** The workspace, font-cell, metrics, and mask colors are FontLab preferences (`fontlab.flPreferences()`, stored under *HKCU\Software\Fontlab\fontlab7*). They set backgrounds, but outlines stay black.
- **The window frame.** The title bar is drawn by Windows, in a color asked of Windows 11 (`DWMWA_CAPTION_COLOR`; earlier Windows keeps its own dark one): the shade's window gray at 85%, so that the bar stands apart from its window by the same step in both shades.

## What dark mode does

1. **Palette and style:** Fusion and a dark palette. Nothing dark mode draws reaches L = 0.9 (HLS lightness). Fonts are left as they are: asking Qt for grayscale text smoothing made GDI drop smoothing altogether at FontLab's small UI sizes.
2. **The color mapping**, used everywhere below, works in OKLab, where lightness follows what the eye sees and hues stay put: lightness is inverted between #1f1f1f (for white) and #dbdbdb (for black), hue is kept, and chroma is kept up to L 0.65 and muted above it, down to a quarter at white. Low-color pixels (channels spreading by 110 or less out of 255), where all text smoothing lives, are mapped linearly instead: their mean is inverted between the two grays, so a blend of text and background stays a blend (the perceptual mapping moves edge pixels to the wrong brightness, and text looks ragged). In the interface they come out gray, which also removes ClearType's colored edges; content (canvases, font cells, brush previews) keeps their tint, since anchors, nodes, and marks carry meaning even when muted, except on sharp edges (text and outlines, where ClearType's fringes are), which turn gray there too. So tinted whites and pastels turn gray (keeping their saturation would turn FontLab's #fffffa metrics background khaki, and bluish whites navy); dark-blue text becomes a readable blue, and red and green marks keep their color.

   Effects apply it by **anchor blends** (*freelab_pixels.c*). A widget paints a few flat colors (backgrounds, text, frames) and smooths their edges with blends of two of them. The helper finds those colors first (the most frequent ones, with how long their runs are across and down: surfaces, lines, or text), maps each one or keeps it, and redraws every other pixel that is a blend of two of them as the same blend of their mapped colors. Text keeps its smoothing, and ClearType's colored fringes turn neutral, where mapping each pixel on its own leaves ragged or haloed text. A frequent color that is only smoothing (between a background and darker text) does not count as a color of its own. Where several pairs could explain a pixel (gray text, gray rows, and gray badges all lie on one line), the colors found within two pixels of it decide. In content, the part of a pixel's color beyond its blend is kept, so node fills and handles keep their tint. An image with no color frequent enough to be one of them (a gray ramp, a single pixel) is mapped pixel by pixel where everything is mapped (islands, icons, ClearType previews); ink then maps only its stray whites.
3. **Stylesheets:** every literal color in a widget's own stylesheet is mapped, according to what it is for: backgrounds and borders only when light, text only when dark. A bar that is dark in light mode stays dark, with its light text. Color swatches are skipped.
4. **Islands**, for widgets that paint fixed light colors with dark text or lines on them: the widget gets FontLab's original light palette, and its own stylesheet is prefixed with its ancestors' original stylesheets (a widget's own stylesheet beats inherited rules whatever their specificity), so everything inside it looks exactly as in light mode. A graphics effect then maps the lightness of every pixel it draws. On the canvas, black outlines turn light, colored nodes and marks keep their hue, and the workspace color preference is set to #f0f0f0 for the duration, which maps to a dark gray. In content, light text on a color it keeps, such as a selected font cell's name on its blue header, stays as drawn: a light pixel with that same saturated color within ten pixels on every side, past the text and its smoothing. Mapped with the white paper, the name would turn dark on the blue. The color must be one the content keeps, and three pixels thick on one side at least: a node's white fill inside its 1-px outline is still mapped. Finding such text adds about 4 ms to a full 1823×1265 canvas with no such color, and about 14 ms to a full font window. The palette an island is given does not outlast its stylesheet, so an island whose delegate takes its text's color from the palette (*Add Glyphs*' list of categories) has that color set in its stylesheet too; mapped as ink, its text came out with rough edges, and its selected row, painted in a fixed light color, could not be read. During a drag that tab docking follows, a large widget is painted once and that picture drawn at each repaint: FontLab's blue drop highlight, as it comes up over the document area, makes every widget under it repaint time after time, Qt keeps no picture of a widget for its effect, and drawing and mapping a font window anew each time (111 ms for 1,715 by 1,329 pixels, measured live; 1 ms held) slowed the drag. The highlight itself is never rendered to see what it paints: it grows step by step, and looked at anew at each size (82 ms, some 26 times) it stalled every such drag for 2.1 seconds. What changes inside such a widget during the drag shows when the drag is over. The same holds while a menu or a dropdown list is open (the popup's own widgets aside): FontLab repaints its windows as the mouse moves over a menu's entries.
5. **Ink**, for widgets on the dark interface that FontLab paints in fixed colors: list rows (the *Export Profiles* names in navy #3a3f7f, *Unicode ranges* and *Codepages* in black, the *Commands & Shortcuts* rows and badges, the glyph images in *Classes*, the *Font Info* sidebar icons), text browsers (*Software Update*), previews, and light badges. Every list, tree, table, and text view's viewport gets it, and so does any other widget whose own rendering shows such colors. Only what is wrong on a dark interface changes: white surfaces and lines, light low-color surfaces (badges, light rows), and dark ink (text-like shapes clearly darker than the background, or dark and colored), which becomes light ink of the same hue. Light text, dark surfaces, and colored surfaces stay, and so does smoothing: ClearType's around light text (a dark, colored pixel right beside much lighter ones) and the dark edge of colored text (a dark pixel of the same hue as lighter text). A solid area counts as ink only when it is near black (large glyph strokes), never a dark panel or a selection; dim gray marks (icons drawn in dark gray, near black against white in light mode) are mapped and lightened a little further; ClearType's fringes of white text on a light badge count as smoothing. A list that paints no background of its own (the *Unicode ranges* and *Codepages* rows: black text over the dark panel beneath) has only its text opaque, so the text's share is measured against the whole area, not against the opaque pixels, where it passed for a background. On an editor's dark paper, smoothing lies between the paper and the text even where the pixels around are all fringes, so the paper always counts as nearby: otherwise ClearType's navy and gray-teal fringes of blue syntax match a far pair of blue and black ink and turn into light dots. Text inside a light surface that turns dark (gray text on a selected row, a white thumbnail's glyph) turns with it, as an island would: the helper records which colors touch which, so text that touches only the light surface is told from that surface's smoothed edge, which touches the background too; pixels lighter than the surface (white digits on a gray badge, and their fringes) stay. A pair of colors far from a pixel explains it only if it lies within 30 levels of the line between them and is no more colored than its ends: otherwise a red digit fits the long black-to-white line and turns white. A color is a blend of two others only if it touches one of them, and not if it is away from the surface and more frequent than the other end (a check box's blue lies between white and its own darker edge). The most frequent surface is the background only if it covers a tenth of the image (an unticked box in a list painted on nothing is not). A white mark with only colored pixels beside it (a tick on its blue box) stays white; the edge of a small white square drawn on nothing (an unticked box a list's delegate paints) gets the check-box frame color. On an editor's paper, ClearType's fringes of colored syntax become the plain blend of the paper and the text; gray text keeps its fringes, which blending thinned. Ink and editor effects weigh the whole widget even when only a part repaints, and draw only that part: a clicked page in *Font Info*'s list or an edited line in *Source*, mapped alone, would come out differently from the rest (dim icons lifted on some pages only). The item icons of FontLab's lists (*Font Info*'s and *Preferences*' pages) are mapped as button icons are. On content (the glyph canvas), clearly colored marks of middle lightness or lighter keep their color (yellow and orange outlines, red nodes, blue labels): inverted, a yellow outline turns dark olive. Radio buttons, check boxes, and push buttons are drawn by the style from the dark palette and never get an ink effect (mapped, their circles turn jagged). A dark colored text is smoothing of a lighter one only if it touches it (otherwise *Export Profiles*' navy names pass for smoothing of its blue boxes, and change color as the mouse moves). Decisions that read a pixel's neighbors (white marks, box edges, ClearType fringes, sharp steps) are made on the pixels as drawn, before any is mapped: the helper maps in place, and read mapped, a box's inside would pass for ink and a comb's fill for edges. A white mark must be small and enclosed by one control color of middle lightness or lighter, and darker than the mark (black text's fringes on a white header are none; a light pink area, the font window's selected *Flag* swatch, is no mark on a control of its own color, and turns dark as any light surface); a box gets a frame only when empty (a glyph thumbnail is not). Dark colored fringes beside a light surface that turns dark are black text's smoothing and map as ink. In ink mode, a kept ClearType fringe of gray text (opposite hues along the row, gray text beside it) turns the gray of its own lightness; on content, such fringes lose their tint, and a thin colored line's smoothing is resolved as a blend of its two colors (mapped alone, it comes out lighter than the line, which looks dotted). A list's rows get a variant of ink: drawn on nothing for a light ground, as the font window's sidebar is, FontLab's dark gray glyph icons (*Number*'s 2, *Mark*'s tilde) are solid enough to pass for dark surfaces, and its gray sub-items (*None*, *uppercase*, *Regular*, drawn translucent) fall just short of dim ink, so plain ink leaves both dark on the dark panel, hollow-looking. There, each connected shape of a dark gray color decides by itself: light letters inside its box make it a badge (the script badges, *Properties*' A), drawn as it is with its letters; a filled block (a selected row's branch cell) stays as its color decides; any other shape (a glyph icon, a word of gray text with its smoothing, the digits on a *Flag* swatch, followed through their blend with the swatch's tint) is mapped as ink, and gray sub-items are lifted as dim ink. Light ink drawn on nothing looks heavier than the dark ink it was (the counters of small bold text filled in), so its smoothing there is thinned, at the edges only: pixels the mapping changed, beside a more opaque one; text drawn translucent throughout (the sub-items) keeps its weight, and light text kept as drawn its smoothing. A row FontLab paints dark itself, with light text (the selected row of *Export Profiles*, #313131 from the dark palette), is a panel of such a dark gray, and the counters of its letters, closed off by the text, are small shapes of that color: mapped as glyph icons, they would fill the letters with light gray, and the text would look struck twice. A shape inside the box of a panel of its own color, with only opaque pixels around it, is a hole in what is drawn on the panel and stays the panel's. A list FontLab paints for a dark interface already, but for one row it paints light whatever the palette (*Preview Features*: light gray text #dbdbdb and dark gray rings #4f4f4f on its own dark base, and the row under the pointer near-white, #e9f0ff, with that same light gray text), can have the colors of its dark rows fit lines to that row's colors: the light text passes for smoothing between the row and the text's own yellowish fringe, and the rings for smoothing between the dark base and a pale fringe of the row's text, or for the row's smoothed edge. Dropped as blends, both would be redrawn as blends with the row's colors as they turn dark: dark on dark, the text down to its colored fringes. A color is no such blend when it lies elsewhere: most of it away from the light surface, while the other end never touches that surface; or touching only the kept background, with nothing that touches it leading to the light surface or to the other end. The text then stays as painted, and the rings are dim ink, as they are when no row is light. Where the light row is mapped, it turns dark as any light surface, and text in the dark interface's own text color stays as drawn on it: what lies between the row and that text is redrawn as the same blend of the dark row and the text (turned as an island, it would leave the text's few full pixels alone, the letters in pieces), unless a light surface of the picture holds dark text, whose palest fringes lie on that same line; the smoothed edge of a colored mark kept on such a row (a checked mark's blue disk) becomes the blend of the dark row and the mark, as its rarer shades do. Light text on a gray badge whose white is the list's own white (*Anchors & Pins*' coordinates: white ClearType digits on a mid-gray pill in a white list) would be mapped with that white (dark digits on the badge, their light fringes kept around them), so it is found first. A light pixel enclosed by one gray, up and down within 6 pixels and across within 24 (a line of digits' bars touch through their fringes), past nothing but light pixels, and thin one way at least (a stroke; the white inside a gray-framed box is as wide as it is tall), is text on that badge: its coverage from the badge toward white (the mean of its channels': grayscale smoothing, without the ClearType fringes drawn for the light badge) is drawn again from the badge as it comes out (from the nearest badge pixel as the mapping drew it, so the text's smoothing meets it exactly), so the text stays light whether the badge is kept or turns dark. A badge with such text that comes out too light for it (white on it below 4.5:1: the *Anchors & Pins* pill kept at #a0a2a4 read at about 2.6:1) is drawn darker in its own hue, at the luminance of the *Classes* panel's badge gray, #646464 (about 5.9:1). The gray is a frequent color of low color, of middle lightness (HLS 0.3 to 0.85), and solid one way at least, a blend dropped as no anchor included (a gray badge lies on the line from the list's white to its black text); a gray letter's strokes are too short to be one. FontLab's *Output* panel writes the compiler's errors in a dark red (about #8b0000) on its dark paper: the red is dark ink, made lighter in its own hue, and its ClearType fringes, each channel at a coverage of its own (#340c00: little red, more of the green and blue gone), mapped alone as dark ink, would come out light pink (#eac2b6), light dots inside every letter. A fringe whose every channel lies between the paper's and that text's, touching it, is drawn again channel by channel from the paper and the text as they come out, as ClearType draws light text on dark. The bright red of Python's tracebacks is another case: its fringes are smoothing of lighter text. Other widgets keep the plain ink mapping, and the other lists, which paint a background, come out the same under the rows' variant as under plain ink.

   Effects nest: each one draws the widgets inside it that have an effect of their own as they come out of it (Qt hands an effect its children already drawn through their own effects: mapped a second time by the font window's ink effect, its glyph cells would turn mid-gray). Qt's `isAncestorOf` is true for a widget itself, which an effect must not take as one of its own insides.
6. **Editors** (QScintilla): FontLab gives each editor light-theme style colors (black and navy text, sometimes white paper). Mapping their pixels leaves ClearType's colored fringes on every edge, changing with each partial repaint (a selection, a click), so dark mode sets each style's colors itself through QScintilla's `SendScintilla`, reached with `ctypes` in FontLab's *qscintilla2_qt5.dll* at the editor's address (which Qt's own description of the widget gives): dark grays made light (light grays kept), light paper made dark (dark paper kept), and colored text lightened to OKLab L 0.76 with its hue kept, on every style from 0 to 255 except the margin's. The editor then draws light text on dark paper with its own smoothing, as any dark editor does. The colors it had are kept and written back on switching off, except those FontLab changed meanwhile, and an editor FontLab made while dark mode was on (dark paper from the dark palette) gets light paper then, and dark text where its text was light gray. Each repaint compares the lexer and predefined styles (0 to 39), the caret, and the caret line with what dark mode set; when FontLab has set its own again, that one paint goes through the pixel mapping and the editor is colored anew right after it (higher styles are caught by the sweep, within 30 seconds). Only the margin is still mapped: the white margin at the left is mapped whole, its numbers to a gray like the *Features* panel's; the margin is found column by column (one row alone stops at the first digit it meets and garbles the numbers). Without that entry point (another QScintilla build), editors keep the pixel mapping: light paper mapped whole, and on dark paper the ink mapping, so dark syntax colors turn light in their own hue. Color swatches, color pickers (the white "no color" slot included), color bars, and the font window's mark slots (`QToolButton#flagRed` and the like) keep their colors, even under an effect that maps the panel around them: every effect draws them over its mapping as they are. Color pickers get a thin dark frame instead of the style's white one, and their dark-gray arrow is mapped light. The popup that holds the swatches is darkened.
7. **Keyed frames:** the dock's own light colors are collected from one render of it alone, and an effect maps only pixels of exactly those colors, plus near-white ones; the frame's antialiased ends on its first and last rows, blended with what lies beneath and so matching no key, take the mapped frame color. A lower lightness threshold would also catch dark mode's own light text.
8. **Fills:** every tab widget fills itself with the dark palette color, covering the panel's #edeeef background before any content draws over it, under translucent labels too.
9. **Panel titles:** an effect maps the strip between a panel's top and its first child.
10. **Bars dark already:** the font window's bottom bar (glyph name and code, metrics, selection count, columns) is dark through its stylesheet, so the font window's ink effect must not map it again: that garbles its labels. Drawn opaque, its light text still gets ClearType's colored fringes. It gets a *defringe* effect instead: drawn as it is, with each fringe of gray text turned to the mean of its channels, which is the grayscale smoothing it would have had. Real colors stay: anything darker than the bar's ground, and runs of three or more pixels led by one channel (the blue selection count).
11. **ClearType text:** *TrueType Hinting*'s preview draws black text with ClearType on purpose, each channel being the paper times one minus that subpixel's coverage. Inverting lightness gives colored edges; the helper's subpixel mapping keeps each channel's coverage and draws it from the mapped paper toward light text, which is ClearType's own light-on-dark rendering (the gutter's colored numbers take the plain mapping). Light text that ClearType draws as blue and yellow fringes with no light core (fields, the font window's *Name: Encoding: None Index* selectors) is drawn in grays before mapping, unless a field has selected text; panel titles likewise, whose hovered text is black with saturated fringes. The simple sliders are left as drawn: their white knob is right on dark already. An icon counts as a color swatch only when it is mostly filled: kept as swatches, the thin red Y and X of the hinting direction buttons would stay black-stemmed.
12. **Icons and label images** are mapped the same way, except that their colored parts of middle lightness (a blue guide, a green fill) stay as they are (inverting their lightness turns light blue icons dark blue): icons once per icon (cached), label images once each, and nothing twice. Color swatches (icons mostly made of clearly colored pixels, such as mark colors) and pictures larger than 64 px (such as the *About* artwork) are left as they are. White artwork on transparency in a label or a stylesheet image (FontLab's logo in the Welcome dialog) stays white and gets no effect: it is white on purpose, and mapped as paper it turns black. List icons that are logos (colorful, or 24 px or more, such as Export Profiles' formats) are drawn as FontLab draws them, over the list's ink mapping, which would invert their dark parts with rough edges; the rows' text is still mapped. A button whose icon is white artwork (a checked View tick) keeps it white and is drawn as it is. A button inside an island that follows a menu action shows the action's original icon, since the island maps it.
13. **Title bars:** `DwmSetWindowAttribute` with `DWMWA_USE_IMMERSIVE_DARK_MODE`, and with `DWMWA_CAPTION_COLOR` for the bar's color on Windows 11 (the shade's window gray at 85%), once per window: each call makes Windows recompute the frame, and repeating it on a dialog still opening keeps it relaying itself out until FontLab stops responding.
14. **Stylesheet images:** FontLab's stylesheets name images in its resources (`url(:/...)`: the glyph window's info-bar icons, combo-box and spin-box arrows, tree arrows). Each is mapped once per shade into *%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\cache*, with its @2x version, and the darkened stylesheet points there.
15. **Check boxes and radio buttons:** Fusion frames them a little darker than the window color, which vanishes on a dark palette, and some of FontLab's lists and property grids draw them with no widget, out of any stylesheet's reach. The style is a `QProxyStyle` around Fusion that draws a visible frame over them on dark palettes, except where a stylesheet draws them as images (FontLab's blue boxes, whose rounded corners the frame would show through). PythonQt cannot read a style option's state, so the stylesheet is what tells them apart. FontLab's own application style is plain `QWindowsVistaStyle` (recorded at launch), so switching to Fusion drops none of its tweaks. A menu's tick (the switches of *Scripts > FreeLab*, FontLab's own *View* entries) is drawn as FontLab's light look has it, the mark alone in the text's color and nothing for an entry not ticked, in place of Fusion's filled box, whose outline, a pixel wider and taller than dark mode's rounded frame, shows as dark pixels around it on the highlighted row. PythonQt cannot read the option's state, so the entry under the box (`QMenu.actionAt`) says whether it is ticked; where it cannot be read, Fusion's box is drawn and framed square, right over its outline. A dark menu also gets the line beside its ticks and icons that the light look has and Fusion lacks, drawn over the menu's panel 23 pixels in at 100% scaling (more on a menu of higher DPI), and its separators start at that line. The line is 2 pixels wide, and dark mode draws the menu's 1-pixel frame itself in the same color (the window color at 145%, nearer the menu's own than the separators'), as the light look's do (the owner, November 23, images 23 and 24); Fusion's own frame is near black.
16. **What's This help:** its window gets a palette of its own, the dark one with its shadow color set to the frame's: Qt dots the shadow color over the frame's right and bottom sides, which shows as a dashed edge on a dark popup.
17. **Disabled menu text:** Fusion etches it with the palette's light color, which leaves a light shadow on a dark menu, so that disabled color is transparent.
18. **Menu separators:** Fusion draws them in a shade barely apart from a dark menu, across the ticks' column. A menu with no stylesheet of its own gets lighter ones as it opens (the window color at 190%), taken off again with dark mode.
19. **Restyles:** FontLab restyles some widgets while it works (fields while a glyph is edited, a button that turns green). Each sweep compares visible widgets' stylesheets with what dark mode set or noted, a first stylesheet included; a widget caught once is watched from then on (an event filter on `QEvent::StyleChange`) and darkened before it paints.

Known island classes are listed in the script; a glyph window's canvas is its `QWidget#widget` only, since its other plain children, such as the text bar, are interface. Any other visible widget is rendered once without its children: if a quarter of it is near-white, it becomes an island when it paints mostly itself (its visible children cover less than half of it, as a canvas with rulers does) with dark marks on it, else an ink area. A self-painted widget with a smaller light part (3% or more, such as a white margin) is an ink area too, and so is one whose own painting the ink mapping would change (dark text or marks, judged after its icons are mapped). A widget inside an ink area gets an effect of its own too, which costs an extra offscreen render but never a second mapping: the outer effect draws it as it comes out of its own. Known classes match their subclasses too (FontLab's *UnicodeCellChart* is a *CellChart*). A widget's unprocessed ancestors are always processed first, since a child of a still-light parent looks light itself, and a widget is looked at again whenever its size changes, since a panel may first be seen at a placeholder size while it opens.

## Options

*Scripts > FreeLab > Dark Mode Settings* chooses the theme, dark or automatic (dark while Windows apps are set to dark, `AppsUseLightTheme` = 0, checked every 3 seconds, so it switches as Windows does; FontLab's own light look otherwise), mixed (a dark interface with FontLab's light glyph canvases, font cells, and previews, which the ink of the windows around them leaves as drawn), and the shade (dark grays, where white becomes #1f1f1f, or medium grays, where it becomes #3c3c3c, with a lighter palette). Each change shows at once; Cancel puts back what was there. There is no light choice: switching off is *Dark Mode*. Where editors keep the pixel mapping (no QScintilla entry point), an editor whose paper came from a light palette (made, or remade, while dark mode was off) is mapped in one piece, margin included (on white paper every column looks like the margin, and its text would be mapped as line numbers): each pixel's ClearType coverage toward the syntax color around it, averaged over the channels, is redrawn from the dark paper toward that color's dark-mode color (blue keys and red numbers kept, black turned light). That is gray smoothing, as the *Features* panel's editor draws; inverting lightness fringes every letter. Under Automatic while Windows is light, the dialog grays out mixed and the shade, which do nothing until Windows turns dark, and looks again every two seconds. Switching by hand (*Dark Mode*) stops following Windows. The settings live in *darkmode.json*: `enabled`, `content` (the older `canvas` still counts), `shade`, and `follow_windows`.

## Platforms and builds

Written and tested on FontLab 7.2.0 for Windows (Qt 5.14.2, PythonQt, Python 2.7). Other FontLab 7 builds share the same interface code and should work, since unknown light widgets are found by rendering them, but only 7.2.0 has been run. FontLab 8 has a dark theme of its own.

FontLab 7 for macOS uses the same Qt, PythonQt, and Python 2.7 (installed separately on macOS 12.3 and newer), so the Qt side of dark mode should carry over. These parts are Windows-only and would need a macOS counterpart: the native helper (the C compiles as a *.dylib*; it loads as a *.dll* now, so dark mode on a Mac would fall back as it does without the helper), the dark title bars (DWM: skipped on other systems), following the system theme (the registry: on other systems, *Automatic* keeps FontLab's light look), and the installer (PowerShell, Windows paths). The macOS menu bar is native and outside Qt's styling. None of it has run on a Mac.

## The native helper

Mapping pixels in Python 2.7 would take seconds per frame. *darkmode/native/freelab_pixels.c* does it in C: the anchor blends (all, ink, relight, icon, subpixel, and syntax), the pixel-by-pixel mapping for icons, the defringing of gray text on bars dark already (`freelab_defringe`), the keyed mapping, the pixel counts for detection, the color collection for keyed frames, and a diagnostic list of the last call's colors (`freelab_last_anchors`). It uses no C runtime and imports nothing, and FontLab's Python calls it through `ctypes` on `QImage.bits()`. *tools/build-native.ps1* builds it with clang or MSVC into *%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\bin\freelab_pixels-v<version>.dll*. The name carries the version because FontLab keeps a loaded DLL locked. Without it, islands fall back to plain color inversion, which flips hues; the ink and keyed effects do nothing, and defringed bars keep ClearType's colored fringes.

## What an older install leaves behind

Dark mode has one engine, the one described above. An older install may hold the settings and library of the native engine it once offered ([HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md#dark-mode)), and they are handled: *darkmode.json*'s `engine` and `native_pending` are read as nothing and dropped at the next save, so `"engine": "native"` runs the script engine with no error; dark mode never loads a *freelab_hooks* library; the installer deletes any left in *%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\bin*, and one a running FontLab still holds stays until the next install, without failing it. If an older dark mode put its hooks in during the FontLab session in which this version is first run (from the *Scripts* menu, before a restart), this version takes them out through the library already loaded, and rebuilds dark mode with the script engine.

## Cost

- Switching on blocks FontLab for about 1.5 to 3 seconds: the style change alone repolishes every widget. The widgets are then processed in 20 ms slices, about 2.5 seconds in all.
- Menus: every menu's `aboutToShow` is connected once, so a menu is darkened as it opens; right after switching on, thousands of widgets wait in the queue, and a menu opened then would flash light.
- New windows: when keyboard focus moves (Qt's `focusChanged`, which fires as a window or tab opens), dark mode processes the visible new widgets at once, for up to 80 ms, so they barely show light. A sweep every 0.5 seconds catches the rest, and every 30 seconds it revisits every widget, catching stylesheets FontLab replaced. The sweep holds every widget's Python wrapper, so PythonQt makes and frees none from one sweep to the next, and tells a new widget by the wrapper it has not held yet rather than by reading a Qt property. Held wrappers are let go only where that frees nothing: those of deleted widgets (on the full sweep), and on switching off those of widgets with a parent; a live widget with no parent keeps its wrapper held for the session, since one made from Python and let go by its maker (another script's dialog) would be deleted by PythonQt the moment its last reference went, many at once, which crashes FontLab. Measured live on November 15 with about 5,000 widgets and nothing to queue: a sweep takes 9 to 10 ms every 0.5 s, with no wrapper made or freed. FontLab panels opened for the first time were looked at after 203, 443, and 664 ms, and a FreeLab window (*Font Compare*, which takes the focus) after 1.26 to 1.39 s; no shown widget was left unlooked at. An application-wide event filter, which would catch new widgets as Qt polishes them, is not used: it calls Python, with a wrapper for the object and the event, for every event of every object in FontLab (each paint and mouse move), far more than a sweep.
- Any widget with an effect is drawn offscreen first. A full canvas render took 40 ms without dark mode and 55 to 100 ms with it (the mapping itself 13 to 18 ms; a full font window, 17 ms). Where everything is mapped, only colored pixels go through the pair search.
- The check-box style runs a little Python for every primitive Qt draws. If the canvas feels slow, tick *Mixed* in *Scripts > FreeLab > Dark Mode Settings* (`"content": false` in *%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\darkmode.json*): the canvas then keeps FontLab's own colors, and only its rulers are mapped.

## Switching off

Everything is recorded before it changes, and switching off puts it back without a restart: style, palette, stylesheets (unless FontLab replaced one meanwhile), palettes, icons, effects, fills, title bars, and the workspace color (unless it was changed meanwhile). The saved workspace color is kept in *darkmode.json* until then. Graphics effects FontLab sets itself are never replaced. An editor FontLab created while dark mode was on took its dark paper and light text from the palette, once. Its colors are set natively on switching off (see Editors above): white paper, and dark text where its text was light gray. Only where QScintilla's entry point cannot be reached does it get a "relight" effect instead, which maps that paper white and that text black, with some rough smoothing; restarting FontLab then gives the exact light look. *tools/install-fontlab.ps1 -Uninstall* also puts the workspace color back, and asks for dark mode to be switched off first when FontLab is running.

## FreeLab's own windows follow each switch

A widget under a stylesheet (on it or on any parent) is given the palette of the moment it is polished, and setting the application style polishes every widget. Dark mode sets the style before the palette, so such widgets are polished with the palette of before and keep it, one switch behind, both ways (in Qt 5.15 offscreen, setting the palette first, or polishing again after the switch, fixes it), and dark mode's mapping then takes their light text on dark ground for ink and garbles it. FreeLab's windows also draw pictures in the text color of the moment (glyphs in lists, previews, the tools button), take item colors from the palette (grayed-out rows, amber warnings), color log lines as they come, and draw a few things in several colors.

Dark mode tells FreeLab once it has switched, either way (`sys._freelab_theme_hook`, set by the tools' shared module; called after the palette and the style are both set, before any widget is looked at when switching on, and once every widget is undone when switching off). Each shown FreeLab window then follows at once (`theme_switched` in *kerning/freelab_auto_kerning.py*): its widgets are polished again, parents first, each given an empty palette (inherit again) where none is set on it: a widget styled through a stylesheet, on it, on a parent, or on the application, keeps the theme before even when polished again (seen live in Feature Code's sections and Font Check's scripts list), and one whose stylesheet sets colors is read again from them (dark mode's islands keep theirs); pictures drawn in one palette color (noted with `inked` as they are drawn) are drawn again in that role's new color, their transparency kept; items' text and background colors that are a palette color take the new one (and the logs' colors for a light background swap with those for a dark one); logs are written again; every list in FreeLab's table layout (`table_layout`: grid lines, check boxes, and selected rows in its palette's colors) has its stylesheet written again in the new colors; and each tool's `theme_changed` draws what it draws in several colors (*Auto Kerning*'s table and choices, *Glyph Compose*'s strip, editors, and references, *Font Check*'s diagram, comparison, and badges, *Font Compare*'s pictures and differences). The windows' own tick (every 0.7 s) notices a palette change no one announced, from an older dark mode. A dropdown's open list is a window of its own (Qt's popup holding the list), outside the walk over a window's widgets, and lies under its box's stylesheet: it is walked with its window (`popup_windows`). The fields and spin boxes a light window draws a shade darker while grayed (`light_fields`) get all three states of that background from the application's palette of now: a palette holds a role for every state at once, so setting one background on a copy of the field's own palette would keep the usable background of the theme before. On a dark window the field takes its window's palette whole again. FontLab's own widgets keep dark mode's order (style, then palette): reordering it would change how every FontLab widget under a stylesheet looks in dark mode, which needs a look in FontLab first.

## Known limits

- **A picture in a glyph is shown with its grays inverted.** The Glyph window's canvas is mapped as a whole: grays turn over, colors stay. A picture pasted or placed in a glyph (a scan to trace, a screenshot) is pixels like the rest, so its white comes out dark and its black light, its colors unchanged; the picture itself is untouched, and what *Autotrace* reads is the picture as it is. To see pictures as they are, tick *Mixed* in *Scripts > FreeLab > Dark Mode Settings*, which leaves the canvases light.
- **Switching can stall on a locked clipboard.** Switching on or off changes FontLab's workspace color preference (when *content* is on). FontLab answers any preference change by checking the clipboard several times, and while another program holds the clipboard open, Qt retries each check with pauses: on 2026-11-12, with the clipboard held by another program (`OpenClipboard` refused everywhere), every preference change took 8.6 s in FontLab with no FreeLab code loaded at all, and switching dark mode took about 10 s on and 11.5 s off, 8.6 s of it in that one step. The rest is about 1.5 s (style and palette) and, on switching on, the widget queue, worked through in slices over about 8 s while FontLab stays usable. Freeing the clipboard (closing or restarting the program that holds it) removes the stall.

## PythonQt traps met on the way

- Qt properties read as values: `widget.styleSheet`, `app.style().objectName`, and `widget.palette` are not callable.
- `QPalette` has no `PlaceholderText` name (the wrappers predate Qt 5.12), but the role works by number (20).
- `QWidget.winId()` returns nothing usable; window handles come from `EnumWindows` instead.
- `findChildren(QWidget)` returns an empty list.
- A Python subclass of a Qt class, such as a `QGraphicsEffect`, is deleted as soon as Python holds no reference to it, even after `setGraphicsEffect` gave it to a widget.
- `QGraphicsEffect.sourcePixmap` takes a `QPoint` to fill with the drawing offset, which is not zero for a widget inside another effect's widget.
- Under any widget stylesheet, `app.style()` is Qt's stylesheet wrapper, whose name is empty. Switching off therefore restores the `windowsvista` style by name.
- Paint events are not delivered while a script runs (a request to FreeLab MCP's listener included), so `widget.grab()` is the way to see the current look.
- Python identity does not tell Qt objects apart: `graphicsEffect()` may return another wrapper for the same effect. FreeLab tags its effects with a Qt property instead: taking its own effect for a foreign one would drop the last Python reference to an installed effect, and FontLab crashes in *PythonQt.dll* on the next repaint.
- A Python 2 module that is no longer referenced has its globals set to None, and FontLab may drop modules from `sys.modules`, as a reload does. Anything Qt still calls into (timers, event filters, effects) then fails with `'NoneType' object is not callable`. Dark mode keeps every version of itself it loaded referenced (FreeLab MCP's listener does the same for its own module).
- `QObject` cannot be subclassed from Python here; an event filter subclasses `QTimer` instead.
- PythonQt deletes a Qt object made from Python with no parent when its last Python reference goes, which may be at whatever moment Python's collector picks, and PythonQt can then free a wrapper twice and crash FontLab. FreeLab hands a closed tool window to Qt (FontLab's main window as its parent, its parentless timers given the window, then `deleteLater` from a plain timer), and tab docking finds the document areas from FontLab's windows instead of walking every widget. The same holds for a timer whose own timeout lets go of it: dark mode's timers have FontLab's application as their parent and are kept and started again, and the settings dialog has FontLab's main window as its parent, its watch timer stopped and the dialog handed to Qt when it closes. The tests' fake timer (*tests/qtfakes.py*) is held only weakly, and `fire_unheld` fires one as Qt does, holding no reference: one deleted inside its own timeout fails the test.
- `flWorkspace.changed()` is not a getter: calling it marks the workspace as changed.
- `ctypes.windll` releases Python's lock during each call. `SetWindowPos` (for the title bars) sends messages to FontLab's own windows synchronously, so Qt runs event filters and PythonQt overrides inside the call without the lock, and FontLab crashes in `PyErr_SetObject` (seen in crash dumps, on opening *Scripts > Update / Install Scripts*). Calls that can re-enter Qt go through `ctypes.PyDLL`, which keeps the lock.
- Switching off from the *Scripts* menu runs a fresh copy of the script, and the relight effects it makes call into that copy, so it is kept referenced too.
- A check box drawn with no widget gets the application palette in its style option, and no stylesheet rule reaches it.
- `QLineEdit.hasSelectedText`, `QWidget.isMinimized` and the like are properties too: calling them raises, and an effect that raises draws its widget unmapped.
- `QWidget.mapTo(parent, ...)` crashes FontLab when `parent` is not an ancestor: it walks the parent chain to its null end. Undocking a panel moves its widgets out of the effects above them, so an effect checks `isAncestorOf` before mapping a widget it protects, and forgets the widgets that left.
