# Dark mode

FontLab 7 has no theme to switch: it colors its interface in several independent ways, and *darkmode/freelab_dark_mode.py* handles each. Everything below was found live in FontLab 7.2.0 (Qt 5.14.2) on 2026-11-06, through the FreeLab listener.

## How FontLab 7 colors itself

- **The application palette and style.** Qt's default Windows style and palette. Dark mode switches to the Fusion style (compiled into *Qt5Widgets.dll*) with a dark palette. FontLab's application stylesheet is empty.
- **Widget stylesheets.** About a thousand of FontLab's 4,700 widgets carry their own stylesheet (490 different ones) with fixed light colors, such as `background:#edeeef` or `color:black`. A widget's own stylesheet beats the palette, so these stay light under a dark palette, and the palettes Qt derives from them are light too.
- **Fixed colors in FontLab's code.** Some widgets paint light colors no setting reaches: the Sketchboard and glyph-window canvases (black outlines and fills on the workspace color), the font window's cells and the *Font Map* (black glyphs on the font-cell color), the rulers, the tool bar, tab strips, search-field frames, the *Preview* panel, list headers, the *History* list's selected row, the feature editor's margin, panel title strips, the background of every panel (`YPanel`, #edeeef, normally hidden under its content), and the 1-px frames the dock draws between panels (`YDock`, #cacbcc).
- **Editors.** The *Source* and *Scripting* editors (QScintilla) paint black text and a white margin on a paper that is already dark.
- **Item delegates.** About 40 classes paint list rows themselves, with colors set in code (`ProfilesDelegate`, `UnicodeDelegate`, `CodepagesDelegate`, `ActionsListDelegate`, and others), and item views carry fixed colors in their items' data.
- **Canvas preferences.** The workspace, font-cell, metrics, and mask colors are FontLab preferences (`fontlab.flPreferences()`, stored under *HKCU\Software\Fontlab\fontlab7*). They set backgrounds, but outlines stay black.
- **The window frame.** The title bar is drawn by Windows.

## What dark mode does

1. **Palette and style:** Fusion and a dark palette. Nothing dark mode draws reaches L = 0.9 (HLS lightness). Fonts are left as they are: asking Qt for grayscale text smoothing made GDI drop smoothing altogether at FontLab's small UI sizes.
2. **The color mapping**, used everywhere below, works in OKLab, where lightness follows what the eye sees and hues stay put: lightness is inverted between #1f1f1f (for white) and #dbdbdb (for black), hue is kept, and chroma is kept up to L 0.65 and muted above it, down to a quarter at white. Low-color pixels (channels spreading by 110 or less out of 255), where all text smoothing lives, are mapped linearly instead: their mean is inverted between the two grays, so a blend of text and background stays a blend (the perceptual mapping moved edge pixels to the wrong brightness, and text looked ragged). In the interface they come out gray, which also removes ClearType's colored edges; content (canvases, font cells, brush previews) keeps their tint, since anchors, nodes, and marks carry meaning even when muted, except on sharp edges (text and outlines, where ClearType's fringes are), which turn gray there too. So tinted whites and pastels turn gray (FontLab's #fffffa metrics background had turned khaki, and bluish whites navy, when HLS saturation was kept); dark-blue text becomes a readable blue, and red and green marks keep their color.

   Effects apply it by **anchor blends** (*freelab_pixels.c*). A widget paints a few flat colors (backgrounds, text, frames) and smooths their edges with blends of two of them. The helper finds those colors first (the most frequent ones, with how long their runs are across and down: surfaces, lines, or text), maps each one or keeps it, and redraws every other pixel that is a blend of two of them as the same blend of their mapped colors. Text keeps its smoothing, and ClearType's colored fringes turn neutral, where mapping each pixel on its own had left ragged or haloed text. A frequent color that is only smoothing (between a background and darker text) does not count as a color of its own. Where several pairs could explain a pixel (gray text, gray rows, and gray badges all lie on one line), the colors found within two pixels of it decide. In content, the part of a pixel's color beyond its blend is kept, so node fills and handles keep their tint.
3. **Stylesheets:** every literal color in a widget's own stylesheet is mapped, according to what it is for: backgrounds and borders only when light, text only when dark. A bar that is dark in light mode stays dark, with its light text. Color swatches are skipped.
4. **Islands**, for widgets that paint fixed light colors with dark text or lines on them: the widget gets FontLab's original light palette, and its own stylesheet is prefixed with its ancestors' original stylesheets (a widget's own stylesheet beats inherited rules whatever their specificity), so everything inside it looks exactly as in light mode. A graphics effect then maps the lightness of every pixel it draws. On the canvas, black outlines turn light, colored nodes and marks keep their hue, and the workspace color preference is set to #f0f0f0 for the duration, which maps to a dark gray.
5. **Ink**, for widgets on the dark interface that FontLab paints in fixed colors: list rows (the *Export Profiles* names in navy #3a3f7f, *Unicode ranges* and *Codepages* in black, the *Commands & Shortcuts* rows and badges, the glyph images in *Classes*, the *Font Info* sidebar icons), text browsers (*Software Update*), previews, and light badges. Every list, tree, table, and text view's viewport gets it, and so does any other widget whose own rendering shows such colors. Only what is wrong on a dark interface changes: white surfaces and lines, light low-color surfaces (badges, light rows), and dark ink (text-like shapes clearly darker than the background, or dark and colored), which becomes light ink of the same hue. Light text, dark surfaces, and colored surfaces stay, and so does smoothing: ClearType's around light text (a dark, colored pixel right beside much lighter ones) and the dark edge of colored text (a dark pixel of the same hue as lighter text). Ink replaces 0.2's selective effect, which mapped only near-white pixels and left the smoothing around them dark. A solid area counts as ink only when it is near black (large glyph strokes), never a dark panel or a selection; dim gray marks (icons drawn in dark gray, near black against white in light mode) are mapped and lightened a little further; ClearType's fringes of white text on a light badge count as smoothing. A list that paints no background of its own (the *Unicode ranges* and *Codepages* rows: black text over the dark panel beneath) has only its text opaque, so the text's share is measured against the whole area, not against the opaque pixels, where it passed for a background. On an editor's dark paper, smoothing lies between the paper and the text even where the pixels around are all fringes, so the paper always counts as nearby: ClearType's navy and gray-teal fringes of blue syntax once matched a far pair of blue and black ink and turned into light dots. Text inside a light surface that turns dark (gray text on a selected row, a white thumbnail's glyph) turns with it, as an island would: the helper records which colors touch which, so text that touches only the light surface is told from that surface's smoothed edge, which touches the background too; pixels lighter than the surface (white digits on a gray badge, and their fringes) stay. A pair of colors far from a pixel explains it only if it lies within 30 levels of the line between them and is no more colored than its ends: a red digit had fitted the long black-to-white line and turned white. A color is a blend of two others only if it touches one of them, and not if it is away from the surface and more frequent than the other end (a check box's blue lies between white and its own darker edge). The most frequent surface is the background only if it covers a tenth of the image (an unticked box in a list painted on nothing is not). A white mark with only colored pixels beside it (a tick on its blue box) stays white; the edge of a small white square drawn on nothing (an unticked box a list's delegate paints) gets the check-box frame color. On an editor's paper, ClearType's fringes of colored syntax become the plain blend of the paper and the text; gray text keeps its fringes, which blending thinned. Ink and editor effects weigh the whole widget even when only a part repaints, and draw only that part: a clicked page in *Font Info*'s list or an edited line in *Source*, mapped alone, came out differently from the rest (dim icons lifted on some pages only). The item icons of FontLab's lists (*Font Info*'s and *Preferences*' pages) are mapped as button icons are. On content (the glyph canvas), clearly colored marks of middle lightness or lighter keep their color (yellow and orange outlines, red nodes, blue labels): inverted, a yellow outline turned dark olive. Radio buttons, check boxes, and push buttons are drawn by the style from the dark palette and never get an ink effect (mapped, their circles turned jagged). A dark colored text is smoothing of a lighter one only if it touches it (*Export Profiles*' navy names had passed for smoothing of its blue boxes, and changed color as the mouse moved). Decisions that read a pixel's neighbors (white marks, box edges, ClearType fringes, sharp steps) are made on the pixels as drawn, before any is mapped: the helper maps in place, and read mapped, a box's inside passed for ink and a comb's fill for edges. A white mark must be small and enclosed by one control color of middle lightness or lighter (black text's fringes on a white header are none); a box gets a frame only when empty (a glyph thumbnail is not). Dark colored fringes beside a light surface that turns dark are black text's smoothing and map as ink. In ink mode, a kept ClearType fringe of gray text (opposite hues along the row, gray text beside it) turns the gray of its own lightness; on content, such fringes lose their tint, and a thin colored line's smoothing is resolved as a blend of its two colors (mapped alone, it came out lighter than the line, which looked dotted).

   Effects nest: each one draws the widgets inside it that have an effect of their own as they come out of it (Qt hands an effect its children already drawn through their own effects, and the font window's ink effect had mapped its glyph cells a second time, turning them mid-gray). Qt's `isAncestorOf` is true for a widget itself, which an effect must not take as one of its own insides.
6. **Editors** (QScintilla viewports): an editor takes its paper color from the palette when it is created, so after switching dark mode off and on it may have light paper; each repaint checks, and light paper is mapped whole. On dark paper: the white margin at the left is mapped whole, its numbers to a gray like the *Features* panel's; the margin is found column by column (one row alone stopped at the first digit it met and garbled the numbers). The rest gets the ink mapping, so dark syntax colors turn light in their own hue. Color swatches, color pickers (the white "no color" slot included), color bars, and the font window's mark slots (`QToolButton#flagRed` and the like) keep their colors, even under an effect that maps the panel around them: every effect draws them over its mapping as they are. Color pickers get a thin dark frame instead of the style's white one, and their dark-gray arrow is mapped light. The popup that holds the swatches is darkened.
7. **Keyed frames:** the dock's own light colors are collected from one render of it alone, and an effect maps only pixels of exactly those colors, plus near-white ones; the frame's antialiased ends on its first and last rows, blended with what lies beneath and so matching no key, take the mapped frame color. A lower lightness threshold would also have caught dark mode's own light text.
8. **Fills:** every tab widget fills itself with the dark palette color, covering the panel's #edeeef background before any content draws over it, under translucent labels too.
9. **Panel titles:** an effect maps the strip between a panel's top and its first child.
10. **ClearType text:** *TrueType Hinting*'s preview draws black text with ClearType on purpose, each channel being the paper times one minus that subpixel's coverage. Inverting lightness gave colored edges; the helper's subpixel mapping keeps each channel's coverage and draws it from the mapped paper toward light text, which is ClearType's own light-on-dark rendering (the gutter's colored numbers take the plain mapping). Light text that ClearType draws as blue and yellow fringes with no light core (fields, the font window's *Name: Encoding: None Index* selectors) is drawn in grays before mapping, unless a field has selected text; panel titles likewise, whose hovered text is black with saturated fringes. The simple sliders are left as drawn: their white knob is right on dark already. An icon counts as a color swatch only when it is mostly filled: the thin red Y and X of the hinting direction buttons had been left black-stemmed.
11. **Icons and label images** are mapped the same way, except that their colored parts of middle lightness (a blue guide, a green fill) stay as they are: inverting their lightness had turned light blue icons dark blue: icons once per icon (cached), label images once each, and nothing twice. Color swatches (icons mostly made of clearly colored pixels, such as mark colors) and pictures larger than 64 px (such as the *About* artwork) are left as they are. A button inside an island that follows a menu action shows the action's original icon, since the island maps it.
12. **Title bars:** `DwmSetWindowAttribute` with `DWMWA_USE_IMMERSIVE_DARK_MODE`, once per window: each call makes Windows recompute the frame, and repeating it on a dialog still opening kept it relaying itself out until FontLab stopped responding.
13. **Stylesheet images:** FontLab's stylesheets name images in its resources (`url(:/...)`: the glyph window's info-bar icons, combo-box and spin-box arrows, tree arrows). Each is mapped once per shade into *%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\cache*, with its @2x version, and the darkened stylesheet points there.
14. **Check boxes and radio buttons:** Fusion frames them a little darker than the window color, which vanishes on a dark palette, and some of FontLab's lists and property grids draw them with no widget, out of any stylesheet's reach. The style is a `QProxyStyle` around Fusion that draws a visible frame over them on dark palettes, except where a stylesheet draws them as images (FontLab's blue boxes, whose rounded corners the frame showed through). PythonQt cannot read a style option's state, so the stylesheet is what tells them apart. FontLab's own application style is plain `QWindowsVistaStyle` (recorded at launch), so switching to Fusion drops none of its tweaks.
15. **Disabled menu text:** Fusion etches it with the palette's light color, which left a light shadow on a dark menu. That disabled color is now transparent.
16. **Restyles:** FontLab restyles some widgets while it works (fields while a glyph is edited, a button that turns green). Each sweep compares visible widgets' stylesheets with what dark mode set or noted, a first stylesheet included; a widget caught once is watched from then on (an event filter on `QEvent::StyleChange`) and darkened before it paints.

Known island classes are listed in the script; a glyph window's canvas is its `QWidget#widget` only, since its other plain children, such as the text bar, are interface. Any other visible widget is rendered once without its children: if a quarter of it is near-white, it becomes an island when it paints mostly itself (its visible children cover less than half of it, as a canvas with rulers does) with dark marks on it, else an ink area. A self-painted widget with a smaller light part (3% or more, such as a white margin) is an ink area too, and so is one whose own painting the ink mapping would change (dark text or marks, judged after its icons are mapped), unless a widget around it already maps it. Known classes match their subclasses too (FontLab's *UnicodeCellChart* is a *CellChart*). A widget's unprocessed ancestors are always processed first, since a child of a still-light parent looks light itself, and a widget is looked at again whenever its size changes, since a panel may first be seen at a placeholder size while it opens.

## Options

*Scripts > FreeLab > Dark Mode Settings* chooses the theme, dark or automatic (dark while Windows apps are set to dark, `AppsUseLightTheme` = 0, checked every 3 seconds, so it switches as Windows does; FontLab's own light look otherwise), mixed (a dark interface with FontLab's light glyph canvases, font cells, and previews, which the ink of the windows around them leaves as drawn), and the shade (dark grays, where white becomes #1f1f1f, or medium grays, where it becomes #3c3c3c, with a lighter palette). Each change shows at once; Cancel puts back what was there. There is no light choice: switching off is *Toggle Dark Mode*. An editor whose paper came from a light palette is mapped with the icon rule, which keeps its syntax colors of middle lightness. Switching by hand (*Toggle Dark Mode*) stops following Windows. The settings live in *darkmode.json*: `enabled`, `content` (the older `canvas` still counts), `shade`, and `follow_windows`.

## Platforms and builds

Written and tested on FontLab 7.2.0 for Windows (Qt 5.14.2, PythonQt, Python 2.7). Other FontLab 7 builds share the same interface code and should work, since unknown light widgets are found by rendering them, but only 7.2.0 has been run. FontLab 8 has a dark theme of its own.

FontLab 7 for macOS uses the same Qt, PythonQt, and Python 2.7 (installed separately on macOS 12.3 and newer), so the Qt side of dark mode should carry over. These parts are Windows-only and would need a macOS counterpart: the native helper (the C compiles as a *.dylib*; it loads as a *.dll* now), the dark title bars (DWM), following the system theme (the registry), and the installer (PowerShell, Windows paths). The macOS menu bar is native and outside Qt's styling. None of it has run on a Mac.

## The native helper

Mapping pixels in Python 2.7 would take seconds per frame. *darkmode/native/freelab_pixels.c* does it in C: the anchor blends (all, ink, and relight), the pixel-by-pixel mapping for icons, the keyed mapping, the pixel counts for detection, the color collection for keyed frames, and a diagnostic list of the last call's colors (`freelab_last_anchors`). It uses no C runtime and imports nothing, and FontLab's Python calls it through `ctypes` on `QImage.bits()`. *tools/build-native.ps1* builds it with clang or MSVC into *%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\bin\freelab_pixels-v<version>.dll*. The name carries the version because FontLab keeps a loaded DLL locked. Without it, islands fall back to plain color inversion, which flips hues, and the ink and keyed effects do nothing.

## Cost

- Switching on blocks FontLab for about 1.5 to 3 seconds: the style change alone repolishes every widget. The widgets are then processed in 20 ms slices, about 2.5 seconds in all.
- Menus: every menu's `aboutToShow` is connected once, so a menu is darkened as it opens; right after switching on, thousands of widgets wait in the queue, and a menu opened then flashed light.
- New windows: when keyboard focus moves (Qt's `focusChanged`, which fires as a window or tab opens), dark mode processes the visible new widgets at once, for up to 80 ms, so they barely show light. A sweep every 0.75 seconds catches the rest, and every 30 seconds it revisits every widget, catching stylesheets FontLab replaced.
- Any widget with an effect is drawn offscreen first. A full canvas render took 40 ms without dark mode and 55 to 100 ms with it (the mapping itself 13 to 18 ms; a full font window, 17 ms). Where everything is mapped, only colored pixels go through the pair search; a font window full of smoothed glyphs took 215 ms before that.
- The check-box style runs a little Python for every primitive Qt draws. If the canvas feels slow, set `"canvas": false` in *%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\darkmode.json* and switch dark mode off and on again: the canvas then keeps FontLab's own colors, and only its rulers are mapped.

## Switching off

Everything is recorded before it changes, and switching off puts it back without a restart: style, palette, stylesheets (unless FontLab replaced one meanwhile), palettes, icons, effects, fills, title bars, and the workspace color (unless it was changed meanwhile). The saved workspace color is kept in *darkmode.json* until then. Graphics effects FontLab sets itself are never replaced. One thing cannot be put back exactly. An editor FontLab created while dark mode was on took its dark paper and light text from the palette, once, and Python can neither read nor set those colors. It gets a "relight" effect, which turns that paper white and that text black and leaves FontLab's own syntax colors; some smoothing comes out rough. Restarting FontLab gives the exact light look, and switching dark mode on again replaces the effect. `tools/install-fontlab.ps1 -Uninstall` also puts the workspace color back, and asks for dark mode to be switched off first when FontLab is running.

## PythonQt traps met on the way

- Qt properties read as values: `widget.styleSheet`, `app.style().objectName`, and `widget.palette` are not callable.
- `QPalette` has no `PlaceholderText` name (the wrappers predate Qt 5.12), but the role works by number (20).
- `QWidget.winId()` returns nothing usable; window handles come from `EnumWindows` instead.
- `findChildren(QWidget)` returns an empty list.
- A Python subclass of a Qt class, such as a `QGraphicsEffect`, is deleted as soon as Python holds no reference to it, even after `setGraphicsEffect` gave it to a widget.
- `QGraphicsEffect.sourcePixmap` takes a `QPoint` to fill with the drawing offset, which is not zero for a widget inside another effect's widget.
- Under any widget stylesheet, `app.style()` is Qt's stylesheet wrapper, whose name is empty. Switching off therefore restores the `windowsvista` style by name.
- Paint events are not delivered while a listener request runs, so `widget.grab()` is the way to see the current look.
- Python identity does not tell Qt objects apart: `graphicsEffect()` may return another wrapper for the same effect. FreeLab tags its effects with a Qt property instead; taking its own effect for a foreign one once dropped the last Python reference to an installed effect, and FontLab crashed in *PythonQt.dll* on the next repaint.
- A Python 2 module that is no longer referenced has its globals set to None, and FontLab may drop modules from `sys.modules`, as a reload does. Anything Qt still calls into (timers, event filters, effects) then fails with `'NoneType' object is not callable`. Dark mode keeps every version of itself it loaded referenced, and the listener keeps its module.
- `QObject` cannot be subclassed from Python here; an event filter subclasses `QTimer` instead.
- `flWorkspace.changed()` is not a getter: calling it marks the workspace as changed.
- `ctypes.windll` releases Python's lock during each call. `SetWindowPos` (for the title bars) sends messages to FontLab's own windows synchronously, so Qt ran event filters and PythonQt overrides inside the call without the lock, and FontLab crashed in `PyErr_SetObject` (on opening *Scripts > Update / Install Scripts*, seen in crash dumps). Calls that can re-enter Qt go through `ctypes.PyDLL`, which keeps the lock.
- Switching off from the *Scripts* menu runs a fresh copy of the script, and the relight effects it makes call into that copy, so it is kept referenced too.
- A check box drawn with no widget gets the application palette in its style option, and no stylesheet rule reaches it.
- `QLineEdit.hasSelectedText`, `QWidget.isMinimized` and the like are properties too: calling them raises, and an effect that raised drew its widget unmapped.
- `QWidget.mapTo(parent, ...)` crashes FontLab when `parent` is not an ancestor: it walks the parent chain to its null end. Undocking a panel moves its widgets out of the effects above them, so an effect checks `isAncestorOf` before mapping a widget it protects, and forgets the widgets that left.
