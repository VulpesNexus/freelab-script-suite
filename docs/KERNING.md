# Auto kerning

*Scripts > FreeLab > Auto Kerning* suggests kerning for the pairs of the font's current master, one script at a time (Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, kana, Armenian, Georgian, Devanagari, Hebrew, or Thaana), by two methods and FontLab's own, shows how far they agree beside the kerning the font has, and writes only the pairs you leave ticked.

## Using it

1. Open the font, pick the master to kern, and run the script: it analyzes the master as it opens, and *Analyze* analyzes the font that is current then. *Script* lists the scripts the font has, Latin first (see [Other scripts](#other-scripts)); choosing another analyzes it. For a font with two scripts or more, its last choice, *All scripts*, analyzes each against its own references and lists their pairs together: a pair's glyphs show its script, and its tooltip names it. Every pair of the script's letters, figures (Latin's), and punctuation the font has is measured by both methods (two marks of punctuation side by side are skipped); about 7,000 pairs take two to five seconds. Each master's results are remembered, each script's apart (in *%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\kerning-cache*, under a hash of the font's path; uninstalling removes them): the next analysis measures only the pairs whose glyphs changed, and everything again when a reference glyph (l, n, o, H, O, 0, 8) did.
2. The list holds the pairs the font does not kern yet that either method would change by at least the given number of units (20 by default); FontLab's own autokern is asked about them too (a call per pair). *Show* narrows it to letters, letters and punctuation, or figures, and *Common pairs* to the pairs worth kerning even in a font kerned only in part: those common in the text of 24 languages and kerned in fonts ([andre-fuchs/kerning-pairs](https://github.com/andre-fuchs/kerning-pairs), MIT; *kerning/common_pairs.txt*), and for the other scripts those common in their text (*kerning/script_pairs.txt*, see below).
3. The list is laid out as the other tools' lists: each pair drawn from the font, as it stands on screen and at the value it takes (the one *Suggested* shows, which would be written; kana as palt sets them), beside its characters as read and its tick box, then its numbers, right-aligned with their headers. It was a table until November 2026, and in dark mode its header came out washed out; the list, like the other tools' lists, asks dark mode to leave it alone, so its header is drawn from the dark palette as it is. A pair picked by hand is tinted in the highlight color of the palette it is drawn with, so the tint follows a dark mode switch, and each picture is drawn again in the new text color. The columns: each method's value, *Spread* (how far they lie apart), and *Suggested*, from *Suggested values*: the average of halfkern and contour distance (the default), any one method, or FontLab's. Changing it changes only that column, never the list or its order. The order: largest change, best agreement (the safest pairs to accept together), most common, or alphabetical.
4. Selecting a pair draws it between two dimmed H's (in the other scripts their own straight letter: Cyrillic's En, Greek's Eta, kana's RO, Armenian's vo, Georgian's in, Devanagari's na, Hebrew's he, Thaana's noonu; a right-to-left pair as it stands on screen, see *Right to left*) as it is set now and by each method, a button each (drawn once a pair is selected), with the method's name, value, and key; the one it will take is pressed, framed and tinted light blue (a method with no value for the pair is dashed and says *not available*: FreeLab's, in the scripts where it did not beat the default, see [The FreeLab method in other scripts](#the-freelab-method-in-other-scripts)). Clicking another picks it for that pair alone (*Now* keeps the current kerning), and so do double-clicking a value in the list and pressing Ctrl and its number (Ctrl+1 *Now*, Ctrl+2 *Average*, Ctrl+3 halfkern, Ctrl+4 *Contour*, Ctrl+5 *FreeLab*, Ctrl+6 *FontLab*) while the list has the focus (a plain letter or number jumps to the pairs starting with it, as in any list). Clicking one of the H's around the pair, in any button, goes to that H's pair with the glyph beside it (*H* and *T* for *HTo*): it is listed whatever the filters, and measured first when it was not (an accented letter's pair). A pair picked by hand is tinted, its Suggested value bold, and *List only pairs picked by hand* appears to list them; *Reset pair*, Backspace, or double-clicking its Suggested value gives it the suggested value again; *Restore suggestions* puts every tick and pick back as the list first came. Space ticks or unticks the selected pair. The line above the buttons says where the pair's value will be written, and the list under it which other glyph pairs that entry kerns (see *Kerning classes*). *Show in Glyph window*, or double-clicking the pair's name, opens the pair between H's, n's, and o's in a Glyph window, to look at it with FontLab's own tools (between the pair's own script's controls where the font has them: Greek's Eta, nu, and omicron, Cyrillic's En, en, and o, kana's RO, NO, and I, Armenian's Vo, vo, and oh, Georgian's In, in, and on, Devanagari's pa, na, and ttha, Hebrew's he, final nun, and samekh, Thaana's noonu, raa, and meemu, zeros for figures). What a write did goes to the log under the previews; after an undo, a redo, or an edit in FontLab, the current values are read again. The dialog's ? button, then a click on any control, explains it.
5. *Include existing kerned pairs* lists the pairs the font kerns too, those written since included. They are never ticked for you: they are the designer's choices, some of which the methods do not model (a j tucked under the letter before it).
6. Untick what you disagree with, and click *Write ticked pairs*. Each pair is written where its value comes from (see *Kerning classes*); a value of 0 removes the entry unless another entry would then apply to one of the pairs it kerns (then 0 is written). Picks end once written, and the list shows the font's new values; the ticks stay, and *Write ticked pairs* stays grayed out until a ticked pair would get a value it does not have. The log's line names the pairs written when they are 12 or fewer, drawn from the font; a glyph clicked there opens in a Glyph window. Writing goes straight into the open font: close it without saving to take it all back.

The kerning the font already has acts as a model: with at least three tightenings in it, each method's tightenings are scaled to match how strongly the designer tightens, and the same for loosenings. Without them, halfkern's tightenings are applied at half strength, as halfkern applies them, and contour distance's in full.

### Kerning classes

*Current* is the kerning FontLab applies to the pair, its classes included (`getPlainPairsDict()`): a font kerned by classes (class *T* against class *o*) shows those values, not 0. A pair is written to the entry its value comes from now: its own pair if it has one, or else the pair of its classes, or of its class and the other glyph. With none, it goes to its classes' pair (or its glyphs' where they have no class). A class pair kerns every glyph of both classes (*Tcaron* with *oacute* too); the line above the buttons and the pair's tooltip name it and count its pairs. Checked pairs that share one entry get the value of the most common of them, and the status says how many. FontLab's scripting takes a name that is also a class's as the class, so where an entry names a glyph that shares a class's name (class *l* against glyph *j*, beside a class *j*), the class is renamed for the moment of writing (to a name no class and neither side of the entry bears) and renamed back; renaming a class keeps its pairs.

The classes are the font's own, made in FontLab's Classes panel: Auto Kerning reads and writes through them, and never makes, changes, or removes one.

### Suggested classes

*Suggest classes…* proposes kerning classes for the master, to review before any is made. Each side is its own set of classes: *first in a pair* groups glyphs by their right side (FontLab's left, or 1st, classes), *second in a pair* by their left side.

- **From the corpus**: *kerning/train/classes.py* reads the kerning classes of the same open-licensed fonts the FreeLab method learned from (155 families, once the same classes under another family name count once, class their kerning; where a font splits its kerning over subtables, a pair counts as classed together where most of them do) and counts, for each pair of characters and each side, how many families put them in one class. The counts are *kerning/class_model.json*.
- **An accented letter** joins its base letter where most families class the two together on that side (*À* with *A* almost always; *Ŀ* with *L* only on one side). For a pair the corpus has not seen, it joins where the two kern alike in this font.
- **Base letters** share a class where most families class them so (*n* with *m* and *h*, *o* with *c*, *e*, and *q*, the straight-stemmed capitals on their left) and every two of them kern alike in this font: within 2% of the em against each of a few partners (*H*, *O*, *o*, *n*, *v*, *T*, and others), measured by halfkern and contour distance.
- **May need pairs of their own**: members that need kerning looser than the class's first glyph by 3% of the em or more are named at the pair where they differ most, with the difference in units (*éT 52 units looser than eT*: the accent keeps *é* from tucking under *T*'s bar as far as *e* does). The class's value would bring that pair too close, so it may want an exception. Members the methods measure tighter are not named: that is air beside an accent the neighbor does not reach (an accent above *T*'s bar), and the class's value only leaves them a little loose.
- **Scripts**: classes are suggested for Latin letters (to Latin Extended-B), figures, and punctuation, the characters the corpus counts and the methods measure.
- **The font's classes come first**: a suggestion makes a class (named *kern1_* or *kern2_* and its first glyph) only where none of its glyphs has one on that side, or adds glyphs to the one class they share, unless that class kerns both sides. Suggestions that overlap several classes are shown and left as they are, and so are the classes the font has already.
- **Moving pairs**: with *Move each new class's first glyph's pairs into the class* (on by default), a new class takes over its first glyph's own pairs on that side, so every member kerns as that glyph did.
- **Pairs too close with their class's value**: the noted pairs the class tightens now (the member kerns beside the partner exactly as the class's first glyph does, below 0) are listed at the end, each with a value of its own: the class's, loosened by the difference measured, never past 0. *Write checked pairs* writes them as glyph pairs, which the class's value no longer reaches; the class kerns its other glyphs as before. The values are estimates: selecting one draws it under the list, *Now*, *Adjusted* (its own value), and *Base pair* (the class's first glyph beside the same partner), and the *Adjusted* pair's right glyph drags left or right (or Left and Right, Shift for 10 units) to set the value by eye before writing.

On the owner's test font, left out of the counts, 60 of the suggestions were classes the font has already.

## Kerning Check

*Scripts > FreeLab > Kerning Check* (or *Check kerning…* in Auto Kerning) holds three lists for the current master. Nothing changes until a list's button is clicked; writing goes straight into the open font, so closing it without saving takes it back.

The window opens as every FreeLab window: its top line names the font and its master (and how many masters it has, only when more than one), the line under it says what the list shown holds (it follows the tab), then a line separates them from the tabs. Each list's settings read as a sentence ended by a period, with *Check* at its end: *List [kerned pairs] whose ink comes within [41 units] [and that many units closer than without the accent (e.g., Ťo vs. To)].* on Collisions, *Also list values smaller than [5 units].* on Cleanup. At zero the line has no count: *No pair is kerned tighter: nothing to check.*, *The only pair kerned tighter does not come within 41 units…*, *None of the 438 pairs kerned tighter comes within 41 units…*. Every count in these lines is bold with its noun, never the number alone (the owner's images 297 and 301): ***2 of the 1,438 pairs** kerned tighter come within **41 units***, ***3 of 120 entries** listed: **2 entries** changing nothing, **1 tiny value***, ***3 differences** between **2 masters***. The three tabs' settings rows are as tall as one another, so the list never moves as a tab is shown (the owner's images 285 and 286: the *Masters* row, *Compare masters* alone, was a few pixels shorter than *Cleanup*'s, whose spin box is taller): each row stands in a widget of its own, whose least height is the tallest size hint of the controls in any of the rows, measured as the window is made and again at each switch (`level_rows`).

- **Collisions**: kerned pairs (tighter than 0) whose ink comes too close at the kerning FontLab applies, classes included, measured on the same renderings as the methods (200 pixels per em, so to about 5 units in a 1,000-unit em). By default a pair is listed when its ink comes within the distance set (2% of the em to start with) *and* that much closer than its base letter beside the same glyph: *Ťj* against *Tj*, *ąj* against *aj*. That is what a class's value does to an accented or barred member it was not set on, and it holds in any font, also one whose letters touch or overlap on purpose (joined crossbars, *TT* kerned to overlap): such pairs are compared with their own base pair, not with a fixed distance. A glyph is a variant of its class's key glyph (the glyph the class is named after, or its first member) when its name starts with the key's and its character is a letter built on the key's letter, or a letter with no decomposition (*Ŧ*, *Ł*); ligatures and other characters (*₩* after *w*) are not. Pairs kerned by an entry of their own are the designer's and are not compared. The list groups the pairs by the glyph that collides (*Ť*: 178 pairs in the owner's test font), closest first; each pair's fix sets it as far apart as its base pair (or, by the absolute measure, at the distance set) and is written as a pair of its own. As in the other tools' lists, each row draws its pair from the font (at its kerning) beside its characters, then its glyphs' names, then its numbers, right-aligned; *Cleanup* draws each entry's first pair. *Of each other, accented or not* lists every kerned pair within the distance, for fonts whose letters never touch. Both measure the same thing, the narrowest horizontal distance between the two glyphs' ink, row by row, at the pair's kerning; the first (*and that many units closer than without the accent*) also asks that the pair come that much closer than the same pair without its accent or bar. *Common kerned pairs* lists only pairs common in text (the list Auto Kerning's *Common pairs* uses).
- **Cleanup**: entries that change nothing (every pair they cover gets the same value without them), entries overridden for every pair they cover, entries naming a class with no members or a glyph the font lacks (these three ticked), and values smaller than the size set (5 units to start with; unticked, to look at first). *Clean up 3 ticked entries* (the count follows the ticks) removes the ticked ones. Entries are judged most specific first, each as if those found before it were removed: an exception equal to its class's value is listed, and the class entry it falls back on is not. Before removing, the ticked entries are judged again on the kerning as it is then: one whose value changed, or that would now change something (an exception whose class pair's value was edited since the check), is left as it is, and the log counts it.
- **Masters**: entries kerned in some masters and not others, entries tightening in one master and loosening in another (an instance between them may collide), and classes whose members differ between masters. A list only: masters differ on purpose as often as by mistake.

Selecting a row draws its pair under the list, its two glyphs see-through so that overlapping ink shows denser: *Now*, *Adjusted* (with its fix, or without the entry), and *Base pair* (Collisions), side by side with a line between them, each under its caption (the value it is drawn at). A collision's *Adjusted* pair can be set by hand: drag its right glyph left or right (only across: the value is all that changes), or click it and press Left or Right (Shift: 10 units); the fix follows, in the list too, and the row is ticked. Up and Down there select the next or previous row. *Show in Glyph window*, or double-clicking a row, opens the selected row's pair (a group's first pair, an entry's first glyph pair) in a new Glyph tab, as FontLab's *Window > New Glyph Tab* does, then sets its text. Each list is checked as its tab is first shown, and again after an undo, a redo, or an edit in FontLab; *Masters* is grayed out for a font with one master. *Tick all* and *Tick none* tick or untick a whole list, each grayed out when it would change nothing; *Restore suggestions* puts a list's ticks back as it first came. *Write ticked fixes* and *Clean up ticked entries* count the ticked rows (*Write 3 ticked fixes*).

Another script that changes glyphs opens Kerning Check on them: Auto Spacing's *Check kerning of these glyphs…* after spacing, and Glyph Compose after building glyphs or moving their diacritics. The lists then hold only what involves those glyphs: Collisions the pairs with one of them (only those pairs are measured, so it is quick), Cleanup the entries naming one or kerning one through a class, and Masters the entries and classes that do. A line in the window's header, over the tabs, says so (*Pairs with the 12 glyphs Glyph Compose built: Ŀ ŀ …*), and *Show all* beside it lists the whole font's. Entries are still judged against the whole kerning: an entry is listed the same, limited or not. A script calls it with `freelab_kerning_check.main(glyphs=[names], reason=u"Glyph Compose built")`, or Auto Kerning's `open_kerning_check(glyphs, reason)`.

On the owner's test font (22,665 pairs kerned tighter, most through classes), the collision check takes about 2.5 seconds and lists 2,735 pairs, mostly accented and barred letters under tall diacritics (*Ť*, *Ŧ*, *ŧ*, *î*, *Ý*, *ĵ*); cleanup lists 34 values under 5 units and nothing else.

## The methods

Both are measured by FreeLab's native core (*kerning/native/freelab_kern.c*) on glyphs rendered from FontLab's outlines at 200 pixels per em, and both hold each kind of pair to its own references, pairs the designer spaced on purpose and that are set with no kerning: capitals to *HH*, *HO*, *OH*, and *OO*; lowercase to *ll*, *nn*, and *oo*; figures to *0* and *8*; pairs across cases to the references of both sides; punctuation to the kind of the letter beside it. A pair is moved until its measure falls within the band its references span.

- **halfkern** ([behdad/halfkern](https://github.com/behdad/halfkern), Copyright 2022 Google LLC, Apache License 2.0): each glyph gets an envelope, its ink over a halo that fades out over about a tenth of the em at half strength; the measure is the overlap of the two envelopes, the sum of their squared products. The halo grows until *ll*, *nn*, and *oo* agree within a factor of two, as halfkern's does, and FreeLab interpolates between whole pixels where the overlap crosses the band. Checked against halfkern itself on the same renderings (Georgia, 19 pairs): the same kerning for every pair, including the two it cannot kern.
- **Contour distance**, after Matthew Skala's description of Tsukurimashou's kerning ([freetype-devel, June 2012](https://lists.nongnu.org/archive/html/freetype-devel/2012-06/msg00118.html)); written from that description, not from its code. Each glyph's left and right edges are found row by row, and their angle is limited (an edge moves at most 0.6 pixels across a row), so that a shape reaches out into the rows above and below it: a period cannot slide under an apostrophe. The measure is the average over the rows of the inverse cube of the gap between the facing edges, found by bisection. A tightening stops where the two glyphs' ink comes within 2% of the em.
- **FontLab's own** autokern (`flPackage.autoKern`, FontLab 8's "K1"), which returns its value without writing it. It is coarse: on the owner's test font it gave −56 for most pairs it tightened.
- **FreeLab**, FreeLab's own learned method: gradient-boosted trees trained on how fonts are kerned. Its inputs are both methods' readings, the gap between the pair's facing ink in 16 height bands, the two advances, and the font's own spacing (its *nn*, *oo*, and *HH*), all as shares of the em, measured by the same code as the other methods. It has two stages: one decides whether a pair is kerned at all, the other how much (a single one learned to say 0, since most common pairs are not kerned). The model, *kerning/learned_model.json*, is evaluated in plain Python.

### What the FreeLab method learned from, and how it was checked

*kerning/train/dataset.py* reads the fonts installed on the computer it runs on and those in FreeLab's corpus folder (*%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\corpus*, open-licensed fonts fetched from Google Fonts), and keeps only those whose name table declares the SIL Open Font License or the Apache License, with no proprietary terms beside it (*Segoe UI Historic* names Apache-licensed components in a Microsoft license, and is left out), and that kern. For the other scripts it also reads two subfolders of the corpus, never for Latin, so that Latin's training set stays the one the model was checked on: *kerning-fonts*, 329 families of Google Fonts fetched for their Cyrillic, Greek, Devanagari, Hebrew, Thaana, Thai, Armenian, kana, Arabic, Syriac, N'Ko, or Adlam (*kerning/train/corpus_fonts.json* pins each file), and the Noto fonts FreeLang is built from: 1,136 fonts on the owner's computer (2026-11-14) before the right-to-left ones. Fonts that kern fewer than 5% of a script's common pairs, the same design under another name, and fonts whose references cannot be measured were left out: for Latin, 899,715 pairs from 646 fonts in 267 families, with the kerning each font applies read through HarfBuzz, ligatures and contextual alternates off (an *fi* ligature is not kerning). The measurements stay on that computer; only the trained model is in the repository. *kerning/train/train.py* trains and checks it.

The families were split into five folds, each held out once (no family in both parts), and the methods' suggestions compared with the held-out fonts' kerning, in units of a 1000-unit em. Families that kern a script alike, within 2 units on 80% of the pairs either kerns, count as one design and share a fold (*Inter* and *Inter V*, the Source Han Sans fonts under their Noto names): 267 Latin families make 234 designs. Each pair is predicted once, by the model trained without its fold, and each script is scored over all its pairs at once (averaging the five folds' scores instead let a script of few designs swing with which fold each design fell in). The model shipped, trained on Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, Devanagari, Armenian, and Georgian pairs, on the Latin designs it never saw:

| Method | Mean error, all pairs | Mean error, kerned pairs | Within 20 units |
|---|---|---|---|
| No kerning at all | 9.4 | 38.4 | 85% |
| halfkern | 17.4 | 22.4 | 69% |
| Contour distance | 27.3 | 31.6 | 56% |
| Average of the two | 22.0 | 24.8 | 62% |
| **FreeLab** | **8.5** | **21.4** | **85%** |

The same model trained on Latin alone scored 8.8 and 21.5 on these folds (the model shipped before 2026-11-14 scored 8.6 and 21.1 on the corpus as it was then, without the merging of designs or the pooled scoring). halfkern and contour distance are scored here with their default strengths; in Auto Kerning, the font's own kerning tunes them, which narrows the gap. The FreeLab method is the only one that beats leaving most pairs alone while also coming closest on the pairs the fonts do kern.

These are averages over many fonts; one font can go the other way. The owner's test font, left out of training (`train.py --exclude`), checked with the model trained on Latin alone (not measured again since), on its 1,395 common pairs with its kerning as HarfBuzz applies it, classes included (817 kerned), each method's strength fitted to that kerning as Auto Kerning fits it:

| Method | Mean error, all pairs | Kerned pairs | Unkerned pairs | Within 20 units |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No kerning at all | 32.8 | 55.3 | 1.1 | 55% |
| halfkern | 29.9 | 34.0 | 24.1 | 47% |
| Contour distance | **22.7** | **29.7** | 12.7 | **63%** |
| FreeLab | 23.8 | 36.8 | **5.5** | 61% |

FreeLab leaves alone what the font leaves alone, but on that font's kerned pairs contour distance came closer: FreeLab leaves some at 0 that the font kerns. That is why the suggestions default to the average of halfkern and contour distance, with FreeLab one click away, and why each pair's methods are shown side by side. (An earlier version of this check read only the font's glyph pairs, missing its class kerning, and favored FreeLab wrongly.)

The two methods often disagree on pairs with diagonals and overhangs (contour distance tightens *To* and *TA* more), and agree on most others. On a font with kerning, contour distance came closer to the designer's own pairs (*LT*: designer −178, contour −154, halfkern −109).

## Other scripts

*Script* offers Cyrillic, Greek, kana, Armenian, Georgian, Devanagari, Hebrew, and Thaana where the font has the script's references (below) and at least 20 of its letters. Each is analyzed on its own, its pairs and their kerning listed and written as Latin's are. *All scripts* runs those analyses one after the other (each with its own references, its own cache, and its factors fitted to its own kerning; kana as palt sets them, left out with a warning when palt does not set them proportionally) and lists their pairs together; a pair is previewed between its own script's straight letter. The status line gives each method's factor per script where they differ.

### What the fonts do

Before any method was chosen, the open-licensed fonts were surveyed: 67 of the 68 Japanese families of Google Fonts (M PLUS 1 Code, monospaced, left out), the Armenian and Georgian ones, and the open-licensed fonts installed on the owner's computer, each pair of their kana (or letters) and punctuation shaped by HarfBuzz with and without kern.

- **Kana**: of those 67 families (a font of each), 14 have palt and 8 kern kana: Noto Sans JP (Source Han Sans, 818 pairs), Noto Serif JP (Source Han Serif, 3,936), IBM Plex Sans JP (2,685), Stick (2,026), Klee One (1,136), and Tsukimi Rounded (332), all with palt; Cherry Bomb One (8,431), whose kana are proportional; and LINE Seed JP (6,887), whose kana are full width with no palt (Slackside One kerns a single pair). Of the open-licensed fonts installed, Otomanopee One (24,109, proportional) adds a design; GenEi Gothic and Satsuki Gendai Mincho repeat Source Han's. Every one of them puts its kana pairs in kern, applied the same with palt on or off. What they kern: katakana with open sides (*アノ*, *クム*, *ケイ*), kana before the comma and full stop (*す。*, *ア、*: 305 of Noto Sans JP's 818 pairs), small kana (*っ*, *ゃ*), and the long vowel mark.
- **Armenian**: Google Sans kerns 2,705 pairs (its question mark, set over the vowel, most of all), Noto Sans Armenian 568, Noto Serif Armenian 346; Handjet, a pixel design, none.
- **Georgian**: Google Sans kerns 539 pairs, most of them Mtavruli or punctuation; Noto Sans Georgian kerns 848, and Noto Serif Georgian 1,237.

On 2026-11-14 the survey was widened to every OFL family of Google Fonts (google/fonts at commit c30b221b) with a Cyrillic, Greek, Hebrew, Devanagari, Thai, Armenian, Japanese, Arabic, Syriac, Thaana, N'Ko, or Adlam subset that the corpus lacked, its regular font each (329 families, 635 MB, in *%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\corpus\kerning-fonts*), and to the Noto fonts FreeLang is built from. Right-to-left pairs were typed in text order and shaped right to left. A family counts as kerning a script where kern changes at least 5% of a sample of its pairs (its letters with each other and beside its punctuation):

- **Cyrillic**: 166 families. In the training set (fonts kerning at least 5% of the common pairs), 158 families in 130 designs, 432,535 pairs, 13% of them kerned, 92% of those tighter: capitals with open sides and diagonals (*Т*, *У*, *Г*, *А*, *Р*, *К*) before the lowercase and punctuation, and *Ь* and *Ъ* before *Т*.
- **Greek**: 71 families by that sample; in the training set, which asks for 5% of the common pairs instead, 77 families in 60 designs, 126,394 pairs, 15% kerned, 95% of those tighter: *Τ*, *Υ*, *Γ*, *Ο*, *Α*, and the lowercase *λ* and *χ*.
- **Devanagari**: 14 families; in the training set 12 in 11 designs, 12,512 pairs, 12% kerned, 61% of those looser: *ka*, *da*, and *pha* (क, द, फ), whose right parts reach past their headline, before another letter, and *ra* (र) and *ttha* (ठ) tighter. Pairs of a consonant and a vowel sign, conjuncts, and half forms are glyphs the font's shaping makes, which Auto Kerning does not pair.
- **Kana**: the 40 Japanese families of Google Fonts the corpus lacked add no kana design: besides Source Han Sans and Serif under their Hong Kong and Chinese Noto names, only LINE Seed JP kerns its kana, full width with no palt (left out, as above). The 8 kana designs of the training set (16 family names) are those above.
- **Armenian**: one design more, Tiny5 (a pixel design, with Tiny5 Duo); **Georgian**: none more.
- **Hebrew**: 42 families, 19 designs in the training set once Rubik's 26 display variants count with Rubik (Noto Sans, Serif, and Rashi Hebrew, IBM Plex Sans Hebrew, Google Sans, Arimo, Secular One, Suez One, Miriam Libre, Gveret Levin, and others), 17,960 pairs; see *Right to left*.
- **Thaana**: one design, Noto Sans Thaana (662 pairs); see *Right to left*.
- **Left out**: Thai: three designs (IBM Plex Sans Thai, its Looped variant, Noto Sans Thai), too few to check a method on. Hangul: none of the corpus's Korean fonts kerns it (see CJK Spacing), and its 11,172 syllables would make millions of pairs. Arabic and the other joining scripts: see *Arabic and the joining scripts*. Samaritan, N'Ko, Adlam, and Mandaic: no font of the corpus kerns them (Noto Sans and the Google Fonts families that have them), so nothing could check a method on them.

Every script takes the measured methods, halfkern and contour distance, with references of its own. The FreeLab method, trained on Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, Devanagari, Armenian, and Georgian pairs, is offered in those six, where, held out on designs it never saw, it came closer than the average of the measured methods; kana, Hebrew, and Thaana show it *not available* (see *The FreeLab method in other scripts*).

### Kana

- **On top of palt**: kana are measured as the font's palt sets them, so the kerning adds only what palt leaves uneven and never undoes a trim. palt is read from the font's feature code (single adjustments, `pos a-hira <-70 0 -130 0>;` as CJK Spacing writes them, `[lists]` and `@classes` too). Kana proportional already (most narrower than 90% of the em) are measured as they are. Full-width kana that palt does not narrow are not kerned at all: kerned, they would be kerned in full-width text too. Write palt with CJK Spacing first.
- **Where the kerning goes**: into the font's kerning, FontLab's kern feature, as every font above kerns its kana: their kana pairs apply the same with palt on or off (HarfBuzz applies kern by default), so in full-width text too unless the application turns kerning off there.
- **References**: one kind for all kana, hiragana, katakana, small kana, and the long vowel mark alike: a square, a round, and an open kana, RO, NO, and I (ロ, の, い), set with each other under palt with no kerning. The band they span is wide, so that most pairs palt sets evenly are left alone. The halo is tuned on RO and grows until every pair of references overlaps: under palt, kana sit far apart, and no three kana agreed within a factor of two in every font tried. Small kana, the long vowel mark, and punctuation beside a kana (*す。*, *ア、*, the pairs fonts kern most) are held to the same band.
- **Pairs**: the 2,970 kana pairs common in text are measured (all of them, some 45,000, would each be listed and asked of FontLab), counted in 600 public-domain works of Aozora Bunko in modern orthography, the ruby and the library's notes taken out; together they make up 95% of the pairs of kana and punctuation in those texts.
- **Previews** are drawn as palt sets the kana. FontLab's Glyph window sets them as the glyphs' widths are, full width where palt narrows them: *Show in Glyph window* proofs a pair between RO, NO, and I, without palt.

### Armenian and Georgian

- **Armenian**: capitals against vo and oh (Ո, Օ), lowercase against vo, seh, and oh (ո, ս, օ), the halo tuned on the lowercase three; previews and proofs between vo, the capital too.
- **Georgian**: Mkhedruli against in, on, and san (ი, ო, ს), Mtavruli against their capitals (Ი, Ო), the halo tuned on in, on, and san, or on in and on alone where san is spaced far looser (Noto Serif Georgian); fonts without Mtavruli are kerned in Mkhedruli alone.
- **Pairs**: 1,614 Armenian and 1,113 Georgian pairs, counted in the introductions of 1,200 Armenian and 680 Georgian Wikipedia articles (Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0; see *kerning/script_pairs.txt*), 99% of their letter pairs. All-capital pairs (Armenian capitals, Mtavruli) are the lowercase pairs set in capitals.

### Cyrillic, Greek, and Devanagari

- **Cyrillic**: capitals against En and O (Н, О), lowercase against en, o, and pe (н, о, п), the halo tuned on the lowercase three (or on en and o alone where pe will not agree); previews between En, proofs between En, en, and o.
- **Greek**: capitals against Eta and Omicron (Η, Ο), lowercase against eta, omicron, and iota (η, ο, ι), iota being Greek's nearest to Latin's *l*; previews between Eta, proofs between Eta, nu, and omicron, as before.
- **Devanagari**: one kind, as Mkhedruli: na, pa, and ttha (न, प, ठ), two letters standing on their stem at the right and one hanging from the headline, set with each other with no kerning, their headlines joined as text joins them; previews between na, proofs between pa, na, and ttha. In 35 Cyrillic and 26 Greek fonts of the corpus no halo made the references agree, as in 81 Latin ones (thin weights, mostly): Auto Kerning says so for such a font, as it does for Latin.
- **Pairs**: 1,654 Cyrillic, 952 Greek, and 736 Devanagari pairs, 99% of the pairs counted: letter pairs from Unilex's word frequencies (counted from web text, case kept) for Russian, Ukrainian, Belarusian, Bulgarian, Serbian, and Macedonian, for Greek, and for Hindi and Marathi, each language weighing the same; pairs of a letter and punctuation from the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in each of those languages and Nepali (public domain), weighed by their share of its text. All-capital pairs as for Armenian, Greek's without the tonos. All pairs of the script's characters are measured, as in Latin (about 13,900 in Cyrillic, twice Latin's, so about twice as long); the common ones are what *Common pairs* lists.

### Right to left

Hebrew and Thaana are set right to left: in text, a pair's first character stands at the right.

- **Measured as on screen, kept in text order**: a pair is measured, previewed, and drawn as it stands on screen, its left glyph first (the gap between the left glyph's right side and the right glyph's left side), as the training read the fonts. The font's kerning keeps it in text order, its right glyph first: that is how FontLab 7 holds right-to-left kerning, read live on Noto Sans Hebrew (2026-11-14): typed yod then bet, the font's 64 units are FontLab's pair (yod, bet), and (bet, yod) is empty; tav and zayin, dalet and final pe, the same. So *bet* left of *yod* is read from, and written to, the pair (yod, bet): where the font kerns it already, Auto Kerning shows that value as *Now* and writes over it, never a second pair the other way round. FontLab 7 has no setting or scripting call for a pair's direction. (FontLab 8.2 and later document the opposite, one storage in visual order, converting from text order where they find right-to-left pairs in a file that keeps them so; a font kerned here and opened there goes through that conversion.)
- **How the font applies it**: a compiled font kerns a right-to-left pair in text order, its first glyph the right one, the value on that glyph's placement and advance, as Noto Sans Hebrew does; FontLab 7 keeps such a font's kern feature as code on import, the same way (`pos uni05D0 uni05DB <-6 0 -6 0>;`, with no *RightToLeft* flag, which shapers need only for cursive attachment). The advance alone (`pos yod bet -64;`) moves nothing on screen: shaped right to left by HarfBuzz, the glyph it shortens is the rightmost. FontLab 7 has no right-to-left kerning of its own (FontLab added it in 8.2; its support pages give, for earlier versions, a kern feature written by hand with each pair's value given as both placement and advance). So check the exported font: where FontLab 7 builds the kern feature from its kerning, a Hebrew pair written by the advance alone does nothing in right-to-left text, and where the font keeps a kern feature of its own as code (an imported font does), pairs written to the kerning reach the exported font only once that feature is made again from it.
- **In the window**: the list shows a pair as a reader sees it (typed in text order, so that its first letter stands at the right and a typed letter finds the pairs it starts in text; a pair with punctuation inside a right-to-left embedding, so that the mark stays on its side and brackets are mirrored), and its tooltip and the line above the previews name the glyph that comes first in text. The previews draw the pair as on screen between two dimmed straight letters; *Show in Glyph window* sets it in text order, as FontLab 7's Glyph window sets typed Hebrew, left to right, and kerns it there by that key; the log draws written pairs from the font, left glyph first. *Common pairs* and *Most common first* look the pair up in text order; *Alphabetical* sorts by the first letter in text. Kerning Check reads the font's pairs in text order and measures, draws, labels, and filters them as on screen (Collisions, Cleanup, and the Adjusted preview alike); Proof's *Common pairs* looks them up in text order.
- **Each pair by its own glyphs**: a font with Latin, Hebrew, and Arabic has keys of each direction side by side, so no tool takes a direction from the font or from the *Script* chosen: each pair is read by its own glyphs' characters. A key is right to left when either glyph is a Hebrew or Thaana letter, or a character of the blocks Unicode gives a right-to-left direction by default (Arabic, Syriac, N'Ko, Samaritan, Mandaic, their presentation forms, and plane 1's right-to-left scripts, from Phoenician to Adlam; `RTL_RANGES`), numbers apart (Arabic-Indic digits are set left to right in right-to-left text, and an Arabic font's pairs of them are left to right in its own GPOS). An unencoded glyph goes by its base glyph's character, named by its name's stem (*uni0628.fina* by *uni0628*, *yod.alt* by *yod*: an Arabic font's kerning is mostly between unencoded contextual forms), and a glyph with neither takes its partner's direction (`direction_char`, `screen_of_key`). Auto Kerning measures only Hebrew and Thaana right to left, but Kerning Check, Font Compare, and Proof read every font's keys, an Arabic font's included: before this, Kerning Check measured an Arabic key's ink gap on the wrong sides, and Font Compare drew a Hebrew pair the wrong way round. The table is Proof's too, and not Python's Unicode data, which FontLab's Python 2.7 has at version 5.2 only, unable to read a character past U+FFFF on its narrow build. That FontLab 7 keeps an Arabic font's pairs in text order, as it keeps Hebrew's, is read from its import of Hebrew (it keeps each GPOS pair in the lookup's own order) and not yet read live on an Arabic font.
- **FontLab's own autokern** (`flPackage.autoKern(a, b, master)`, the *FontLab* column) measures the glyphs as given, *a* at the left: on Noto Sans Hebrew, its values follow FreeLab's own measure of *a* set left of *b*, not the reverse (live, 2026-11-14: bet left of yod, −55 against FreeLab's −48 and −49; yod left of bet, −35 against −30 and −17), so it has no notion of direction, and Auto Kerning asks it for the pair as it stands on screen.
- **Brackets**: right to left, an opening parenthesis is set with the glyph of the closing one (Unicode's mirroring): the *)* glyph at the right of a Hebrew letter is an opening parenthesis typed before it, and is counted, listed, and shown so.
- **Hebrew**: one kind, against he, final mem, and samekh (ה, ם, ס), an open letter, a closed square one, and a round one; previews between he, proofs between he, final nun, and samekh. Points and cantillation marks are marks, placed by the font's mark positioning, and not measured. In two fonts of the corpus (Rubik variants) no halo made the references agree.
- **Thaana**: against noonu, raa, and meemu (ނ, ރ, މ); its vowel signs (the fili) are marks over and under the letters, so letters stand side by side as text sets them.
- **Pairs**: 375 Hebrew pairs, letter pairs from An Crúbadán's Hebrew character trigrams (2010, NLTK's *crubadan*, GPL 3 or later; Unilex has no Hebrew), punctuation pairs from the declaration; 331 Thaana pairs from the declaration in Dhivehi alone (Unilex has no Dhivehi). Points and fili were taken out before counting. Each is 99% of the pairs counted, kept in text order.

### Arabic and the joining scripts

Arabic, Syriac, N'Ko, Adlam, and Mandaic join their letters, and shaping puts each letter's initial, medial, final, or isolated form in its place. Auto Kerning measures a character's glyph, the one the font maps it to, which for these is the isolated form; the forms that stand side by side in text, and that fonts kern, are others. In the corpus, 33 families kern Arabic (10 of them 5% of a sample of letter pairs or more: Aref Ruqaa, Gulzar, Harmattan, Jomhuria, Katibeh, Markazi Text, Mirza, Qahiri, Scheherazade New, and Aref Ruqaa Ink), most of it between letters that do not join: after alef, dal, thal, reh, zain, waw, and teh marbuta, which join no letter after them, the next letter starts a new part of the word, and the gap between the two parts is what is kerned. Syriac's kerned pairs (Noto Sans Syriac and its Western variant, Idiqlat, Ramsina) are mostly joined, a final form before an initial one. N'Ko, Adlam, and Mandaic are kerned by no font of the corpus.

Kerning them on a par with the other scripts would take:

- **Glyphs for the forms**: each letter's final and isolated forms (the right glyph of a pair across a break) and its initial and isolated forms (the left glyph), read from the font's init, medi, fina, and isol substitutions in its feature code (FontLab keeps the font's features as code), or from the glyph-name suffixes FontLab's own names give (*beh-ar.init*): Auto Kerning's pairs are of characters, and these would be pairs of glyphs, a letter and its form.
- **Pairs and references**: the pairs of a right-joining letter's final or isolated form and the next letter's initial or isolated form, counted in text by those positions, and references for the gap between word parts (alef's isolated form, the commonest such gap, and a round and a toothed letter); figures and punctuation besides. Marks (harakat) and kashida stay out.
- **Measuring and training**: the forms rendered by glyph rather than by character (the training renders characters), and the fonts' kerning read by shaping a letter between context letters that put each in its form; the corpus's 33 families would then be measured and checked as Hebrew was.
- **The window**: a pair's label with the forms shown as text joins them (a zero-width joiner after an initial form, before a final one), and the same right-to-left order as Hebrew.

None of that is done: it changes what a pair is throughout Auto Kerning and Kerning Check, and it was not done halfway.

## Scripts and characters

Auto Kerning lists pairs of these characters only, a script at a time:

- **Latin**: the letters A to Z and a to z, the figures 0 to 9, and the punctuation `. , : ; ! ? ' " - ( ) [ ] { } / & *`, the curly quotes, guillemets, and the en and em dashes.
- **Kana**: hiragana and katakana (small kana, the iteration marks, and the long vowel mark included), with the ideographic comma and full stop, the corner, angle, and lenticular brackets, the full-width comma, full stop, colon, semicolon, question and exclamation marks, and parentheses, the middle dot, the wave dash, the ellipsis, and the curly quotes.
- **Armenian**: the capitals and small letters (U+0531 to U+0587), with Armenian's apostrophe, emphasis mark, exclamation mark, comma, question mark, full stop, and hyphen, and the Latin comma, full stop, colon, parentheses, guillemets, hyphen, and dashes.
- **Georgian**: Mkhedruli (U+10D0 to U+10FF) and Mtavruli (U+1C90 to U+1CBF), with Latin's punctuation and the low and high double quotes.
- **Cyrillic**: the letters of Russian, Ukrainian, Belarusian, Bulgarian, Serbian, and Macedonian (U+0401 to U+045F, less the four with a grave accent, and Ukrainian's ghe with upturn, Ґ and ґ), with Latin's punctuation and the low double quote.
- **Greek**: monotonic Greek, the capitals and small letters with the vowels with tonos and dialytika (U+0386 to U+03CE), with Latin's punctuation.
- **Devanagari**: the independent vowels and the consonants (U+0905 to U+0939), with the danda and double danda, Latin's comma, full stop, colon, semicolon, exclamation and question marks, quotes, hyphen, parentheses, and dashes.
- **Hebrew**, right to left: the 27 letters, final forms included (U+05D0 to U+05EA), with the maqaf, sof pasuq, geresh, and gershayim, Latin's comma, full stop, colon, semicolon, exclamation and question marks, quotes, hyphen, parentheses, and brackets (mirrored, see *Right to left*), the low and high double quotes, and the dashes.
- **Thaana**, right to left: the letters (U+0780 to U+07A5, and naa), with the Arabic comma, semicolon, and question mark, and Latin's full stop, colon, exclamation mark, quotes, hyphen, parentheses, and dashes.

Any other character, accented Latin letters (é, Å, ł), the letters of other Cyrillic orthographies (Kazakh's ә, ғ, қ), Devanagari's vowel signs, Hebrew's points and Thaana's fili, Arabic and the other joining scripts, and kanji included, is not measured, not listed, and never written: its pairs keep the kerning the font gives them.

| Method | What it could measure | What it is set for |
|---|---|---|
| halfkern | any glyphs: it measures shapes | each script's references: Latin's *l*, *n*, *o*, *H*, *O*, *0*, *8*, and those above (Cyrillic's *Н*, *О*, *н*, *о*, *п*; Greek's *Η*, *Ο*, *η*, *ο*, *ι*; Devanagari's *न*, *प*, *ठ*; Hebrew's *ה*, *ם*, *ס*; Thaana's *ނ*, *ރ*, *މ*); another script needs references of its own |
| Contour distance | any glyphs | the same references; Tsukurimashou tuned the method for Latin, Japanese, and Korean, with other settings for each |
| FontLab's own | any glyph pair | nothing of FreeLab's: FontLab's own settings |
| FreeLab | the pairs it was trained on | Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, Devanagari, Armenian, and Georgian pairs of open-licensed fonts; used for those six, where it beat the default on designs it never saw; not for kana and Hebrew, where it did not, nor Thaana, kerned by one design (*not available*) |

Accented letters take their base letter's kerning where the font's classes hold both: a pair written to a class pair kerns them too.

## Limits

- Classes are used, not made: a font without classes gets glyph pairs, and its accented letters keep their own kerning. Classes are suggested for Latin only.
- Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, kana, Armenian, Georgian, Devanagari, Hebrew, and Thaana (see *Scripts and characters*); pairs across scripts (a katakana word before a Latin one) are not measured. Thai, Hangul, Arabic and the other joining scripts, Samaritan, N'Ko, Adlam, and Mandaic are not, for the reasons in *What the fonts do* and *Arabic and the joining scripts*.
- FreeLab's suggestions are given in every script but kana, Hebrew, and Thaana; in Devanagari, no method beat leaving the pairs alone.
- Right-to-left kerning is written as FontLab 7 keeps it, in text order; whether FontLab 7's export gives it the placement it needs in right-to-left text is to be checked in the exported font (see *Right to left*).
- Kana are kerned only as palt sets them or where they are proportional already; their kerning applies wherever kern does, full-width text included, as in the fonts that kern kana.
- Each method has blind spots: halfkern loosens *Ty* in Georgia, where the T's bar meets the y's arms, and cannot kern *VA* there. Review before writing.
- Other published methods, and the one not to implement (Apple's, patented until 2033), are in [FEATURE-SURVEY.md](FEATURE-SURVEY.md).
