# Glyph Compose

*Scripts > FreeLab > Glyph Compose* builds the glyphs a font lacks from the glyphs it has, on two tabs. *Diacritics* builds the letters with diacritics and the voiced kana, and places the anchors of scripts whose marks attach (below). *Glyph generation* builds spaces, hyphens and dashes, duplicates, punctuation, Greek and Cyrillic look-alikes, ligature letters, vulgar fractions, arrows and triangles, turned and reversed letters, superscript and subscript signs and letters, signs made of letters (*™*, *№*, *℃*), circled figures and letters, full-width and half-width forms, small capitals, letters with bars, small kana, squared words and abbreviations, Kangxi radicals, telegraph symbols, Roman numerals, and parenthesized and numbered forms ([Glyph generation](#glyph-generation)). Nothing is made until you click *Build ticked glyphs* (on *Mark anchors*, *Add ticked anchors*), and *Correct mirrored components* changes only the glyphs it lists. *Edit > Undo* takes a write back, in two steps when some of its glyphs are made of others it adds ([The list, the log, and the History](#the-list-the-log-and-the-history)).

What the tool saved under its earlier name, Diacritics, is kept: its reference font and *Compact diacritics to fit the line* are read once from *diacritics.json* into *glyph_compose.json* (FreeLab's state folder), mark features whose opening line reads *made by FreeLab Diacritics* are read as its own and replaced by the next write, and the installer removes the old *freelab_diacritics.py*.

## The window

The window is laid out as every FreeLab window is ([WINDOWS.md](WINDOWS.md#layout); the owner's review of 2026-11-17). Under the top line, the shown tab's status lines (what its list holds, what is left out, and why), then the header's line, and only then the tabs, so the busy bar of the top row never meets them. The bottom row holds the shown tab's buttons: at the left those that act on the list's ticks and rows (*Tick all*, *Untick all*, *Tick high-value glyphs*, *Restore suggestions*, *Show in Glyph window*, *Check kerning of these glyphs*, *Correct mirrored components*), at the right the committing action (*Build ticked glyphs*; on *Mark anchors*, *Write the feature code* then *Add ticked anchors*), then *Close*.

## Diacritics

The *Diacritics* tab builds the letters with diacritics a font lacks (Latin, Greek, polytonic Greek, Cyrillic, and the voiced kana of Japanese), as glyphs of components, in every master, on its first subtab, *Letters with diacritics*; on its second, *Mark anchors*, it places the anchors and writes the mark features by which scripts that attach their marks (Khmer, Thai, Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, Bengali, Gujarati, Tamil, and others) set them. Nothing is made until you click *Build ticked glyphs* (on *Mark anchors*, *Add ticked anchors*). It needs no groundwork in the font: no anchors, no accented glyphs to learn from, no *.case* marks, not even marks. Where the font has some, it follows them.

### Diacritic and mark

FreeLab's tools say *glyph*, not *letter* or *character*, wherever they mean a glyph of the font. In the same way, they say *diacritic* and *mark* each for its own thing, as type design does:

- **Diacritic**: the sign as a reader sees it on a letter, an acute on *é*, a dieresis on *Ӛ*. What is placed, moved, scaled, lined up, made compact, or too tall for a line is a diacritic, and a letter is a letter *with* diacritics: *Click a diacritic to move or scale it*, *Same diacritic*, *Reset diacritics*, *Line up diacritics above*, "diacritics placed by hand", "gaps closed and diacritics at 85%".
- **Mark**: the glyph that draws it, and everything type design and OpenType call so: a mark glyph (*acutecomb*, *uni0304*, a draft made of a period), a component a glyph is built from, what the font has or lacks ("Need a mark or letter the font lacks"), Unicode's combining (nonspacing) marks, and what attaches by anchors (*_top*, mark classes, the *mark* and *mkmk* features, the *Mark anchors* tab and its sample marks). A mark *is* a glyph; a diacritic is what it shows.

Where a sentence speaks of a glyph by its name, it is a mark; where it speaks of where something sits on a letter, it is a diacritic. "Mark" is also FontLab's word for a glyph's color flag, which is neither. Where the tool and its subject go without saying (*Suggested (11 right, 46 down)*: in Diacritics, what moves is a diacritic), no noun is needed.

### What it builds

- **Which characters**: every character of the chosen set (*Character set:* Latin-1 to Latin Extended-B, Latin Extended Additional, Greek, Greek Extended (polytonic), Cyrillic, Kana with dakuten (Japanese), or *All*) that the font lacks and that Unicode's canonical decomposition splits into a letter and marks (*ǎ* is *a* with a caron, *й* is *и* with a breve). A script the font has no letters of is left out, and the status lines say so ("The font has no Cyrillic, Greek, or kana letters: theirs are not listed"). A glyph left empty (one added without its layers) is listed again and filled under its own name. A glyph that would come out empty (a part with no outline in a master) is not built: the log says which part, the glyph the build added for it is taken out again, and the other ticked glyphs are built. Where FontLab keeps none of a glyph's components in a master, the log names that master: a new glyph is left empty there, and a glyph the font had keeps its own outlines there.
- **Which marks**, in this order:
  1. The font's own, by name (*caroncomb*, *caron*, *uni030C*, and the names fonts give the marks below: *caroncomb.alt*, *commaaccentrotate*) or code point; for capitals, its *.case* form first; the script's own form before all (Greek's *tonos* for an acute, Cyrillic's *brevecomb-cy* for the short i's breve).
  2. For a mark below, its twin above (a dot below from the dot accent, a macron below from the macron; a comma below from the comma).
  3. A draft made of the font's punctuation: a dot accent of the period, a dieresis of two periods, a macron of the hyphen, a ring of the degree sign, a tilde of `~`, a circumflex of `^`, a caron of `^` turned upside down, a double acute of two acutes, a vertical caron of *quoteright* (else the modifier apostrophe or the comma), a turned comma above of the modifier turned comma or *quoteleft* (else the comma turned about), a dakuten of `”`, a handakuten of the degree sign. A draft is sized as that mark is in the corpus (a dot accent 0.11 em high, for example) and the list says it is one: a starting point to redraw. Upside down is a half turn (the component scaled by −1 both ways), never a mirror: a mirrored component's contours run the other way, and a renderer shows a hole where it overlaps another part (see *Limits*). The half turn is also the better shape: it gives a caron its thick stroke at the left, as a circumflex has its own at the right, and a slanted mark keeps its slant. An inverted breve the font lacks is its breve, turned the same way.

  *i* and *j* (and Cyrillic *і* and *ј*) take their dotless forms under a mark above. Where the font has no dotless *i* (*ı*, U+0131) or *j* (*ȷ*, U+0237), its *i* or *j* is read in every master it is drawn in, by its contours:

  - **Two contours, one clearly the dot**, the same one in every master: the dot is the higher contour, its center above the other's top (it may overlap the stem's top), the smaller of the two by its box, and over the other, not beside it (a *j*'s tail makes its body's box wider and taller, which changes nothing). The dotless glyph is made of the other contour (*dotlessi*, *dotlessj*), with the letter's advance width, so its sidebearings too, in every master, at its code point, and the letters are built on it as on a drawn one. Their rows say so (*dotlessi is i without its dot, built here first*), the dotless letter itself is listed to build where the character set has it (*i without its dot*, ticked), and the log names the glyph made ("Made dotlessi from i without its dot in every master: the font had none."). It is made before the glyphs built on it, in a History step of its own (see *The list, the log, and the History*), and taken out again when none of them is built.
  - **One contour**: the font's *i* is drawn without a dot, and is used as it is (*on i itself: the font's i has no dot*).
  - **Anything else** (three contours or more, a component, masters that differ, or two contours neither of which is clearly the dot: the higher one the larger, or its center under the other's top): the letter is not taken apart. The glyphs are built on the dotted letter, as before, ticked, as a glyph with a diacritic too close is, and said as such a glyph is: their row says why (*the dot of i stays under the diacritic: the font has no dotless i, and its i has 3 contours; draw U+0131*), their letter is amber in *Same diacritic*, and the log names them in amber at the build ("2 glyphs built on i with its dot: … Draw U+0131, then rebuild them: ì í."). The dotless letter itself has no row then.

  Cyrillic *ї* is built on the dotless *i* made so, as on a drawn one. Greek's dialytika with tonos (*ΐ*) takes the font's *dieresistonos*, or its dieresis and tonos set over each other, the tonos between the dots. Glyphs that still cannot be built are listed apart, with what the font lacks (Vietnamese's horn and hook above have nothing to be drafted from).
- **Marks fonts draw otherwise**: for a few letters, fonts draw another mark than the one Unicode's decomposition names (Unicode's names for them predate the conventions). Diacritics builds them as most fonts do. *compose/train/conventions.py* told, in 433 open-licensed families, which mark each font draws on every letter it can build: by the components' names and code points in a composite glyph (*uni0326*, *caroncomb.alt*, *quoteright*), or by finding a mark glyph's outlines, point for point, in a glyph of outlines; a comma is a comma below under the letter, a vertical caron beside a stem's top. These are the letters where most fonts depart from the decomposition (the fonts counted are those whose mark could be told):

  | Letter | Unicode's mark | The fonts' mark | Fonts |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Ģ | cedilla | comma below | 224 of 232 (97%) |
  | ģ | cedilla | turned comma above | 155 of 180 (86%) |
  | Ķ ķ | cedilla | comma below | 234 of 241, 243 of 250 (97%) |

  Latvian (and Livonian, for *ḑ*) draws a comma below; Marshallese, which also writes *Ļ ļ Ņ ņ*, a cedilla. Latvian is by far the more common, and the fonts follow it: so does Diacritics (a font for Marshallese keeps its own cedilla glyphs). *Ţ ţ* and *Ş ş* keep their cedilla: most fonts draw one (Romanian's comma-below letters are *Ș ș Ț ț*, which Unicode decomposes with a comma below already). Each such mark is the font's own first (*commaaccentcomb*, *uni0326*, *commaturnedabovecomb*, *caroncomb.alt*, *caroncommaaccent*), then a draft, and its row says why (*the comma below fonts draw for Unicode's cedilla*). The vertical caron sits right of the stem's top, its top level with the letter's (0.048 em right of *d*'s and *l*'s stem, 0.091 em of *L*'s; on *t*, 0.042 em right and 0.1 em over its top, level with the ascenders), the turned comma above *g* like an acute (0.053 em over it, centered), as the corpus's medians set them.
- **Kana**: the voiced kana (*が* is *か* with a dakuten, *ぱ* is *は* with a handakuten; the katakana too, *ヴ*, *ヷ* to *ヺ*, and the voiced iteration marks) are built like the letters with diacritics, from the base kana and the font's dakuten or handakuten (the combining U+3099 and U+309A, else the spacing U+309B and U+309C, by code point or name), else a draft. The mark's ink is centered where the open-licensed Japanese fonts center theirs, from the right end and the top of the kana's ink: *compose/train/kana.py* found the mark in each voiced kana of 42 families (as a component, by its outlines, or as the contours its kana lacks) and measured it against the kana as drawn there. A dakuten's center sits a median 0.049 em left of a hiragana's right end and 0.065 em under its top (katakana: 0.037 and 0.025), a handakuten's 0.083 and 0.031 (katakana: 0.074 and 0.001), and each kana has its own row where 3 fonts or more show it. Each font left out in turn, the marks land a median 0.044 em from where that font puts them by the per-kana rows, 0.088 em by the hiragana and katakana medians alone. 37% of the fonts' voiced glyphs move or redraw their kana a little for the mark; Diacritics keeps the font's kana as it is. Voiced kana are named *uniXXXX*, as kana fonts name them. The set is listed when the font has kana.
- **Names**: Glyphs' names for the characters (*iishort-cy*, *Alphatonos*, *adotbelow*), from glyphsLib's *GlyphData.xml* (MIT License, its notice in *compose/GlyphData_LICENSE*), kept in *compose/glyph_names.json*; else the letter's name and the marks'; *uniXXXX* where those are taken. Each glyph gets its Unicode value.

### Where the marks go

- **Gaps**: from the font's own composites, per mark and case, where it has two or more (a composite counts where its character decomposes into its components: not a fraction, a guillemet, or *Ŀ*'s middle dot). Each mark counts as the character's own (a macron borrowed for a macron below is a mark below, not a macron above), measured from its letter as the composite places both (a letter component moved right moves its marks with it); a Greek capital's mark at its left counts on no side, and a composite with a component scaled, flipped, rotated, or slanted is not counted. Otherwise, from the corpus: *compose/train/marks.py* measures how the open-licensed fonts at hand (250 families) build their accented letters as composites (the mark fonts draw, where it is not Unicode's: Latvian's comma below is measured as a comma below, not as a cedilla), into *compose/mark_model.json*. A font whose own gaps run larger or smaller than the corpus's has the corpus's gaps scaled by the same amount.
- **Across**: each mark is centered on the center of the letter's top fifth (bottom fifth for marks below; the ogonek hangs from the right end of it), offset by a share of the letter's width. That share is the font's habit on that side and case, all its marks pooled (so a breve and a caron agree on *i*), or the corpus's, plus how far the corpus puts marks on that very letter from its habit: over *b*'s bowl rather than its stem (44% of its width), *K* and *Y* almost centered. A slanted hand's lowercase habit does not carry over to capitals. The corpus's letters are cut into fifths as Diacritics cuts the font's in FontLab, their outlines, curves and all (not their points, whose off-curve handles reach past the ink: measured that way, a top fifth's center ran 2 to 3% of the letter's width left of FontLab's on *c*, *C*, *G*, and *Æ*, and up to 7% off either way on *o* in a tenth of the fonts). Against 120 fonts' own composites, a mark above lands a median 17 units across from theirs (18 before), 18 on *c* (22), 22 on *C* (28), 16 on *G* (19).
- **Stacks**: marks stack the way the corpus stacks each pair. An acute or a hook above sits beside a circumflex, to its right and a little lower than its top, as Vietnamese is set; a tilde over a circumflex, or a macron over a dieresis, sits on it, about 0.05 em apart.
- **Greek capitals** carry their tonos (and breathings) at their left, the mark's top level with the capital's, 0.03 em away; the glyph is made wider by as much, so the mark keeps the letter's left sidebearing.
- **Polytonic Greek**: a breathing and its accent are one mark, the font's own (*psilioxia*, *dasiavaria*) or its two marks side by side, the breathing first, 0.025 em apart; a perispomeni sits on its breathing (*psiliperispomeni*) or dialytika; the dialytika takes its tonos or varia between its dots. The iota subscript goes under a small letter (*ᾳ*), and after a capital as an adscript (*ᾼ*: the font's *prosgegrammeni*, else its iota), on the baseline, the glyph made wider. A capital's breathings and accents sit at its left, as one.

### Room above and below

A glyph's diacritics must stay clear of the lines around it at a 120% line spacing (applications' automatic leading): its top below where the line above's descenders come down (1.2 em plus the descender), its bottom above where the line below's tallest letters (the ascender or cap height) come up. Only what the diacritics add counts: a letter that reaches past a line by itself keeps that reach, and its diacritics are measured from it. In Noto Sans, whose ascender is 1069, the line below's letters come up to −131, and *y*'s descender reaches −240 by itself: *ӯ* counts only what its macron adds, measured from that reach.

*Compact diacritics to fit the line*, beside the preview, left of *Reset diacritics*, makes the diacritics of a glyph that would not fit compact, by no more than it needs: the gaps of its stack closed just enough to fit, down to 40% of them, and only where that is not enough, its diacritics made smaller too, to the largest whole percent that fits, down to 70%; the list says what it took ("A + dieresis + macron (gaps closed to fit a 120% line)", "(gaps closed and diacritics at 85% to fit a 120% line)"). One that still does not fit is listed unticked: it needs a shorter letter or marks drawn for it. The letter itself is never made compact. The box keeps its place whatever is selected: the editor, its buttons, and the *Same diacritic* strip keep their room when hidden, and the three buttons beside it are as wide as their widest text ("Apply to 3 related letters", "Line up vertical carons"), which the selected row changes. The status lines count in glyphs, in sentences of their own ("42 glyphs that were too tall for a 120% line were made compact."), and the log's line on switching names the box first, with how many glyphs it changes ("*Compact diacritics to fit the line* off: diacritics keep their own gaps and size, and 9 glyphs are too tall for a 120% line (their rows say by how much)."). The box is unticked at first, and the choice is remembered in *%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\glyph_compose.json*, under a key of its own: a choice saved under the earlier key, when the box was ticked at first, is not taken for this one.

Unticked, every diacritic keeps its own gap and its full size, a glyph too tall says by how much ("A + dieresis + macron (62 units too tall for a 120% line)") and stays ticked, and the status lines count those ("42 too tall for a 120% line as placed") in place of the compacted ones: the compacting is yours to do. Switching it either way changes the glyphs still placed as suggested. A glyph whose diacritics you moved, scaled, lined up, or took from a related letter keeps what you set, either way: the hand's changes are measured from the suggestion it was placed under, so that glyph keeps that suggestion until its diacritics are put back (*Reset diacritics*, *Restore suggestions*), and then follows the box again. Its row speaks of what you set, not of the suggestion under it: whether it fits, and by how much it does not ("62 units too tall for a 120% line; diacritics placed by hand"). Its tick follows what you set too: the suggestion under it, too tall even compact, does not untick it; what you set, too tall while the box is ticked, unticks it, its row saying by how much. The notes follow their counts: "gap closed" and "diacritic at 85%" for a glyph with one diacritic, "diacritic placed by hand" for one moved. Ticks follow the suggestion too (a glyph too tall even compact is unticked), but for the ticks you changed yourself. The list stays where it is scrolled, its sections open or closed as you left them. Nothing changes in the font until you build: a glyph the font has (*Also list existing glyphs*) is only compared with the other suggestion, and is rebuilt as the list shows it only if you tick it.

### Placing diacritics by hand

The reference, the editor, and *Same diacritic* show only what there is to show: nothing until a row is selected, the editor and its buttons only for a glyph it can draw (a row that needs a mark the font lacks shows its letter in the reference and what it needs), and the strip only when it has letters. With no font open, the choices over the list (*Character set:*, *Also list existing glyphs*) are grayed, and the search field while the list is empty; *Compact diacritics to fit the line* shows with the preview, while a row is selected; the buttons are grayed whenever they would do nothing. The *Mark anchors* tab does the same: *Script:* shows only with a script to name, and *Write the feature code* (the compile tick of *Add ticked anchors*, beside it in the bottom row) is grayed without a list to write, and with several masters.

Under the list, the selected glyph is drawn large between its letter and a letter the font has with the same base, with the baseline and, as faint dashed lines named at one end, the font's lines, as Font Check's *Vertical metrics* reads them. They are of two kinds:

- **Limits**, past which something real happens, each saying what in parentheses: *winAscent* and *winDescent* ("winAscent 1160 (Windows clips above)"), the hhea ascender and descender ("hhea descender -288 (macOS clips below)": macOS lays lines out by them, and applications there may clip past them), and the room a 120% line leaves ("120% line 1080 (meets the line above)"). A limit a diacritic crosses is drawn in amber.
- **Reference lines**: the font's ascender and descender and the typo ascender and descender. They are drawn and never tinted: every accented capital rises over the ascender, and a diacritic below comes under the descender, with nothing clipped and no line met.

Lines at the same height share one line and one label ("typo and font ascender 880"); where a reference line and a limit coincide, the line is the limit's ("hhea descender, winDescent -288 (Windows and macOS clip below)"). A value the font does not set draws none. No text is drawn over another, at any size and with any font's lines: the captions under the glyphs (*Current*, *Suggested*, with how far the suggestion sits from the font's own, "Suggested (11 right, 46 down)") have a band of their own under the lines' names, and stay on one line: where the two are too wide for their glyphs, *Suggested* keeps its place beside *Current* and its detail in parentheses goes on a second row under it, the two moved apart only as far as keeps them clear (a caption goes a row down only in an editor too narrow for the two side by side), and a line's name goes over the line (above the baseline) or under it, at its left end, at its right end where a name nearby takes the left, else as little off its line as it takes. The row's notes about a glyph too tall stay as they are. The *Mark anchors* tab's editor draws the same lines.

The glyph is drawn as a renderer would fill it with its components kept: one outline, each component transformed as it would be written, filled by nonzero winding. A hole shown there (a mirrored component over another part) is a hole the font would have. The rows' pictures are drawn the same way.

- Click a diacritic to select it (it is drawn in the highlight color, in a frame with handles). Drag it, or press the arrow keys (Shift: 10 units), to move it; Shift while dragging keeps it on one axis. While dragged, a diacritic above or below its letter snaps, within 5 pixels, its center to the letter's: the center of its ink's box, and, where it differs (*a*, *r*, *G*), the center of its topmost ink (lowest, for a diacritic below); a thin line, named, shows the center while it holds. Alt held drags it freely.
- A drag ends when the button is let go, wherever the pointer is then, outside the editor or the window too; the picture keeps its scale while a diacritic is dragged and is fitted again afterward. A diacritic can be moved up to 16,000 units either way, and scaled between 10% and 1,000%: TrueType keeps a component's offset and every coordinate in 16 bits (−32,768 to 32,767), and a diacritic anywhere in the largest em OpenType allows (16,384 units), moved that far, still lands on coordinates the format can hold. A move within that stays exactly as you made it. The line under the editor says how far, in whole units ("moved 1,250 right, 40 up"). The picture opens to hold a moved diacritic up to an em past its lines; a diacritic moved farther is outside it: an arrow at the nearest edge points to it (a click on the arrow selects the diacritic), the line says so ("outside the view"), and Backspace or *Reset diacritic* brings it back.
- Double-click the glyph (or its row) to open it in a Glyph window; one not built yet opens its letter. A double-click beside the glyphs does nothing.
- Double-click the letter with diacritics drawn after the glyph (*ò* beside a new *ớ*) to go to its row and see how its own diacritics sit. It is a glyph the font has: its row is listed, unticked, while *Also list existing glyphs* stays off, and *Tick all* leaves it alone (the log says so). It is listed only while you look at it: once you go on (another letter selected or clicked, a search, a tick changed), it leaves the list again, unless you ticked it. Tick the box to rebuild the font's own glyphs.
- A diacritic moved or scaled ticks its row, so that *Build ticked glyphs* builds it (a click that moves nothing leaves the tick as it was).
- Drag a corner of the frame to scale the diacritic both ways (Shift: in proportion), or a side to scale it one way; the opposite corner or side stays put.
- Tab selects the next diacritic, Backspace puts the selected one back, Escape deselects; *Reset diacritics* (*Reset diacritic* for a glyph with one) puts all of the glyph's diacritics back.
- *Apply to related letters* gives the diacritics moved or scaled by hand on the selected letter to the related ones: the same diacritic, on the same side and at the same place in its stack, over letters of the same case (a circumflex raised on C: every capital with a circumflex). Raised and scaled as much; across, a diacritic the hand centered on its letter (snapped to a center) is centered on each letter, on the same center, and otherwise its offset from its letter's center goes over in units, not the hand's move: centered stays centered on a wider letter (*ǽ* after *ó*), each letter keeping where the fonts set diacritics on it (an acute right of *ĺ*'s stem). A stacked diacritic, a diacritic beside its letter, and an ogonek take the hand's move as it is. The button is grayed once applied, until the diacritic is moved again. It stays clickable while a related letter holds a placement made by hand for a diacritic that is back at the suggestion here: after *Reset diacritic* on a letter whose bad placement went to all, *Apply to related letters* puts theirs back too, and the log says so. Each letter's diacritic is then measured against its letter and its other diacritics, outlines and all: one that overlaps or comes within 1.2% of the em is named in the log and in its row, to look at; *Reset diacritics* on its row puts it back. A diacritic drawn joined to its letter (an ogonek, a true cedilla, a horn) is judged the other way: touching its letter is how it should sit, and it is named when it does not touch it. The vertical caron stands beside its letter, like any other diacritic. A placement that leaves a diacritic far off makes the log's line a warning, not a success: a diacritic outside the view (over an em past the font's lines), past a line where Windows or macOS clips (winAscent, winDescent, the hhea values), or more than 250 units (per 1000) from its letter and its other diacritics. A line under it names the letters for each reason, and their rows say it too ("a diacritic past winAscent 1143 (Windows clips above)"). *Line up diacritics* warns the same way.
- *Line up diacritics above* (or *below*) sets every diacritic of the selected one's group at its height: the bottom of each diacritic above (the top of each diacritic below) level with the selected diacritic's. A group is the same side, the same case, and letters of the same height (ink tops within 6% of the em for diacritics above, bottoms for diacritics below), so *h*'s circumflex is not set as low as *e*'s. Stacked diacritics, conventions, and taller or shorter letters are left as they are, each reason named in the log with its letters (click one to select it). The moved diacritics count as moved by hand. Diacritics joined to their letter line up with their own kind only, by the edge fonts keep level (measured on the corpus's 140 Latin fonts): ogoneks by their bottoms (level across *A*, *E*, *I*, *U* in half the fonts exactly, three in four within 6/1000 of the em), true cedillas (*Ç*, *Ş*, *Ţ*: within 5) by their bottoms, horns (*Ơ*, *Ư*) and vertical carons (*ď*, *ľ*, *ť*; the capital *Ľ*'s sits 37/1000 lower, its own group by case) by their tops. Select one and the button reads *Line up ogoneks* (cedillas, horns, vertical carons).
- Under the list, *Same diacritic* shows the letters with one of the selected glyph's diacritics on the same side, both cases: click one to select it and see how the diacritic sits on it.
- *Also list existing glyphs* adds the letters with diacritics the font already has (Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic) in two sections of their own, unticked, each drawn beside its glyph as the font has it (*Current*, *Suggested*): *Existing glyphs with suggestions (tick to rebuild)*, where the suggestion places a diacritic otherwise than the font, and *Existing glyphs that match suggestions (tick to rebuild)*. Placements applied to related letters and lined-up diacritics reach them, so a font can be made consistent; only the ticked ones are rebuilt, their outlines in each master replaced by the letter and marks as components. A rebuilt glyph keeps its own advance width where that is wider than its letter's (the room *ď*, *ľ*, and *ť* often keep for the vertical caron). Once built, it is listed unticked again, so a later build does not rebuild it over edits made since. Placement already follows the font's own accented glyphs where it has them.
- The glyphs just built stay in the list, unticked, in a section of their own (*Built in this window (tick to rebuild)*), with *Also list existing glyphs* off too, so that what is set right after a build reaches them (*Correct mirrored components*, a placement applied to related letters, diacritics lined up). An undo of the build lists them to be built again, ticked as the plan ticks them.
- The changes stay with the glyph as you go down the list, its row's picture follows, and they are built. A diacritic moved by hand moves by as much in every master, scaled about its own center.

*Restore suggestions* puts every tick and every diacritic moved by hand back as the list first came.

### Reference

Left of the editor, the selected glyph's character is drawn in another font, to compare. At first that is *FreeLang*, the font FreeLab bundles for Font Check's lists (Noto subsets, with their mark features; it has 844 of the 846 letters with diacritics of the *All* set): Diacritics registers it with Qt through Font Check's own function, so it is there without Font Check having been opened. It is the first entry of the list, *FreeLang (default)*, followed by the fonts you picked last (up to five, latest first), above a line that sets them apart from the installed fonts (among which the default is not listed a second time), as applications set recently used fonts over the others: the usual choices are at the top, where the list opens, not among the couple of thousand fonts Windows and FontLab install. Each font's name is written in that font, the default's too (a font with no Latin letters, a symbol font, keeps the dialog's font: its name would not read in its own). Pick another font and the choice is remembered (*%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\glyph_compose.json*), and it joins the top of the list; pick the first entry again and the pick is taken back. The family's name is read from Font Check, never written in Glyph Compose. A pick saved by an earlier version is kept while its font is installed; one whose font is gone since (a family renamed, as FreeLang's was, or a font removed) is neither listed nor chosen: the default is used, the pick is forgotten, and the log says so once. The *Mark anchors* tab has the same list, and a pick on either tab is the choice of both. Where the bundled font cannot be registered, the reference starts on the installed font, among families known for wide coverage (Noto Sans, Gentium Plus, Charis SIL, DejaVu Sans, Segoe UI, Arial, and others), with the most of the listed characters. A font that lacks the character (as QRawFont reads the font itself: Windows would otherwise draw another font's glyph under its name; a font QRawFont cannot read counts as lacking it) gives way to the font picked before it, or to the next of those families, and the note says so ("Fette Mikado lacks ĭ: shown in Noto Sans.").

The reference is drawn whole: it keeps its size and its baseline where it fits, is moved down or up only as far as its ink needs, and is made smaller only when it is taller or wider than the picture, so a font whose ink rises high or reaches low (a stacked capital, a tall mark, long descenders) is never cut at its edge. FreeLang itself stays within its own lines: its *ÿ* runs from −240 to 730 against a winAscent of 1,143 and a winDescent of 472 (a test checks every Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic letter of FreeLang Essential so).

The reference is filled as text is, by nonzero winding, so a mark over a letter or over another mark shows no hole the font does not have.

### The list, the log, and the History

- Double-click a row (or select it and click *Show in Glyph window*) to open its glyph between its script's control glyphs (H, n, and o for Latin) in a Glyph window; a glyph not built yet opens its letter. The rows that cannot be built are selectable too: their character is drawn in the reference font.
- *Find a glyph*, over the list, shows only the rows whose glyph name, or the name of a glyph it is built from, holds what is typed (A to Z in any case), whose character it is, or whose Unicode value it is (*U+0105*, *0105*, *105*), as it is typed, with the count beside it ("12 of 341 glyphs shown"; the field's placeholder reads *Find a glyph…*). The count takes its text's width only where the row has room, never a least width of its own, so it never makes the window wider. A character typed alone is that glyph: its row, its forms (*h.sc*), and the letters with diacritics built on it (*h* finds *ĥ* and *ḣ*; a letter with a mark finds those built on it, *ü* finds *ǖ* and *ê* finds *ế*), not every name that holds the letter. Notes are never searched. Rows not shown keep their ticks, and *Build ticked glyphs* builds every ticked glyph, shown or not: while a search hides ticked rows, the count beside the field says so ("3 of 341 glyphs shown (5 ticked among those hidden)."). The *Mark anchors* tab has the same field over its list (name, character, Unicode value; never its anchors or notes).
- *Tick all* and *Untick all* act on the rows shown, and the log says how many rows each click changed and how many are ticked in all, those a search hides included and said so ("Ticked 30 glyphs (42 ticked in all, 12 of them not shown)", "Unticked 5 glyphs (1 still ticked, not shown: a search hides it)", "*Untick all* changed nothing"). *List only high-value glyphs*, beside *Also list existing glyphs*, shows only the glyphs of that set (described next); it is grayed until the set is read, and the rows it hides keep their ticks, as a search's do. *Tick high-value glyphs* ticks, among the rows shown, the glyphs Font Check lists under *High-value missing glyphs* for this font (what the languages already within reach miss), and unticks the others: gaps are closed without new ones being opened. The set comes from Font Check's own functions, with its window's settings where it is open and the settings it starts with otherwise; it is read once the list is shown, and again after a build. The button is grayed while it is read, when Font Check's data cannot be read (the log says why), and when no row shown is in the set or the checks are so already.
- Glyphs handed over by Font Check (*From Font Check* in *Character set:*) are each accounted for: a glyph is a row, or the status lines and the log say how many are not listed and why, each named (the font has it already, which is the case once it is built; its script has no letters in the font; it is not built from a letter and marks; it is not one character). In the log, a glyph the font has is a link. A glyph built in this window is said to be built here, not to be in the font already (a combining mark has no row once the font has it); a combining mark named there stands on a dotted circle. Once a build leaves nothing of the hand-over to build, nothing failed, and the lists still show what was handed over (*From Font Check* on each tab that was handed glyphs), Font Check opens again in the window's place, reading the font afresh: the glyphs built are no longer missing there. Where something is left or failed, the window stays, its log saying what.
- The *Built from* cells and the notes of both lists are written as short notes: no period, and data in parentheses ("A + gravecomb (suggested: 25 right, 4 up)", "A + dieresis + macron (gaps closed to fit a 120% line)"). A test walks every row a made-up font of some ninety glyphs produces, in both lists, and fails on a cell that ends with a period or holds a dash or an interpunct.
- *Correct mirrored components* sets right the mirrored components of the font's glyphs listed (under *Also list existing glyphs*, and those just built; those shown), on a click, recorded in FontLab's History as a build is (below; see *Limits* for what a mirrored component does). A component cannot be reversed where it stands: its contours are its glyph's. So the mark is mirrored into a glyph of its own, with its contours reversed, named after it (*caron.flipped* for a mark turned upside down, *comma.mirrored* for one mirrored left to right; a number is added where that name is another glyph's, and a numbered glyph an earlier run made is used again when it still matches), made once, in every master, and used by every letter that needs it; each letter's component then points to it with what is left of its transform, which mirrors nothing. The letters stay glyphs of components and look as they did. (Decomposing the mark into each letter would fill the hole too, but would leave as many copies to keep alike as there are letters, and no composites: so the mark is not decomposed.) The corrected glyph is a copy: redrawing the mark later does not redraw it. Everything is read back: the corrected glyph's nodes against the mirror of the mark's, each contour's direction against the mark's, the letter's picture against what it was; where anything differs, the glyph is left as it was, the log says why, and the note stays in its row. A letter is set right in every master or in none: every master is planned first, and when one cannot be done (the mark has no outline there, or its read-back differs), the masters already written are put back, so the masters keep matching. A *Build* follows the same rule for a mirrored part, and takes out the corrected glyphs it made that no built glyph ended up using. A mark that is itself made of components is left (decompose it first), and only the current master is looked at to tell which glyphs have mirrored components.
- *Check kerning of these glyphs* opens Kerning Check on the pairs with the glyphs built since the dialog opened (their marks may collide with their neighbors').
- The log under the editor says what each build did: done in green, warnings in amber, errors in red. A letter named in it to look at is a link: a click selects its row. A glyph the list does not have (the font has it: built in an earlier session, or there all along) is listed on its own, unticked, without switching *Also list existing glyphs* on, and selected there, until you go on; or, where that would not list it (another character set chosen since), opened in a Glyph window.
- A build is recorded in FontLab's History, the font's list of glyphs included: *Edit > Undo* takes the new glyphs back, in one step for most builds. A build that adds glyphs others of it are made of (a corrected glyph for a mirrored part, *R.mirrored*; a dotless *i* made of the font's *i*; on *Glyph generation*, a glyph built first for another, *‘* for *“*) is two steps: those glyphs first, in a step of their own, recorded just before the first glyph made of them is written, then the rest; the log says so. In one step, FontLab's *Undo* takes the parts out before it puts back the glyphs made of them, and reports a missing component; in two, the first *Undo* takes the glyphs back, the second the parts. A build refused before anything is written records no step, the parts' neither (they are taken out again). The dialog reads the font again after an undo, a redo, or an edit made in FontLab, keeping its ticks (but for the glyphs built or rebuilt, which come back unticked, and those an undo took back, which are ticked to be built again) and the marks moved by hand. Every glyph of the font a write records in its step (the letters given anchors, the glyphs rebuilt or given a code point) is recorded before the write too, where it has no state of its own yet, so that the write's step has something to put it back to. Its own writes (*Build ticked glyphs*, *Correct mirrored components*, *Add ticked anchors*) are not taken for one: the History watch holds while it writes, and the step a write makes is its own, even when FontLab records it a moment later. A build every glyph of which is refused makes no step either (the *(before)* step, recorded before anything is written, may stay: it changes nothing). A write that finds nothing to write (every ticked glyph's anchors in every master already, the mark code as the font has it) makes no step and says so (*Nothing was added*). Another font is read afresh, nothing carried over: the window closes with its font, and a window opened with no font open takes the next font you open.
- New glyphs are added at the end of the font, and FontLab's Font window shows them at once.

On the owner's test font (no anchors; 106 composites, most of them with marks placed by hand), 269 glyphs can be built, 93 of them with a mark below borrowed from above and 12 with a mark drafted from punctuation, and 59 need a mark the font lacks.

### Limits

- Ligature-like letters (*ǆ*, *ĳ*) and letters with no decomposition (*ŧ*, *ø*) are not built: they are drawn.
- A dotless *i* or *j* is made only where the font's letter can be taken apart for sure (see *What it builds*). A dot drawn as a component, an *i* of three contours or more, masters that differ, or a dot that cannot be told from its stem are not taken apart: the dot stays under the diacritic, and the rows and the log say so. An *i* whose dot is joined to its stem is one contour, and is taken for an *i* drawn without a dot: nothing can tell the two apart, so look at the rows that say *on i itself*. The dotless glyph made is the letter's other contours copied, not redrawn. Made live in FontLab on 2026-11-24, on a font of 103 glyphs without either dotless letter: *dotlessi* and *dotlessj* came out as the stem alone at the letter's width, with their code points, *ì*, *í*, and *ĵ* were built on them as components, and two steps of *Edit > Undo* took the three back, then the two dotless glyphs. Not yet tried live: a font of several masters, and a dot in a shape of its own.
- Marks are made smaller, not redrawn: a stack that needs flatter marks (as fonts draw for Vietnamese capitals) is better drawn by hand.
- A mark that always attaches (cedilla, ogonek) attaches as the corpus's do; a drafted cedilla (a comma) is only a placeholder.
- A mark that joins its letter (a horn, an ogonek, a cedilla) overlaps it, as two components, and Diacritics does not merge them: that is the export's matter. FontLab 7's export profiles have *Remove Overlaps*, which its manual says is needed for the non-variable final formats and not for variable ones. An OpenType PS font has no components (its glyphs are exported as outlines), so that option merges the mark and its letter. An OpenType TT font keeps them as components by the profile's *TTF: use composites*: components kept are not merged, TrueType allows them to overlap, and rasterizers fill the overlap as long as both are wound the same way, though text set outlined or stroked shows where they cross; decomposing the composites lets them be merged. A variable font keeps its overlaps, and the `glyf` flags `OVERLAP_SIMPLE` and `OVERLAP_COMPOUND` tell the rasterizers that need telling (Apple's) to fill them: check the exported font for them.
- Overlapping parts fill only while they are wound the same way. A mirrored component (scaled by a negative number one way, not both) has its contours reversed, and where it overlaps another part a renderer filling by nonzero winding leaves a hole. Diacritics writes none. What it turns upside down, it turns by a half turn. Where a mirror would still come from the font (a glyph a mark is drafted from that is itself a single mirrored component: a `^` made of a flipped caron, which the build replaces by the glyph it points to, the two transforms combined), the build puts the mark's corrected glyph in its place, mirrored with its contours reversed, as *Correct mirrored components* does (see *The list, the log, and the History*), and the log says which glyph was added; only where that glyph cannot be made is the mirrored component written, and named in the log with what to do. A glyph the font has whose own components are mirrored is named in its row when *Also list existing glyphs* is on, and *Correct mirrored components* sets it right when you click it; a glyph you leave keeps its note.

  When the hole shows was measured, on 2026-11-13, on small fonts built by script (fontTools 4.65): a square base, a bar-shaped mark laid over its top edge, as a composite glyph and as one glyph of outlines (contours copied in, overlaps kept), in TrueType and (outlines only, CFF having no components) in CFF. Each was rendered large by FreeType 2.14.3 (through PIL), drawn through HarfBuzz 11.2.0's outlines, and drawn by Windows GDI into a bitmap, and the overlap looked at:

  | The mark | TrueType, a component | TrueType, outlines | CFF, outlines |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | as it is | filled | filled | filled |
  | mirrored one way (across or up) | **hole** | **hole** | **hole** |
  | turned by a half turn | filled | filled | filled |
  | mirrored, its contours reversed | filled | filled | filled |

  FreeType, HarfBuzz, and GDI (antialiased or ClearType) agree on every cell. So the hole shows exactly where two overlapping contours run opposite ways, in either format, and none of the three sets a mirrored component's direction right by itself: a composite shows the hole just as its outlines do. One more case stands apart: GDI without antialiasing draws a CFF font with a hole at every overlap, whichever way its contours run (all four rows), which no direction can mend; in a CFF font the overlaps have to be removed. The FreeType part is kept as a test (`HoleRenderingTests`, run where fontTools and PIL are installed); HarfBuzz and GDI were run once, by the same script with two more renderers.

  What FontLab does at export was not measured: FontLab cannot be run from the tests. The owner's report is that fonts exported from FontLab showed these holes and that *Remove Overlaps* mended them. That agrees with the table: once the overlap is removed, no two contours are left to run opposite ways over each other, whichever way they ran. It does not tell whether FontLab keeps a mirrored component as a component in a TrueType export (kept components are not merged, as the item above says) or decomposes it, nor whether it sets its direction right when it does; so a font with mirrored components is best corrected in the font, and an exported font looked at where a mark meets its letter.
- The corpus model is measured on Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic composites (250 families); a Greek capital's left-hand marks are left out of it, as they are not above it.

### Mark anchors

Scripts whose marks are not precomposed (Khmer, Thai, Lao, Myanmar, Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, Bengali, Gujarati, Tamil, and the combining marks of any script) are set by the font's mark features: each mark's anchor meets its base's. The *Mark anchors* tab places them, for the script you pick among those the font has marks of:

- **Bases** (the script's letters that take marks, and their unencoded forms, *beh-ar.init* or *i.2*, by the name before the period) get *top* and *bottom* anchors, every one of them (a combining mark goes on letters Unicode has no precomposed form for: a dot below *p* or *x*); in Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic, the side anchors only where their precomposed letters need them (*a*: *ogonek*; *o* and *u*: *horn* too), and the list sorts the letters by that (*Marks above and below*, *With side marks too*). Marks stack on each other by the marks' own anchors, so a letter whose precomposed forms stack marks (Vietnamese's *ế*) takes no other anchors and has no section of its own. Which letters take marks: in Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic, those Unicode composes letters with marks on (*a*, *w*, *ø*, *ł*, the dotless *ı*), not those it has precomposed already (*ẅ*) nor those it composes nothing on (*ª*, *ƒ*); in every script, those at least half the corpus's fonts having them attach marks to (Thai's 46 consonants, not its vowels). A script the corpus says nothing of (Myanmar) takes every letter. The status lines count the letters left out; **marks** (Unicode's nonspacing marks) get the anchor they attach by (*_top* or *_bottom*, by where their ink sits: above or below half the x-height) and the one the next mark attaches to (*top* or *bottom*), for mark-to-mark. Anchors a glyph has already are kept; its row names those the marks attach by (*top*, *bottom*, a font's own *above* where its marks have *_above*), not the others it may carry, which say nothing of where marks go. A mark attached by an anchor of its own (a horn's *_topright*, an ogonek's *_bottom*) takes no other: in two mark classes, fontTools' compiler would refuse it.
- **Hebrew's points** attach where most of the corpus's Hebrew fonts attach them (27 of its 30 Hebrew families attach their points by anchors): the dagesh (and mapiq) by *_center*, inside the letter, every base getting a *center* anchor where the fonts land the dagesh in that letter (in its counter: 31% across and halfway up, as the median of its letters); the holam and the sin dot by *_topleft*, at the letter's top left; the shin dot by *_topright*, which shin alone gets, at its right head. Of the cantillation marks (10 families have them, and all 10 attach them), pashta and telisha qetana go at the top left too, yetiv and dehi at the bottom right (*_bottomright*), the others over or under the letter like the vowel points. Nothing stacks on these marks, so they get no anchor of their own for the next mark. Letters get these anchors only when the font has marks that use them.
- **Ligatures** (Arabic's *lam with alef* and the like: an encoded presentation form such as U+FEFB, a *uniXXXXYYYY* name, or a name of joined letters, *lam_alef-ar*) get *top_1*, *bottom_1*, *top_2*, *bottom_2* (a pair per letter they join; the first letter, read first, is on the right), and the code attaches marks to each part (`pos ligature`). Two-part ligatures take their parts' anchors where the corpus's fonts set theirs: 72% and 22% across, from 19 fonts' mark-to-ligature anchors; longer ones are split evenly.
- **The reph** (the ra over the consonant it comes before, in Devanagari, Bengali, and Gujarati: *rephdeva*, *Reph.dv*, *dvReph*, *Reph.bn*, *bn_reph*, *gjReph*, *rephgujr*, *reph-gujarati*, a ra and virama by their code points such as *uni09B009CD*, and the like, not Marathi's eyelash ra; a reph whose name names another script is left to that script) gets *_reph*, every base a *reph* anchor where the corpus's fonts of that script land it, level with its top: Devanagari's 78% across the consonant (32 fonts), Bengali's 89% (11), Gujarati's 84% (11).
- **Where**: a base's anchor where the corpus's fonts land a mark on that very letter, shaped through HarfBuzz with all their means at once (anchors, forms they substitute, contextual positioning), from at least 3 fonts: on Thai's *po pla*, *fo fa*, and *fo fan*, a mark above sits left of the ascender, not over it. A letter the corpus has too little of takes its script's rule: over (under) the center of its ink's top (bottom) fifth. Latin takes the letter's placement across only, and its script's rule for the height, which landed marks closer. A mark's anchor sits under (over) the center of its ink. Both by how the corpus's fonts of that script place theirs: *compose/train/anchors.py* reads the *mark*, *abvm*, and *blwm* features of 260 open-licensed fonts (Noto Sans and Serif Khmer, Thai, Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, Bengali, Gujarati, Tamil, Battambang, Hanuman, Sarabun, Kanit, Amiri, Cairo, Frank Ruhl Libre, Heebo, Hind and its Bengali, Gujarati, and Tamil kin, Mukta, Poppins, and others) into *compose/anchor_model.json*. Thai and Devanagari set their marks right of center (Thai's top anchor at 43% of the base's width right of center, its marks' at their right edge), and Bengali and Gujarati too; Arabic 0.10 em above the ink, Khmer 0.07 em. A letter's placement is taken from the fonts attaching that script's marks only: a font that leaves them where they are drawn says nothing of where they go. Where a script's marks are set off their center (Thai's), a base's anchor is set so that the mark sampled (Thai's *mai ek*) lands where the fonts land it, and the others sit as their own anchors say.
- **Reference**: beside it, the selected glyph's letter carrying the sample marks at once, one below and one above, and, where marks stack on it (a letter whose precomposed forms stack them, or any letter of a script that composes none, such as Thai), a second mark on the one above (on *e*, for instance, a dot below, a circumflex, and an acute), then the letter with each side mark; a mark is shown on the sample letter, alone and with another mark on it. All in the reference font (the tab has the first tab's list of fonts, one choice for both), as that font's own features place them, falling back as the first tab's does, then to fonts of that script (Noto Sans Thai, Noto Serif Hebrew, Noto Sans Bengali, Hind Vadodara, Leelawadee UI, Nirmala UI, and so on). An example is drawn only as the font can draw it: a letter and marks Unicode has one character for is drawn as that character (*Ấ* for *A* with a circumflex and an acute); what stays a letter with marks is kept when the font sets the marks on the letter, which Diacritics tells from what Qt would draw (the example's ink against its letter's alone, and its advance against the letter's: FontLab has no shaper to ask); else it is tried with one mark fewer, and left out when the font sets not even one. No mark is ever shown standing beside its letter, and the line under the picture says what was left off. A mark glyph drawn on the other side than its code point's (Bellefair's *uni0326.salt*, a comma above, named for U+0326, the comma below) is drawn by its twin of that side (the turned comma above, U+0312; a circumflex below for a circumflex, and so on), or not at all where it has none, the line saying so. The reference draws the character's edges too, dashed, as the font's own preview does, so a space or a glyph of no ink shows its width (owner's image 353).
- **By hand**: the selected glyph is drawn with a mark attached; drag the mark, or press the arrow keys (Shift: 10 units), to move the glyph's anchor (a base's where the mark should sit, a mark's own so that it sits as shown). A mark is drawn with another mark on it too: drag that one to move the anchor the next mark attaches to (mark to mark). A letter where marks stack is drawn with a second mark on its mark above, which follows the first.
- **The sample marks**: a letter is drawn with marks a reader knows, the first the font has of each: a circumflex above (an acute on it, where marks stack), a dot below (else a macron below, a comma below, a ring below, and so on), and, where the letter takes them, the ogonek and the horn, each at its anchor; in other scripts, the script's sample mark (Thai's *mai ek*), else the first mark of that side. A mark is drawn on a letter: on its anchor of the mark's own side, else on a letter's anchor the mark attaches by in the font (Noto Sans's comma below by its *_m.uni0326*), else hung where its own anchor of its side would be; on a dotted circle only where the font has no letter to put it on. A mark the font attaches by anchors of other names than *top* or *bottom* (FontLab names those it reads from a font's mark features after their classes: *_t.cyrillicbreve*, *_m.uni0326*) is hung where its *_top* (*_bottom*, *_ogonek*, *_horn*) would be: shown, not dragged, as it has no such anchor to move. Marks over or under two letters (U+035C to U+0362, U+1DCD, U+1DFC: the double breves, tildes, and macrons) are no letter's marks: they are neither listed nor drawn as a sample, and they place no anchor. The sample is otherwise the font's own mark, so it differs from font to font: the representative one where the font has it, else the script's sample, else the font's first of that side.
- **Moving anchors**: drag a sample mark (or press the arrow keys) to move the anchor it hangs on, one to add or one the glyph has already (after adding, for instance); *Reset anchors* puts the selected glyph's back, in the list and the picture. A moved anchor of the glyph's own is written as a move. Ticks and moves stay when the list is read again (another script, after writing), for the same font only; a move once written leaves the list (the font has it), and one an error stopped stays. A mark attached by an anchor of its own of another side (an ogonek kept on its *_bottom*) is drawn hanging on that anchor.
- **The code**: *Add ticked anchors* adds them (and moves the glyph's own, where moved by hand) in every master, each measured on its own; a glyph with anchors to add only in another master (Bold lacking what Regular has) is listed ticked, its row saying so, and written there, the current master left as it is; and, for a font with one master, writes the *mark* and *mkmk* features between FreeLab's marker lines (`markClass` per mark, `pos base` per base, `pos ligature` per ligature, `pos mark` per mark), and the ticked substitutions (*ccmp*, *pres*), checked by fontTools' feature parser first. With several masters, the code is left to FontLab's automatic features, which read each master's anchors.

A script's anchor heights come from at least 3 fonts of it; fewer (Greek's and Cyrillic's marks below, and Tamil's, which has none below) give way to Latin's. With one script, the *Script:* choice is a plain name.

Tried on fonts of the corpus, their own anchors taken away, the marks land a median 31 units (Noto Sans Thai) to 47 units (Sarabun) across and 27 to 35 up from where the fonts put them; Arabic is roughest (36 across, 117 up in Amiri, whose marks keep heights of their own). The anchors are a start, to adjust by eye.

An empty glyph (a CID-keyed font's: its code point mapped, nothing drawn, as 07LightNovelPOP's À and most of its Latin) is listed to build, *its glyph is in the font, empty: filled when built*, and is never used as a mark, a base, a source drafted from, a dotless i, or a letter to compare with: the next candidate is tried.

## Glyph generation

The *Glyph generation* tab lists the characters of its scope the font lacks, in groups, each row with its glyph drawn as it would be built, its recipe, how sure the recipe is, and a note; ticked rows are built in every master by *Build ticked glyphs*, which *Edit > Undo* takes back (in two steps when some of them are made of others built with them). It works on a bare font: most recipes start from glyphs any Latin font has (the space, the period, the hyphen, the letters), the CJK ones from the font's own kana and ideographs, and a row that needs a glyph the font lacks says which, under *Need a glyph the font lacks*, its text dimmed as on the *Diacritics* tab. Where several rows are made of one glyph that nothing the font has can make, that glyph is listed there to draw ("draw it: ⇐ ⇑ ⇓ are built from it"). Left of the picture, the tab has the *Diacritics* tab's own *Reference* section: the heading, the reference font's chooser (one choice for both tabs), the selected row's character in it, and the caption under it saying which font draws it. On every tab, a vertical line sets the reference apart from the preview at its right, and the reference shows only while a row is selected. *Tick all* and *Untick all* log how many as on the *Diacritics* tab; *Tick high-value glyphs* and *List only high-value glyphs* work as they do there, with the same set from Font Check (not every row is in it: Font Check lists what the languages already within reach lack, such as the no-break spaces, quotes, or guillemets a language uses). *Also list existing glyphs* and the search field stay on a line of their own, under the four widths (the settings of how the glyphs are drawn first, then what is listed and the search, right over the list: owner's image 249): with *List only high-value glyphs* beside them, one line of all of them would need about 1,100 pixels (1,300 with the *List* chooser). The rows keep Qt's own spacing between their controls, with no wider gaps (owner's image 307), and *Vary turned and mirrored glyphs* sits under the preview it changes, as *Compact diacritics to fit the line* does on the *Diacritics* tab, which keeps the widths' line narrower than the window opens. The widths' line is as tall as the *Diacritics* tab's own tab bar and the line over the list as tall as that tab's, so the list starts at the same height on every tab; the *Mark anchors* line keeps its dropdown's height while the dropdown is hidden. On every tab, a line sets the *Reference* heading apart from the list above it, shown with the heading. The lists draw their characters in FreeLang, Font Check's list font, each in the family of the five that has it, and so does the caption under the reference: the dialog's font falls back to MS Gothic, which lacks some of them (*㏞*, *㏟*, *㏿*) that FreeLang's CJK font has.

A recipe may use a glyph the same list builds: *“* is two *‘*, *‘* the turned *’*. The list builds such glyphs first and the row says so (*made of quoteleft, built here first*), and it is no surer than what it is made of: *ǝ* copies *ə* (17 of the 40 families that build *ǝ* do so), an accepted recipe, but *ə* is *e* turned, a starting point, so *ǝ* says *starting point* too (*made of schwa (starting point), built here first*), or *likely usable as it is* where *ə* is, and is unticked at first. There is no loop: *ə*'s only way is a turned *e*; a ticked row whose glyph of the list is left unticked is not built, and the log says to tick both. Nothing the font has is replaced unless *Also list existing glyphs* is ticked and its row ticked (the row says *replaces the font's own glyph*); an existing duplicate is rebuilt as a component of its source, since its own glyph cannot hand its code point on.

No glyph is ever made a component of itself, directly or through others. *Ð* and *Đ* are each a copy of the other: where *Đ* is already made of *Ð*, *Ð*'s row says *made of Dcroat, which is made of Eth* and cannot be ticked; where both are ticked, the first is rebuilt and the second is left as it was, the log saying why. The build checks every master's components, the font's and those it is about to write, before it writes anything, a mirrored part included: where *R* is made as a mirrored component of *Я*, the mirrored *R* that rebuilds *Я* would cancel into *Я* itself once flattened, so *Я*'s row says *made of R, which is made of Ya-cy*. Each layer's parts are checked once more as they are written (flattened, their mirrors pointed to corrected glyphs), before the layer is cleared. The list's own checks follow the glyphs it builds too: with *Ð* built from *Ɖ*, a rebuilt *Ð* would be a copy of *Ɖ*, itself a copy of the font's *Đ*, made of *Ð*: its row says so when planned, not only at the build. A glyph planned for another and refused there only because its ways go through that one (*↑* from *→* while *→* is planned from *←*) is planned again on its own. Components that cannot be read rule no loop out: the glyph is left as it was, and the row or the log names the glyph and master that could not be read.

A glyph rebuilt in several masters is rebuilt in all of them or in none: where a master's write fails, or FontLab keeps too few of its parts there, every master written or tried is put back from what was saved before the build (the failing one too, where its own put-back failed), each is read back (every shape's transform, a component's glyph, an outline's nodes, contour by contour, with their places and kinds), and the log says *every master left as it was* only when they all read as they were. Should one not go back, the log names it (*Edit > Undo* takes it back).

### Monoline: when a turned or reversed letter is likely usable

A mirror of *R* is a fair *Я* only where mirroring changes nothing a reader sees. In a design with stroke contrast, *N*'s thick diagonal comes out on *И*'s thin side; an *O* whose stress leans left leans right once mirrored; and a serif on one side of a stem moves to the other. A half turn keeps contrast and stress as they are, and only uneven serifs count against it. The tab measures, in each master:

- **Contrast**: *O*'s thicker strokes over its thinner ones, its sides at mid-height against its top and bottom at mid-width (1 for a monoline *O*), else *o*'s. It counts both ways: an *O* whose top and bottom are four times as thick as its sides measures 4, as one with sides four times as thick does (reverse contrast is no monoline either).
- **Stress**: how much *O*'s left and right sides differ a quarter up and a quarter down, as a share of their mean (0 for an upright stress).
- **Serifs**: how unevenly *I*'s foot and head reach out on either side of its stem, as a share of the stem (0 for none, or serifs alike), else *l*'s.

The thresholds come from the corpus (*compose/train/generation.py*): for each family, how much of its drawn *Я* and *И* the mirrored *R* and *N* cover alike (intersection over union, its ink centered; 0.9 counts as the same), and of its *ə* a turned *e*, against the three measures. *Likely usable* must be right for at least 70% of the families so measured, and 10 points more often than for any family; within that, the thresholds that find the most. For a mirror: contrast at most 1.10, stress and serifs at most 0.03, right for 74% of 462 letters (where 33% of all are), finding 40% of those drawn as the recipe makes them (the thresholds came out the same once contrast counted both ways; 11 of the 658 measures had read under 1). For a half turn, contrast hardly matters (it keeps contrast as it is): contrast at most 2.0 and serifs at most 0.1, right for 85% of 196 families (75% of all), finding 74%. Stems' terminals are not measured. Rows so labeled stay unticked, as every starting point does: the label says where to look first, not that nobody needs to.

### How each is made

- **Width**: an empty glyph of a set advance in each master.
- **Second code point**: the code point added to the source glyph's own (`fgGlyph.unicodes`), FontLab's lookups rebuilt (`rebuildMaps()`), and read back; where FontLab does not keep it, the code point is taken back and the log says so. The glyph is recorded with the font before the write, so that *Undo* takes the code point back (recorded with the font alone, *Undo* moves the History and leaves the code point).
- **Component**, **move**: one component, as is or raised (the middle dot's center at 62% of the x-height, the corpus's median over 495 families; the research's "middle of the x-height" is lower than most fonts set it).
- **Turn**: a component turned by any angle about the ink's center (a turned letter's about the middle of its body: the table says whether that is the cap height or the x-height, as Unicode's data has it when the table is made; FontLab's Python 2.7 has Unicode 5.2, where *Ʞ* and *Ʇ* are not capitals yet, and the notes name characters as the table does for the same reason). A half turn keeps the advance and swaps the sidebearings (as the corpus's turned quotes: 517 families, no shift); *¡* and *¿* are then set with their top at the x-height, 0.01 em above it (494 families, quartiles 0 and 0.03 em: a much tighter rule than a fixed drop of the turned mark, whose quartiles span 0.06 em). A quarter or eighth turn takes the source's sidebearings times the corpus's factor for that kind (1.45 for vertical arrows, 2.06 for diagonal ones, 0.78 for triangles). Quotes are turned, never mirrored.
- **Mirror**: left to right. A glyph of outlines is mirrored into the new glyph with its contours set right (the *Diacritics* tab's corrected-glyph machinery, `make_corrected`); where the source is made of components, each part points to its corrected glyph (*comma.mirrored*), made once and used by every glyph that needs it, as the *Diacritics* tab does for mirrored marks. A turned component reverses nothing and needs neither.
- **Row**: components side by side, the first part's left sidebearing and the last part's right one kept. Between them, the corpus's gap: the ellipsis's periods and the digraphs' letters stand at their own advances (the gap between their inks is their inner sidebearings, 0.96 and 1.0 times, medians); double quotes stand 0.035 em apart (517 families), the low double quote 0.033 em, guillemets overlap by 0.029 em, *‼* 0.125 em, *⁇* 0.043 em, *⁈* and *⁉* 0.09 em, double primes 0.031 em. *Ŀ* and *ŀ* place their dot as the corpus's composites do (96 and 102 families): its left 0.193 em inside *L*'s advance at 51% of the cap height, 0.019 em inside *l*'s at 68% of the x-height.
- **Stretch**: a dash drawn longer, not scaled: the nodes right of the hyphen's middle move right, so the dash keeps its thickness and its ends (rounded or angled), and its sidebearings stay the hyphen's. The en and em dashes take their width from the two settings (the corpus's medians, 507 families each); the figure dash a tabular figure's; the two- and three-em dashes twice and three times the em dash (the font's own where it has one, its ends kept).
- **Minus**: the hyphen stretched to the plus's ink width and set on the plus's center: the plus's advance and height, the hyphen's thickness (486 families: the minus's ink as wide as the plus's, centered on it).
- **Fraction**: numerator, fraction slash, and denominator, each at its own advance.
- **Square**: the font's own drawn glyphs set in the em square (a full-width glyph's advance): kana and ideographs in rows, Latin in one row scaled to fit, the telegraph symbols in one row condensed at full height.
- **Form**: the font's own drawn superior, inferior, or small capital of the character, as a component, found by Feature Code's suffixes as Feature Code finds a glyph's forms (its `forms_index`: *n.sups*, *h.sc*, the *sc* of *a.sc.ss01*).
- **Script**: one component scaled about the baseline, raised or lowered, centered in a wider advance (*Superscript and subscript signs and letters*); **scaled letters**: components scaled alike, side by side at a gap between their inks (*™ ℠ ℡*); **numero**: *N*, a scaled and raised *o*, and the hyphen drawn under it; **over**: a letter scaled over the fraction slash and another under it, at their advances (*℅ ℆*); **circle**: the font's ring, and the character scaled and centered in it; **cell**: one component scaled across and up and set where the fonts set it in a full-width cell, half of one, or its kana's advance; **small capital**: the capital scaled to the x-height. Every one is made of components with a transform, as the squared characters are, so the glyph follows its sources when they are redrawn; none is redrawn with thicker strokes (Feature Code's *Strokes*, which offsets outlines, has no part here), and the row says *scaling thins its strokes* where a part is made smaller.
- **Bar**: the letter as a component, and the hyphen drawn as long as the bar (its nodes right of its middle moved, as the *Stretch* of a dash moves them), made thinner, and turned for a slash: outlines, added in each master from that master's hyphen.

Every glyph is built in every master, each measured on its own; a master where a source has no outline is left as it was, and the log names it. A component turned, mirrored, or scaled that points to a glyph made of components is replaced by that glyph's own components (FontLab 7 refuses a whole layer otherwise).

### Settings

Over *Also list existing glyphs*: *Thin space: 1/6 em*, *Hair space: 1/11 em*, *En dash: 52.8% of the em*, and *Em dash: 86.2% of the em* (captions in the label form, a colon before the control), kept in *glyph_compose.json* under *generation*. Unicode gives the thin space as 1/5 or 1/6 em and the corpus's median is 0.158 em; the hair space's median is 0.090 em. *Restore suggestions* puts the ticks and the four settings back, and switches *Vary turned and mirrored glyphs* off.

### Vary turned and mirrored glyphs

A turned or mirrored glyph is a carbon copy of its source, which shows in a hand-drawn or quirky font. *Vary turned and mirrored glyphs*, under the preview (shown while a row is selected, as the reference is), with *Strength:* (*Subtle*, *Moderate*, *Bold*) and *Seed:*, changes each glyph built by a turn or a mirror a little: the turned and reversed letters, the turned quotes, *¡* and *¿*, the arrows and triangles. The changes are those Feature Code's *Stylistic alternates* builder makes its variants with (*features/freelab_alternates.py*'s `variant_params` and `variant_matrix`, loaded beside Glyph Compose as that tab loads it, never copied): a slight rotation, uneven scaling, a slant, and a baseline shift, about the glyph's ink, within the strength's reach. Nothing else is varied: a look-alike, a copy, a row, a stretched dash, a width, or a second code point stays as it was.

- **The same seed, the same glyph.** The changes are drawn from the seed and the glyph's name (a CRC, the same on FontLab's Python 2.7 and on Python 3); another seed gives others, and a glyph rebuilt with it (*Also list existing glyphs*, ticked) is replaced.
- **Every master alike.** Each master's glyph is changed by the same numbers about its own ink's center: the change is affine, so every node stays, in its place in its contour, and the masters stay compatible.
- **A glyph of components stays one.** The change goes into its components' transforms (a turned *‘* is still *quoteright*, turned and varied), so it still follows its source when that is redrawn; nothing is decomposed, and its note says so (*varied (seed 7, moderate), still following its source*). A mirrored glyph of outlines is varied once it is made, each master read back node by node, in order and within half a unit (FontLab rounds to whole units); a master FontLab did not change as asked takes the new glyph out again, and the log says so; where it cannot be taken out (read back), the log says which masters are varied and which not, and the glyph stays in the step *Edit > Undo* takes back.
- **Every component read back.** Each master's components are read back after the write, varied or not: their glyphs in order, their whole transforms (the 2x2 part within three steps of TrueType's F2Dot14, 3/16,384, the offsets within a rounding to whole units), and the advance. A master where FontLab kept the components but not their transforms (a varied turn's rotation dropped in Bold) fails the glyph as a master it kept none of does: a new glyph is taken out (read back), a rebuilt one put back in every master tried. A count of shapes alone would take such a master for built.
- **The preview and the rows' pictures** show the glyphs as varied, and change with the strength and the seed. The selected row stays selected as the list is planned again, and its preview is drawn from the new plan, though the list is refilled with its signals held. A varied glyph's preview outlines the glyph unchanged under it, dotted in the selection's color, and the line beside it says what changed ("Varied (bold, seed 1): turned 1.4° counterclockwise, slanted 3.5° right, 95% as wide, 102% as tall, raised 5 units"): the strengths change a glyph by a degree or two, which alone hardly shows.

It is off at first, and the choice, the strength, and the seed are kept in *glyph_compose.json*. It is never switched on by itself: FreeLab has no measure of how hand-drawn or irregular a font is. The monoline measure (contrast, stress, serifs) tells a mirror's or a turn's fit, not a hand: a monoline hand and a monoline geometric sans read alike, and Feature Code's builder measures nothing either (its user picks it for a handwriting font). The no-loop and all-masters rules above hold for varied glyphs as for any: the varied parts point to the same glyphs.

### From Font Check

Font Check's *Build with Glyph Compose* hands over the ticked glyphs that either tab can build: a character that decomposes into a letter and marks goes to the *Diacritics* tab (its *Character set* then reads *From Font Check*), another of Glyph generation's table to that tab, whose *List* chooser reads *From Font Check (N glyphs)* (or *Every group*), those rows alone and ticked. They are planned against the whole table all the same: a glyph handed over that is made of another the font lacks (*‟* is two *‛*) is built with it, and the log says so (*‛ was built too, though not listed: ‟ is made of it.*). Only an exact or accepted recipe is built so: a starting point is listed with the glyphs handed over, unticked, as in the whole table (*ǝ* copies *ə*, a turned *e*: *ə* is listed, and *ǝ* is built once it is ticked too). A glyph built for one that then fails is taken out again, and the log says so (*‛ was built for ‟, which was not built: it was taken out again.*). Whether it is still used is read from the components of the glyphs left in the font, in every master: where one that failed stays made of it, where FontLab does not take it out, or where those components cannot be read, it stays, and the log says which. The window opens on the tab with more, and its log says what the other holds: two tabs, each with its own list and build, are clearer than one list mixing two kinds of recipe. Font Check counts as buildable what the Diacritics tab can build and what Glyph generation can build that is not a draft (a starting point counts only where it is likely usable), and each row's tooltip says which tab builds it.

### The data

*compose/glyph_generation.json* is FreeLab's own recipe table, made by *compose/train/generation.py* from the research inventory of 2026-11-16 (which characters, how sure, the corpus's counts), Unicode's data (names, decompositions), GlyphsInfo's glyph names, and measurements of the corpus (667 open-licensed families: only medians and counts). Each entry holds its character, its group, Glyphs' name for its glyph, its ways in order (the first the font can take is used), and how many of the corpus's families have it and build it of other glyphs; its *rules* are the layout constants above, chosen from the survey's statistics by the script (a row's gap in ems unless the parts' own sidebearings explain it). Nothing of FontLab's *alias.dat* (no license) is read, shipped, or derived: the inventory's quotes of it are never read, and the script refuses to write a table that names FontLab. Where the inventory and the corpus disagree, the corpus wins and the script says so: Unicode's confusables map the Cyrillic *І*, the Greek *Ι*, and the palochka to the Latin *l*, but the corpus's fonts copy the capital *I*. Sources and licenses: *compose/glyph_generation_LICENSE* and [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

```
python compose/train/generation.py <inventory.json> <GlyphsInfo GlyphData.xml> [--corpus <dir>] [--limit N]
python compose/train/generation.py --refresh compose/glyph_generation.json [--records <calibration records>]
```

*--refresh* makes again only what the table takes from Unicode's data (each turned letter's body, the *characters* the notes name) and, given the calibration's records (*--calibration*'s file), the monoline thresholds; the rest stays as it was, and no corpus is read.

The next scope (2026-11-24) is added to the table by *compose/train/generation_next.py*: its characters from Unicode's decompositions (the interpreter's: Unicode 16), its ways, and its *rules* (`scripts`, `signs`, `circled`, `cells`, `smallcaps`, `bars`, and the degree signs' gaps among `rows`), measured on the same 667 families (86 with kana or ideographs). Each family's medians are kept in a records file while the corpus is read in slices (each run under two minutes, through *heavy-run.py*); the last run writes the table, the medians behind the rules under `next_layout`. A superscript's or subscript's ink height against its character's, its baseline where the scaled character's lands, its advance against the scaled one; a sign made of letters cut into its letters by its contours (*№*'s tall ones its *N*, its small ones its *o*, a flat one under them its underline; *℃*'s high ring at its left its degree sign; *℅*'s tallest contour its slash); a circled glyph's ring its contour of the largest box and the largest inside it about the same middle, the rest its character; a cell's ink against its character's, and its middle against its advance; a bar the cells of the barred letter its letter does not cover, laid where it covers the most, in the rows most of them are in (a slash: along their long axis). Only medians and counts leave the fonts. The superscript and subscript signs' numbers are Feature Code's, read from *features/freelab_feature_code.py* (`CORPUS_SCALE`, `CORPUS_WIDTH`, `SUPS_BASELINE`, `SUBS_BASELINE`), so that both tools place them alike.

```
python compose/train/generation_next.py --records <records.json> --start N --count N [--corpus <dir>]
python compose/train/generation_next.py --records <records.json> --write
```

### Proven live, and not yet

Live on 2026-11-16, FontLab 7.2 and Varela Round from the corpus, never saved:

- **The tab**: the window (*FreeLab Glyph Compose (Varela Round)*) with its two tabs; Glyph generation listed 119 glyphs it could build (21 exact, 98 accepted) and 36 that need a glyph the font lacks.
- **Builds**, one row at a time, each read back: the narrow no-break space (empty, 167 units), the Kelvin sign and the ano teleia (second code points on *K* and *periodcentered*; `findUnicode` finds them), the Cyrillic *А* (a component of *A*), the two-dot leader and *⁈* (rows: the exclamation mark 0.095 em after the question mark), the turned comma (a half turn), *⅓* (*one.numr*, *fraction*, *three.dnom*), the two-em dash (outlines: 1,782 units, twice the font's em dash, its round ends kept), *‛*, *⸮*, and *⹁* (outlines mirrored, contours set right), *‟* (made of the *‛* built just before), and *Ǳ*.
- **Undo**: one *Edit > Undo* takes back one build: the ano teleia's code point went (*K*'s stayed), and builds that added glyphs took them out one build at a time.

Two FontLab facts came out of it, both now in the code:

- **A new glyph's layers must be made through `setGlyphComponents`**, with no component when there is none (an empty glyph, a glyph of outlines added after). A new glyph whose only layer was made by `getLayerByName(master, True)` crashed FontLab (0xC0000005 in *FontLab 7.exe* at 0x72b370) when its `fgGlyph` was first read, empty or with an outline added by `addShape`, whether or not the Python `flGlyph` was still held; made through `setGlyphComponents([], width, package, False)` first, both kinds took their code point (six runs; FreeLab MCP's `add_component_glyph`, which makes its layers so, worked in the same session). The *Diacritics* tab's corrected glyphs (`make_corrected`) are made the same way now.
- **`applyTransform` mirrors a layer's advance too**: a mirrored copy of *quoteright* (238 units) read back an advance of −238. `make_corrected` sets the source's advance again after mirroring, and checks it.

Then, from the installed scripts (*Scripts > FreeLab > Glyph Compose*, no Diacritics entry left): on Varela Round, the *Diacritics* tab built *Ǎ* and *ǒ* and one *Undo* took both back, and *Mark anchors* listed 83 bases and 41 marks and drew the selected mark on its letter; on Rubik (three masters, Light, Bold, and Black), one build of a row of each kind (width, copy, stretch, second code point, row, mirror, turn, fraction), every master read back and measured on its own (the figure dash 600 units in Light, 700 in Black), and one *Undo* for all eight; on Bellefair, Font Check's *Build 3 glyphs with Glyph Compose* opened the window on *Diacritics* with the set *From Font Check (3 glyphs)*.

- **A variable font's sparse masters**: Rubik's Bold has no layer for many glyphs (the hyphen, the period, the right single quote). A glyph made of them gets no layer there either, never an empty one (the first build gave three glyphs an empty Bold layer); the log says once per master which glyphs have none, and a layer made for a write FontLab did not keep is taken out again.

Exported live on 2026-11-24 (*OpenType TT*, a font of 103 glyphs): the empty *nbspace* the tab had built is in the file with no contours, the space's advance, and U+00A0 (the export profile names it *uni00A0*). Every glyph Python makes for a font is held for the session (`sys._freelab_made_glyphs`): FontLab adds a copy, so it is not needed, and it costs nothing.

Not yet run live either: the next scope's builds (2026-11-24). They make no FontLab call the earlier ways do not make: scaled components (`GlyphComponent` with a `QTransform`, through `setGlyphComponents`, as the squared characters are written) and a dash's outlines drawn longer (cloned contours, `place_node`, `flShape`, `addContours`, `addShape`, as the en dash is written), each master read back as before. The tests build each kind through FontLab's objects stubbed, in two masters planned apart.

### Next

The research's next scope (superscript and subscript signs and letters, signs made of letters, circled figures and letters, and full-width forms; then, as starting points, small capitals, letters with bars, small kana, and half-width forms) is built since 2026-11-24 (*What it builds*). What it leaves, and why:

- **Thicker strokes for scaled glyphs**: the research asked for stroke-compensated scaling. Every scaled glyph of the next scope is its sources scaled, as components, so its strokes are thinner than the font's; thickening them, as Feature Code's *Strokes* does its figures, would make each glyph outlines that no longer follow their sources, and Feature Code measures its compensation on figures only. Until it is measured on letters and signs, those glyphs stay starting points, and their rows say *scaling thins its strokes*.
- *‰*: its third ring is a contour of *%*, which the tab would have to take out of it (a design whose rings join the slash would defeat that); the tab composes whole glyphs.
- The kanbun annotation marks (*㆒* to *㆟*, 14, `<super>` of ideographs): small ideographs set beside the line, which too few of the corpus's fonts have to place them.
- The circled Hangul (*㉠* to *㉾*, 31): Unicode decomposes them to conjoining jamo, positional forms, not the letters; the parenthesized ideographs and Hangul (*㈠ ㈀*, 67, `<compat>`), not measured yet.
- The half-width Hangul (U+FFA0 to U+FFDC, 52: compatibility jamo, positional as above) and signs (U+FFE8 to U+FFEE, 7), not measured yet.

A survey of Unicode 16's compatibility decompositions (the owner's question of 2026-11-18, image 315: "whether our glyph generator can cover more glyphs"), by tag, with what the tab builds since 2026-11-24: `<compat>` 720 (423 built: the table's 45, the 212 Kangxi radicals, the 68 telegraph symbols, the 32 Roman numerals, and the 66 parenthesized and numbered forms); `<square>` 286 (206 built); `<fraction>` 20 (all built); `<noBreak>` 5 (4 built); `<wide>` 104 (all built); `<super>` 249 and `<sub>` 64 (156 built, and the 22 figures and ordinal indicators Feature Code builds); `<circle>` 240 (203 built); `<narrow>` 122 (63 built).
