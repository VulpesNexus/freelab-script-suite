# Auto spacing

*Scripts > FreeLab > Auto Spacing* suggests sidebearings for the drawn glyphs of the font's current master (Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, Armenian, Georgian, Devanagari, Hebrew, Thaana, and kana that are proportional already), shows each glyph between its references as it is and as suggested, and changes only the glyphs you leave ticked.

## Using it

1. Space the references by eye first: *H* and *n*, then *O* and *o* (Walter Tracy's method), and *l*, *0*, and *8*. They set the spacing everything else is measured against, and the script changes them only where they look unspaced and you tick them (below).
2. Run the script: it measures the master as it opens (about a second for 150 glyphs), every glyph whose character is a letter, a number, punctuation, or a symbol (*Show: Everything*) unless you pick less. The options sit in one row over the list, *Analyze* at its end: *Show*, *List changes of at least*, and *Glyphs built from components follow the glyphs they are built on*. *Letters* covers the Latin letters to Latin Extended-B and the letters of the other scripts above (see *Other scripts*); *Letters and figures* adds the figures (leave them out while they are tabular, one width for all); *Letters, figures, and punctuation* adds punctuation; *Everything*, the choice as it opens, adds every other glyph whose character is a letter, a number, punctuation, or a symbol (marks, spaces, and characters past U+FFFF are left out): the letters of scripts without references of their own (Coptic, Arabic) are spaced by their case (beside *H* or *n*), and symbols beside *n*.
3. The list holds the glyphs with a side at least the given number of units (6 by default) from its suggestion, laid out as the other tools' lists: the glyph drawn from the font beside its character, its name, and its code point (*Glyph*, *Name*, *Unicode*), then each side's sidebearing now, the change suggested (below 0: closer to its neighbors), and the width after, right-aligned. With no font open, its settings are grayed out and the list stays empty until one is opened. Selecting a glyph draws it between two of its references on each side, as it is (*Now*) and as suggested (*Suggested*), the two previews side by side with a line between them. Under the font line ([WINDOWS.md](WINDOWS.md#layout)), a line says how many glyphs are listed, and the note on the glyphs built from components follows it.
4. Untick what you disagree with, and click *Space ticked glyphs*. A glyph's outline moves by its left change and its width changes by both. One click is enough: see [One click](#one-click). *Show in Glyph window*, or double-clicking the glyph's name, opens the glyph between its references in a Glyph window, to adjust it by hand. Double-clicking a reference beside the glyph in a preview opens that pair in Auto Kerning (a single click there does nothing). *Tick all* and *Untick all* tick or untick the whole list; *Restore suggestions* puts the ticks back as the list first came.

A font with nothing done yet has its references unspaced too. When H's or n's sidebearings look unspaced (a side at its ink, or one side three times the other), the references come first in the list, unticked, spaced as the open-licensed fonts space theirs: *spacing/train/references.py* measures H, O, l, n, o, and zero in 398 families into *spacing/reference_model.json*, as shares of the cap height or x-height (H about 0.08 of the cap height on each side, n 0.09 of the x-height, o 0.077; l, measured in 457 families, 0.10 of the x-height on its left and 0.07 on its right). l, n, and o are suggested together, since every glyph is measured against the three: n and o spaced without l leave ll far from nn and oo, and nothing can be spaced after. For a font not spaced yet, tick them, space them, then click *Analyze*: the other glyphs are measured against them. A font tight on purpose looks the same (the owner's test font sets H's left side at -23 units), so they stay unticked unless you tick them.

## When a font cannot be spaced

Every glyph is measured against *l*, *n*, and *o*, each set beside itself (*ll*, *nn*, *oo*): the measuring's reach is grown until those three pairs agree within a factor of two. A font whose *l*, *n*, and *o* sit too differently apart (one far tighter or looser than the others, as in some display fonts), whose *l*, *n*, or *o* has no stroke thick enough to measure from, or that lacks one of them gives nothing to measure by. The window's top line still names the font, the line under it says *Could not space this font: the log says why.*, and the log says in plain words what was tried, why it failed for this font, and what to do (space *l*, *n*, and *o* by eye first). *Analyze* and *Show* are grayed meanwhile, since clicking them would fail the same way; the window measures the font again by itself as it changes (an edit, an undo), and they come back once it can be measured. An error that does not come from the font leaves *Analyze* clickable, to try again.

Changes go straight into the open font, as one step of FontLab's History: *Edit > Undo* takes them back, and the dialog measures the glyphs again after an undo, a redo, or an edit in FontLab. What a write did goes to the log under the previews; a glyph the log names is drawn there as the font draws it, and a click on it opens it in a Glyph tab. The font's kerning was set for the old sidebearings: *Check kerning of these glyphs…* opens Kerning Check on the glyphs just spaced and those built on them that followed ([KERNING.md](KERNING.md#kerning-check): its lists then hold only the pairs and entries with them), and Auto Kerning, run again with *Include existing kerned pairs*, shows which pairs now want other values.

## How it measures

Each side is measured as a pair with the glyph's straight reference: *H* for capitals, *n* for lowercase and punctuation, *0* for figures, by the kind of the glyph's base letter (*Ø* takes *H*). Auto Kerning's two methods, halfkern's and contour distance, find the kerning that pair would want to sit as the references sit beside each other (*HH*, *HO*, *OO* for capitals; *ll*, *nn*, *oo* for lowercase); that kerning is what the sidebearing lacks. *T*'s right side is the pair *TH*, its left side *HT*. The two methods' values are averaged, halfkern's tightenings at half strength as in Auto Kerning before its factors are fitted. A side within the references' own range is left as it is, so most sides of round and straight letters stay.

## One click

A measure does not land where the methods want a side: halfkern closes half of a tightening at a time, and both methods are measured on pixels, inside bands the references set, so a glyph spaced once asks for more when measured again. So the analysis measures each change again with it made (the glyph drawn moved and widened, as spacing would), and again, until each side's next step is under 0.2% of the em (2 units in a 1,000-unit em), overshoots (half of the step back is taken), or six passes are done. The list shows where each glyph ends up, and the status line says how many passes the analysis took. Offline, on open-licensed fonts unspaced at random by up to 40 units a side, Noto Sans, Arimo (2,048 units per em), and EB Garamond listed 42, 33, and 36 glyphs on the first click, and 0, 2, and 1 on the second, each at most 7 units off (the measuring's pixels: 5 units in a 1,000-unit em, 10 in a 2,048-unit one); measuring again after writing (below) spaces those in the same click. The changes are larger than one measure suggests: they are where repeated clicks would end up.

*Space ticked glyphs* then writes the ticked glyphs and measures them again: those still a side the tolerance or more off are spaced again, up to three passes, all one step of the History; the log says how many passes it took. Ticked references (from the corpus, for a font not spaced yet) are spaced first, and the other glyphs measured against them again before they are spaced, so their changes differ from the list's; the log says so. If FontLab did not give the new outlines back before the step ended, the log says so and the list shows what is left.

Open shapes get the largest suggestions (*L*, *T*, *K*, *F* on the right): spacing them to look right beside *H* tightens them, where some designers keep them looser and kern their pairs instead. Untick those to keep them for kerning.

## Other scripts

Each script is spaced against references of its own, the letters Auto Kerning measures it by ([KERNING.md](KERNING.md#other-scripts)), where the font has them; they are changed only where they look unspaced and you tick them (the paragraph after this list):

- **Armenian**: capitals beside vo (Ո), lowercase beside small vo (ո), held to vo and oh set with each other (ՈՈ, ՈՕ, ՕՕ; ոո, ոս, օօ).
- **Georgian**: Mkhedruli beside in (ი), held to in, on, and san (ი, ო, ს); Mtavruli beside their in (Ი), held to in and on (Ი, Ო).
- **Cyrillic**: capitals beside en (Н), held to en and o (Н, О); lowercase beside small en (н), held to en, o, and pe (н, о, п).
- **Greek**: capitals beside eta (Η), held to eta and omicron (Η, Ο); lowercase beside small eta (η), held to eta, omicron, and iota (η, ο, ι).
- **Devanagari**: beside na (न), held to na, pa, and ttha (न, प, ठ), set joined along the headline as text sets them.
- **Hebrew**: beside he (ה), held to he, final mem, and samekh (ה, ם, ס). Sidebearings read as on screen, left and right, whatever the direction of the text.
- **Thaana**: beside noonu (ނ), held to noonu, raa, and meemu (ނ, ރ, މ), as on screen like Hebrew.
- **Kana**, only where the font's kana are proportional already (most narrower than 90% of the em): beside RO (ロ), held to RO, NO, and I (ロ, の, い). Full-width kana are set proportionally by palt, which CJK Spacing writes ([CJK.md](CJK.md)); they are left out here.

A script whose references the font lacks has its letters spaced by case against H and n, as *Using it* says. When a script's straight reference (vo, in) looks unspaced, its references come first in the list, unticked, spaced as the open-licensed fonts space theirs: *spacing/reference_model.json* holds them as shares of the straight reference's height: Armenian's capital vo 0.12 of its height on the left and 0.09 on the right, oh 0.08 on each side; small vo 0.12 and 0.12, small oh 0.11 and 0.11; Georgian's in and on 0.10 on each side, Mtavruli's 0.09. They come from the only three open-licensed families with each script at hand (Google Sans, Noto Sans and Noto Serif Armenian or Georgian): a starting point, not a norm. The corpus holds no such shares for Cyrillic, Greek, Devanagari, Hebrew, or Thaana yet: their references are never suggested, so space them by eye first.

## Glyphs built from components

A glyph made only of components (*é* from *e* and an acute) is not spaced: it follows the glyph it is built on. Moving *e*'s outline moves it inside *é* too, since the component draws *e*'s outline (checked in FontLab 7.2: shifting a layer moves its nodes, and component offsets and widths can be set); with *Glyphs built from components follow the glyphs they are built on* (on by default), the accents of *é* move with it, and *é* takes *e*'s change of width. A composite built on one that follows (an acute over *ê*) follows in turn. One whose diacritics then stick out further past its width or before its start (a horn on a tighter *O*, a vertical caron on *d*) follows all the same, since its base glyph's outline has moved under its marks already, and the log names it in a warning, to check its marks. One built on two changed glyphs (a ligature made of *f* and *i*) is left as it is and counted, to look at.

On the owner's test font, 72 of 156 drawn glyphs had a side 6 units or more from its suggestion, mostly capitals on their right side, and 10 composites followed them.

## Limits

- Only the current master changes. Each master is spaced on its own.
- Sidebearings are read from the ink's bounds, so italic angles are not taken into account.
- Metric keys (FontLab's linked metrics, such as `=H`) are not read: a glyph whose sidebearing is linked takes its key's value again when FontLab recomputes it.
- Anchors and guides of a drawn glyph move with its outline only where FontLab's layer transform moves them; the owner's test font has none, so this was not checked.
