# Font Check

*Scripts > FreeLab > Font Check* holds three views of the current font: the languages its characters cover, its vertical metrics, and what keeps its masters from interpolating. Nothing in the font changes until one of its two writing buttons is clicked: *Write suggested values* (Vertical metrics), which *Put back old values* takes back (FontLab's History does not cover the font info), and *Fix* (Masters), one step of *Edit > Undo*. Its other buttons open Diacritics (which writes only when its own button is clicked), FontLab's Font Info, or a Glyph window.

Each view puts the answer first, then the action: a summary at the top, pictures where the matter is visual (characters, glyphs, lines), one line per thing with its details in a tooltip or on selection, and a few states shown as colored marks rather than sentences. The window keeps the conventions of FreeLab's other dialogs: a tab per view, a settings row and a top line with the counts in bold, grid lines between the cells of the lists, a log, a right-click on a glyph to flag it in FontLab (F3 and Shift+F3 go to the next and previous flagged row), *Show in Glyph window* (or a double-click), each view checked as its tab is first shown and again after an undo, a redo, or an edit in FontLab, and the dialog's ? button to explain any control. At 1000 by 1000 pixels nothing scrolls sideways: long texts are cut with an ellipsis and shown whole in their tooltips.

## Coverage

Each language's orthography, from [Hyperglot](https://github.com/rosettatype/hyperglot) (Rosetta Type, Apache License 2.0), names the characters it is written with: its **base** characters (required in common text), its **auxiliary** ones (loan words, names, letters falling out of use), and the **marks** it needs on their own. A language is:

- **covered** when the font has every base and auxiliary character and every mark;
- **within reach** when it misses at most the number of base characters set (*Within reach: missing at most*, 3 to start with; a missing mark counts as one), or only auxiliary characters;
- **missed** otherwise.

The view has three parts.

- **The scripts** the font has (at least five of their letters, or a language covered), the most languages first, each with a thin bar of its languages covered (the palette's highlight color), within reach (amber), and missed (gray), and a count: *340 of 569 languages · 60 within reach*. *All scripts* comes first. Clicking a script shows only its languages below. The scripts the font has none of are left out; a line under the list counts them, and *Show them* lists them too.
- **Add these to reach more languages**, at the left: what the languages within reach need, one row per character (a capital and its small letter together when both are missing, since a language needs them together), drawn large in the dialog's own font (a missing character cannot be drawn from the font), with its short Unicode name, its code points, **+N**, and the languages' names. *+N* counts the languages the row completes on its own; a row that completes none on its own (its languages miss other characters too) shows *+0* in gray and names what else they miss: *Wolof (with ɓ)*. Rows are ranked by *+N*, then by how many languages need them. Checking rows updates the line above the list: *With 6 characters checked: +14 languages (Latin)*. It counts sets, not sums: a language counts once every character it needs is checked, so the line is exact whatever rows are checked. Three buttons over the list set the checks at once: *Check all* checks every row listed (the checks of rows another script's filter hides are kept), *Check none* clears every check, and *Check only what Diacritics can build* checks the rows listed with a character Diacritics can build and unchecks the rest; each is grayed out, with a tooltip saying why, when it would change nothing. What a language needs is its missing base characters and marks (Hyperglot's own notion of support); a language missing only auxiliary characters needs those, and its rows say so in their tooltips. *Build with Diacritics* opens Diacritics on the checked characters it can build (its own planning asked once per check, from the font's letters and marks); it is unavailable, with a tooltip saying why, when it can build none of them. *Copy characters* copies the checked characters (or the selected row's).
- **Languages**, at the right: a field to find a language by its name, autonym, or code, and a choice of all, covered, within reach, or missed ones. Each row has a colored dot (its state), its name and autonym, and, within reach, its missing characters in short (*ß*, or *Ǎ ǎ +1*); a language covered thanks to the anchors says *composed*. Its tooltip says everything: the base, auxiliary, and mark characters missing, the ones composed, and Hyperglot's status. Selecting a language checks nothing but sets the characters it needs in bold in the list at the left, scrolled to the first; double-clicking it opens them in a Glyph window, where the missing ones show as FontLab's placeholders.

A character the font lacks as one character still counts when the font can **compose** it: Unicode's decomposition (NFD) gives its letter and combining marks, the font has them, and the letter has an anchor each mark attaches to (*top* on the letter, *_top* on the mark), so the font's mark feature puts them together. The longest part the font has is used, so *ḗ* stands on the font's *ē* with an acute. A character given decomposed (Hyperglot writes some letters as a base and marks) counts when the font has its composed form (NFC). A letter and a mark the font has but no anchor joins are missing, and the row's tooltip says the font has their parts; unchecking *Count letters its anchors compose* counts only the font's own characters. Hangul syllables do not compose from jamo here (jamo are letters, not marks), nor modifier letters from their base letters.

Historical languages (no native speakers left: Latin, Ancient Greek) are left out unless *Historical languages* is checked; constructed ones (Esperanto) are in.

Diacritics today takes no list of letters: it opens on all its blocks, the missing letters among the ones it lists, checked as it checks every letter it can build. Font Check calls `main(characters=[...])` first and falls back to `main()` when Diacritics does not take them; for Diacritics to list only those letters, its `main` would take `characters=None`, hand it to its dialog, and the dialog would add a first entry to its *Character set* box (*From Font Check*) whose `characters()` are those letters, selected as it opens. Diacritics is not changed by this script.

### Why these parts

FontLab 7 and 8 have no view of language coverage (FontLab 8's audit covers kerning) and draw no metrics. Glyphs relies on plugins: TalkingLeaves lists the languages a font nearly supports from Hyperglot's data, with a button to add glyphs, and mekkablue's Vertical Metrics Manager reports the highest and lowest glyphs and writes the values; Hyperglot's web app lists the languages supported per script; Google Fonts' metrics guide is text only. None of them ranks the missing characters by the languages they would complete, and none draws the metrics: those two ideas carry this design.

### The data

*checks/language_coverage.json* is made by *checks/train/languages.py* from Hyperglot 0.8.2 (its release archive, downloaded over HTTPS and checked against its SHA-256): 829 orthographies of 799 languages, 260 KB. For each language it keeps the primary orthographies (and the historical ones of historical languages), as Hyperglot's own language detection does; secondary orthographies and transliterations are left out. Hyperglot's inheritance (`<iso>`, `<iso auxiliary>`, `<iso Script>`, `<default Script>`) is resolved, characters are kept composed (NFC), digraphs are split into their letters (Hyperglot does not check them as units either), and the marks kept are Hyperglot's "required" ones: listed marks no character of the orthography decomposes into, and the marks of letter-and-mark clusters Unicode has no single character for. Lists shared by several orthographies (Chinese's) are stored once. *checks/language_coverage_LICENSE* carries Hyperglot's license and says what was changed.

```
python checks/train/languages.py --download <folder>
```

### The languages list's font

The languages list is drawn in one font of its own, *FreeLab Languages* (*checks/language_font/FreeLabLanguages.ttf*, 2.1 MB), at 1.25 times the dialog's size, its rows, section headings, and column headings alike; tooltips keep Qt's own font. In the dialog's font, Qt 5 took each character the font lacked from another one: rare scripts' autonyms (Canadian syllabics, Tifinagh, Vai, Tai Viet, Tai Tham, Ethiopic, and others) showed as boxes, since Qt searches only some of the installed fonts, and some Cyrillic autonyms were spaced wide. The bundled font has every character the list shows: the languages' names and autonyms in every script, the script names, the digits and punctuation marks, and the characters of every orthography's base, auxiliary, and mark lists, which the *Missing* column shows (a mark on a dotted circle). Only the ideographs, Hangul syllables, cuneiform signs, and Egyptian hieroglyphs that the *Missing* column alone can show are left out (about 17,000, which would add several megabytes): a font of Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Akkadian, Sumerian, or Ancient Egyptian within reach shows those in the system's fonts. The font claims each writing system it draws (OS/2 Unicode ranges and code pages), or Qt 5 would take another family for that script's text. Its line holds the deepest and highest marks the list draws (Tibetan stacks), so rows are a little taller than the dialog's text. Without the file, the list keeps the dialog's font, still enlarged.

*checks/train/language_font.py* makes it from 46 Noto fonts (SIL Open Font License 1.1): Noto Sans for Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic, a Noto Sans font per script, Noto Serif Tibetan, and Noto Sans JP, KR, and SC (variable, instanced at Regular) for the kana, jamo, and ideographs the autonyms use. It downloads them at pinned commits of notofonts.github.io and google/fonts, outside the repository, and checks that each one's name table declares the OFL and that its license reserves no name it uses (the CJK fonts reserve *Source*). Each character comes from its script's font (a punctuation mark or a combining mark: from the font of the texts it is in, so marks attach to their letters); each font is subset to its characters with its shaping kept (GSUB, GPOS), unhinted, and the subsets merged into one TrueType font renamed *FreeLab Languages*, so it never stands for an installed Noto font. HarfBuzz shapes every run of what the list shows with the merged font and with its source font: they agree but for a lone zero-width joiner and non-joiner. *checks/language_font/language_font.json* lists the characters bundled and left out, and each source's version, SHA-256, scripts, and characters; *FreeLabLanguages_LICENSE* carries the sources' copyright lines and their licenses. The same sources give the same bytes.

```
python ../.workspace/tools/heavy-run.py --cap 3 --reserve 2 -- python checks/train/language_font.py
```

### How it compares with Hyperglot's own checker

On twelve open-licensed corpus fonts (Noto Sans, EB Garamond, Gentium Plus, Poppins, GFS Didot, Noto Sans Arabic, Devanagari, Thai, Khmer, JP, KR, and Amiri), Font Check's base-level support (no composing) and Hyperglot 0.8.2's checker (`Checker.get_supported_languages`, no shaping, draft validity and up, living and constructed languages) agree on 283 to 624 languages per font, and differ in three ways only, each by design:

- Letter-and-mark clusters with no single character (Hausa's *r̃*, Marshallese's *m̧*): Hyperglot without its shaping checks counts them when the font has the letter and the mark; Font Check asks for an anchor that joins them (about 50 languages in each Latin font with all the combining marks). Counting every mark as attached, and Hyperglot in its decomposed mode, the two agree on Noto Sans and Gentium Plus (673 and 658 languages) but for the next two points.
- Languages whose orthographies Hyperglot asks for together (`preferred_as_group`: Serbian in Cyrillic and Latin, Japanese in kana and kanji, Ainu, Pontic): Font Check lists each orthography under its own script, so a Latin-only font covers Serbian's Latin orthography.
- Hyperglot's decomposed mode also takes a modifier letter from its base letter (*ᶿ* from *θ*, by NFKD); Font Check does not, since a modifier letter needs a glyph of its own.

## Vertical metrics

The values are read from FontLab: the current master's ascender, descender, x-height, cap height, and line gap (`flPackage.ascender_value` and its siblings), and the font's OpenType line metrics from its font info (`fgPackage.info`: `openTypeOS2TypoAscender`, `openTypeOS2TypoDescender`, `openTypeOS2TypoLineGap`, `openTypeHheaAscender`, `openTypeHheaDescender`, `openTypeHheaLineGap`, `openTypeOS2WinAscent`, `openTypeOS2WinDescent`, and bit 7 of `openTypeOS2Selection`, *Use Typo Metrics*). An ascender, descender, or win value of 0 reads as not set. Every glyph's ink is measured in every master (its outline as FontLab draws it, components included).

The checks follow Google Fonts' vertical metrics guidance ([gf-docs, VerticalMetrics](https://github.com/googlefonts/gf-docs/tree/main/VerticalMetrics)). In short:

- Windows cuts off whatever reaches above winAscent or below winDescent, so those two should be the family's highest and lowest points, over every glyph of every master. With Use Typo Metrics on, Windows spaces lines by the typo values instead, so the win values can grow to stop clipping without loosening the lines.
- macOS spaces lines by hhea, Windows (with Use Typo Metrics) by typo: the two sets should be equal.
- Line gaps should be 0: applications add a line gap above the line, browsers split it above and below, and Windows without Use Typo Metrics ignores it, so a gap spaces lines differently everywhere.
- For Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic, the line (ascender plus descender) should be 120% to 130% of the em, the default leading of page layout applications; taller scripts (Devanagari, Arabic, Vietnamese's stacked marks) may need more.
- The line's ascender should clear *À*: some macOS applications place the first line by the top of *À* or the ascender, whichever is taller.
- Capitals centered in the line (the room above the cap height equal to the room below the baseline) help web designers center text in buttons.
- An update of a family already released keeps its line spacing, so documents set in it do not reflow: when Use Typo Metrics was off, the old win values become the typo values, and the win values then grow to the new ink.

The view has four parts.

- **The diagram**, across the top: the font's three tallest and three deepest glyphs by ink (each in the master where it reaches farthest, its name and master under it) on one baseline, and the metrics as lines across: winAscent, the typo and hhea ascenders, the cap height, the x-height, the baseline, the typo and hhea descenders, and winDescent, labeled at the right edge with their values; equal values are one line with both names (*typo and hhea ascender 965*). Ink past winAscent or winDescent is red: Windows clips it. A bracket at the left spans the line (ascender minus descender, plus the line gap) and gives its share of the em (*120% of the em*). The suggested values that differ are dashed in the highlight color. A glyph selected among the clipped ones is drawn in it too, in the highlight color.
- **The values**: typo ascender, descender, and line gap, hhea ascender, descender, and line gap, winAscent, winDescent, and Use Typo Metrics, now and as suggested, the suggested ones in bold where they differ. Under them, *Write suggested values*, *Copy suggested values*, *Open Font Info*, and the strategy, worded for the font's own designer. *Line spacing [120]% of the em*, chosen to start with, suggests the line spacing at the share set, capitals centered in it, raised to the top of *À* and lowered to the deepest descender of *a* to *z* (and Greek and Cyrillic), with line gaps of 0, hhea equal to typo, win at the ink's extremes, and Use Typo Metrics on; a font whose letters are mostly of another script (Arabic, Devanagari, Han) has the line shared as its ascender and descender are, then raised and lowered to clear its letters' ink. This reproduces the guidance's worked example exactly (typo 965 and −235, win 1116 and 320). *Keep the font's current line spacing*, for an update of a font already released, keeps the typo values if Use Typo Metrics was on, or else takes the old win values as typo values (the share of the em is then grayed out). An earlier wording (*New font: line at*, *Released font: keep its line spacing*) read as addressed to someone modifying another's font; FreeLab's user is the font's designer. The rows' tooltips give the reasons.
- **The checks**, beside the values: one line each, a drawn icon (an amber warning, a gray dot for what is good to know, a green check for what passed, in a quieter color) and a short sentence: *Windows clips 15 glyphs at the top, up to 82 units (circumflex.case)*, Use Typo Metrics off, hhea and typo values differing, line gaps (*Line gap 100: set it to 0 and add it to the ascender and descender*), the line's share of the em, *À* above the line's ascender, capitals not centered, the glyphs reaching past the line's ascender or descender (the farthest 40 each way: they may touch the line above or below, which tall marks often do), and win values far past the ink. The checks that passed are listed too, so the user sees what was checked. Each line's tooltip has the full sentence; clicking a line about clipped glyphs selects them below.
- **Clipped on Windows**: a wrapping row of small pictures of the glyphs Windows clips, the worst first, the part cut off in red and the overflow in units under each. With several masters, *Master* shows every master's (each glyph once, in its worst master) or one master's. Double-clicking a picture opens the glyph in a Glyph window; right-clicking flags it. The row is hidden when no glyph is clipped.

The line's share and centered capitals are judged only for a font whose letters are mostly Latin, Greek, or Cyrillic.

The design first put the diagram at the left, most of the height, beside the values: six glyphs side by side with their labels need the window's width, and beside the values they came out about a quarter as tall, so the diagram spans the window and the values and checks sit under it.

*Write suggested values* (with the count of values that differ: *Write 5 suggested values*) writes those values into the font info, for the whole font (`fgPackage.info`): the typo values (ascender, descender, line gap), the hhea values (the same three), the win values (floats), and *Use Typo Metrics* (bit 7 added to `openTypeOS2Selection`, a list of bit numbers). Only the values that differ are written; each is read back, the log lists each value old and new (*Typo ascender 800 → 965*) and any FontLab did not keep, and the view is checked again. FontLab's *Edit > Undo* does not take the font info back (tried live: it reverted *Use Typo Metrics* only), so FreeLab keeps the values it replaced: *Put back old values* writes them again, into the font written to only, while it is the current one (told apart by `fgPackage.id`, the same through every wrapper of one font: two unsaved fonts of one family name share their path-less key). A value that was not set before stays as written, since no way to unset a font info value from a script is known: the log names it, to clear in *Font Info*. A value FontLab refuses to take back stays held, and the button tries it again. The masters' own metrics (`ascender_value`, `descender_value`, `xHeight_value`, `capsHeight_value`, `lineGap_value`: the current master's, which FontLab draws its guides with) are not part of the suggestion and are never changed. A win, typo, or hhea ascender or descender of 0 reads as not set, so a suggested 0 where the font has none counts as the same. *Copy suggested values* copies them as text, and *Open Font Info* opens FontLab's Font Info (`flItems.requestFontInfoDlg`).

## Masters

Masters interpolate only where every glyph has the same structure in each. Each master is compared with the one chosen in *Compare every master with* (the font's default master to start with), glyph by glyph, and each difference names the glyph, the master, the contour (numbered from 1, in FontLab's order), and what differs:

- a glyph the master lacks;
- the number of contours, and contours in another order (the same node counts, shuffled);
- a contour open in one master and closed in the other;
- the number of nodes in a contour, and the first node whose type differs (on-curve, off-curve);
- a contour's direction (the sign of its area), also when the reversal shows first as node types in another order; a contour whose area is under 5% of its box's (a figure eight, whose halves cancel out) is not judged;
- a contour's start point: with the same nodes and types, the contour's points (centered and scaled to its size) are compared with the reference's at every start the node types allow; when another start fits at least four times as well as the one in place, and the one in place fits poorly, the row says how many nodes on or back the start sits. This is a judgment from the shapes: a contour whose shape changes a lot between masters (a dot drawn as a square in one weight and as a diamond in another) can look moved when it is not. A closed contour whose node types differ but match from another start (a start moved from a corner to the end of a curve) is a moved start too, at the start that fits best;
- components, by glyph name, and their order;
- anchors, by name.

The view has three parts.

- At the top, a chip per kind of problem with the number of glyphs that have it (*Node counts 3*, *Anchors 15*, *No layer 9*), all shown to start: each shows or hides that kind of problem in the list, and a glyph left with no kind shown leaves it. A chip shown is filled in the highlight color, a chip hidden is a gray outline: the chips carry a stylesheet of their own drawn from the palette, as FreeLab's tab bars do, and dark mode leaves them alone (Windows' own style drew a checked chip light whatever the palette, which dark mode garbled).
- At the left, one row per glyph with problems, the worst first (those that cannot interpolate: a missing layer, contour or node counts, node types, contour order, an open contour; then directions, start points, and components; then anchors): its picture, its name, a mark per master in the font's order (a ring for the master compared with, red where the glyph cannot interpolate, a dashed square where it has no layer, amber where it interpolates oddly, a faint dot where all is well), and whether Font Check can fix it: *Fixable*, *Partly fixable*, or *Needs the designer*. A row it can fix has a check box, checked to start. Under the list, *Fix checked* (with the count: *Fix 3 checked glyphs*) and *Fix this* (the selected glyph).
- At the right, the selected glyph in the master compared with and in another (*Beside it*: the first master with a problem to start with, each master with problems saying how many), side by side at one scale: the contour at fault in the highlight color, its nodes numbered from 1 and its start point ringed, every contour's number at its start when their count or order differs, and anchors as small crosses with their names (those one master lacks in the highlight color); a master without the glyph shows a dashed empty frame. Under the pictures, the problems in that master, one sentence each, and what *Fix* sets right and what needs the designer.

### Fix

FontLab users often wish FontLab would force incompatible glyphs to interpolate. Font Check fixes what has one safe mechanical answer, in every master but the one compared with, which never changes:

- **the contours' order**: when the contours' node counts are the same but shuffled, the master's contours are matched with the reference's, one to one, at the least cost (Kuhn and Munkres' assignment): a contour pairs only with one of its node count, and costs the distance between their centers and between their widths and heights (in their glyph's box), plus one when their node types match in neither direction from any start. They are put in that order within their own shape (FontLab's contours belong to shapes; a contour is never moved from one shape to another, which is left to the designer);
- **a contour's direction**: the contour reversed (`flContour.reverse()`, which keeps its first node first);
- **a contour's start point**: the start set (`setStartPoint`) on the node that fits the reference's start, judged as the check judges it (above), and only on an on-curve node of a closed contour (FontLab moves a start onto on-curve nodes only; a start that would fall on an off-curve node, or an open contour's, is left to the designer). After a reversal the start is found again;
- **the components' order**: the master's components put in the reference's order (the same names taken in turn), the other shapes left where they are.

Contour and node counts, node types, open contours, other components, missing layers, and anchors need the designer: the row says so, the row's tooltip and the sentence under the pictures name what is left, and *Fix* does not touch them. A glyph with both kinds has what can be fixed fixed. Each contour is fixed only when the result matches the reference's node types: a contour whose types differ whatever its direction and start is not reversed or moved either.

Every fix is planned from the outlines first, carried out, then read back and compared with the plan's result; the master compared with is read back too. A master whose shapes share their data with the reference's (FontLab's shared elements, by `shapeData.id`) is skipped, since the reference would change with it. The fixes of one click make one step of *Edit > Undo*; the masters are compared again and the comparison drawn again, and the log says what was fixed (*o in Bold (contour 1 reversed, its start point moved 1 node on)*), what was not and why, which glyphs still need the designer, and any other glyph with new problems (an element shared with a fixed glyph).

A font with one master says there is nothing to compare. The outlines are read through FontLab's own objects (`layer.shapes`; a shape's `isComponent` and `shapeData.componentGlyph`; its `transform` and `contours`; a contour's `nodes()` and `closed`; a node's `x`, `y`, and `type`; `layer.anchors`), the shape's transform applied, as Auto Kerning's `layer_path` draws them. Components' positions and scales, node smoothness, and guides are not compared: they interpolate whatever their values.

## How it was checked

The checks and what the views show (the characters to add and their ranking, the languages a checked set completes, the diagram's lines and labels, the clipped glyphs, the check lines, the masters' marks) sit above the Qt line and are unit tested under Python 3 and 2.7 (*tests/fontcheck_tests.py*). The dialog is run on fakes of PythonQt and of a two-master FontLab font (every Qt call accepted and ignored; its contours, shapes, and layers edited as FontLab's were seen to edit, and `reorderShapes` read each way it might), *Fix* and *Write suggested values* included, which exercises its code paths but not Qt's or FontLab's answers. The redesigned views were also run offscreen under PySide6 (Qt 6), PythonQt's ways emulated (properties read as attributes, signals connected by name), on corpus fonts read with fontTools into a fake FontLab font (variable fonts instanced at their weight axis's ends as masters, a few faults planted for the Masters view), in light and dark palettes, and their pictures looked at; that harness is not in the repository. FontLab's Qt 5 and PythonQt can still differ, so the calls below are to be looked at live.

- **Coverage**: compared with Hyperglot's own checker on twelve corpus fonts, as above.
- **The languages list's font**: under Qt 5.15 (PyQt5, Windows' own font database, no window shown), every language label, script heading, and bundled *Missing* text was laid out in *FreeLab Languages* alone: no run taken from another family, no box; a picture of a sample of rows was looked at (Cyrillic, Indic, Southeast Asian, CJK, Tibetan, and rare scripts). With the earlier family list (*MS Shell Dlg 2*, then the font Font Check bundled before this one) Qt 5.15 did not show the wide Cyrillic spacing seen in FontLab's Qt 5.14, so that cause is not proven; one family claiming Cyrillic leaves Qt no other font to pick. *tests/language_font_tests.py* reads the font (no fontTools) and checks it has every character bundled and every character of the list but those left out, is a static TrueType font named apart from Noto that claims its writing systems, stays under 3 MB, and has its license beside it, and that Font Check finds it beside the script or in *language_font/*, registers it once, sets the list in it at 1.25 times the size (or only enlarges it without the file), and never raises.
- **Vertical metrics**: run on 169 open-licensed corpus fonts (variable fonts at their default and at each axis's ends, standing in for masters), and compared with what fontbakery's checks say of the same fonts (*win_ascent_and_descent*, *os2_metrics_match_hhea*, *linegaps*, *use_typo_metrics*): the same verdict for every font and check but two, Jua and Playpen Sans Hebrew, whose *head* table's box (control points) reaches past winDescent while their ink does not; Font Check measures the ink. In 22 variable fonts only a master other than the default clips, which a check of the *head* table cannot see.
- **Masters**: six variable corpus fonts (Noto Sans, EB Garamond, Cairo, Vazirmatn, Noto Sans Devanagari, Markazi Text; 13,407 glyphs), instanced at each axis's ends (compatible by construction, so anything found is a false alarm), then glyphs with one fault planted each (a start point moved, a contour reversed, a node dropped, components swapped, contours reordered, a glyph removed). 1,194 of the 1,200 planted faults were found (the six missed swap two components of the same name, or reorder contours of the same size: no change at all). False alarms came in 21 glyphs, all in Cairo's and Vazirmatn's Arabic dots and signs, whose shapes change between weights and slants: start points (66 contours) and two contours whose direction does flip in Vazirmatn's extremes. A plain comparison of the normalized points found 1,095 of 1,100 moved start points; fitting each start to the reference by a stretch and a slant first found 806, at as many false alarms, so the plain one is kept.
- **Masters' fixes**: the same six fonts instanced at their weight axis's ends (13,407 glyphs), one fault planted per glyph in the heavy master (4,444 component pairs swapped, 1,097 pairs of contours of other node counts swapped, 2,618 contours reversed, 2,748 starts moved onto another on-curve node), each fix planned, its result taken as FontLab's edits make it (a reversal keeps the first node first), and compared again: every one of the 10,907 came out compatible, and 10,870 exactly as the heavy master was drawn (the others: a symmetric contour or dot started at an equivalent node, or matched with its twin). On the compatible pairs, a fix was planned in 6 glyphs only, the Cairo and Vazirmatn signs whose start points the check already flags falsely (above): their rows show, so a fix there is the user's call.

## FontLab calls not yet proven live
