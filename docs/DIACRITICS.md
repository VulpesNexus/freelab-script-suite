# Diacritics

*Scripts > FreeLab > Diacritics* builds the letters with diacritics a font lacks (Latin, Greek, polytonic Greek, Cyrillic, and the voiced kana of Japanese), as glyphs of components, in every master, on its first tab; on its second, *Mark anchors*, it places the anchors and writes the mark features by which scripts that attach their marks (Khmer, Thai, Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, Bengali, Gujarati, Tamil, and others) set them. Nothing is made until you click *Build checked glyphs*. It needs no groundwork in the font: no anchors, no accented glyphs to learn from, no *.case* marks, not even marks. Where the font has some, it follows them. It was called Accented Glyphs: not every diacritic is an accent (a cedilla, an ogonek, a hook, a breathing).

## What it builds

- **Which characters**: every character of the chosen set (*Character set:* Latin-1 to Latin Extended-B, Latin Extended Additional, Greek, Greek Extended (polytonic), Cyrillic, Kana with dakuten (Japanese), or *All*) that the font lacks and that Unicode's canonical decomposition splits into a letter and marks (*ǎ* is *a* with a caron, *й* is *и* with a breve). A script the font has no letters of is left out, and the top line says so. A glyph left empty (by FreeLab's first version, which added glyphs without their layers) is listed again and filled under its own name. A glyph that would come out empty (a part with no outline in a master) is not built: the log says which part, the glyph the build added for it is taken out again, and the other checked glyphs are built. Where FontLab keeps none of a glyph's components in a master, the log names that master: a new glyph is left empty there, and a glyph the font had keeps its own outlines there.
- **Which marks**, in this order:
  1. The font's own, by name (*caroncomb*, *caron*, *uni030C*, and the names fonts give the marks below: *caroncomb.alt*, *commaaccentrotate*) or code point; for capitals, its *.case* form first; the script's own form before all (Greek's *tonos* for an acute, Cyrillic's *brevecomb-cy* for the short i's breve).
  2. For a mark below, its twin above (a dot below from the dot accent, a macron below from the macron; a comma below from the comma).
  3. A draft made of the font's punctuation: a dot accent of the period, a dieresis of two periods, a macron of the hyphen, a ring of the degree sign, a tilde of `~`, a circumflex of `^`, a caron of `^` turned upside down, a double acute of two acutes, a vertical caron of *quoteright* (else the modifier apostrophe or the comma), a turned comma above of the modifier turned comma or *quoteleft* (else the comma turned about), a dakuten of `”`, a handakuten of the degree sign. A draft is sized as that mark is in the corpus (a dot accent 0.11 em high, for example) and the list says it is one: a starting point to redraw. Upside down is a half turn (the component scaled by −1 both ways), never a mirror: a mirrored component's contours run the other way, and a renderer shows a hole where it overlaps another part (see *Limits*). The half turn is also the better shape: it gives a caron its thick stroke at the left, as a circumflex has its own at the right, and a slanted mark keeps its slant. An inverted breve the font lacks is its breve, turned the same way.

  *i* and *j* (and Cyrillic *і* and *ј*) take their dotless forms under a mark above. Greek's dialytika with tonos (*ΐ*) takes the font's *dieresistonos*, or its dieresis and tonos set over each other, the tonos between the dots. Glyphs that still cannot be built are listed apart, with what the font lacks (Vietnamese's horn and hook above have nothing to be drafted from).
- **Marks fonts draw otherwise**: for a few letters, fonts draw another mark than the one Unicode's decomposition names (Unicode's names for them predate the conventions). Diacritics builds them as most fonts do. *diacritics/train/conventions.py* told, in 433 open-licensed families, which mark each font draws on every letter it can build: by the components' names and code points in a composite glyph (*uni0326*, *caroncomb.alt*, *quoteright*), or by finding a mark glyph's outlines, point for point, in a glyph of outlines; a comma is a comma below under the letter, a vertical caron beside a stem's top. These are the letters where most fonts depart from the decomposition (the fonts counted are those whose mark could be told):

  Latvian (and Livonian, for *ḑ*) draws a comma below; Marshallese, which also writes *Ļ ļ Ņ ņ*, a cedilla. Latvian is by far the more common, and the fonts follow it: so does Diacritics (a font for Marshallese keeps its own cedilla glyphs). *Ţ ţ* and *Ş ş* keep their cedilla: most fonts draw one (Romanian's comma-below letters are *Ș ș Ț ț*, which Unicode decomposes with a comma below already). Each such mark is the font's own first (*commaaccentcomb*, *uni0326*, *commaturnedabovecomb*, *caroncomb.alt*, *caroncommaaccent*), then a draft, and its row says why (*the comma below fonts draw for Unicode's cedilla*). The vertical caron sits right of the stem's top, its top level with the letter's (0.048 em right of *d*'s and *l*'s stem, 0.091 em of *L*'s; on *t*, 0.042 em right and 0.1 em over its top, level with the ascenders), the turned comma above *g* like an acute (0.053 em over it, centered), as the corpus's medians set them.
- **Kana**: the voiced kana (*が* is *か* with a dakuten, *ぱ* is *は* with a handakuten; the katakana too, *ヴ*, *ヷ* to *ヺ*, and the voiced iteration marks) are built like the letters with diacritics, from the base kana and the font's dakuten or handakuten (the combining U+3099 and U+309A, else the spacing U+309B and U+309C, by code point or name), else a draft. The mark's ink is centered where the open-licensed Japanese fonts center theirs, from the right end and the top of the kana's ink: *diacritics/train/kana.py* found the mark in each voiced kana of 42 families (as a component, by its outlines, or as the contours its kana lacks) and measured it against the kana as drawn there. A dakuten's center sits a median 0.049 em left of a hiragana's right end and 0.065 em under its top (katakana: 0.037 and 0.025), a handakuten's 0.083 and 0.031 (katakana: 0.074 and 0.001), and each kana has its own row where 3 fonts or more show it. Each font left out in turn, the marks land a median 0.044 em from where that font puts them by the per-kana rows, 0.088 em by the hiragana and katakana medians alone. 37% of the fonts' voiced glyphs move or redraw their kana a little for the mark; Diacritics keeps the font's kana as it is. Voiced kana are named *uniXXXX*, as kana fonts name them. The set is listed when the font has kana.
- **Names**: Glyphs' names for the characters (*iishort-cy*, *Alphatonos*, *adotbelow*), from glyphsLib's *GlyphData.xml* (MIT License, its notice in *diacritics/GlyphData_LICENSE*), kept in *diacritics/glyph_names.json*; else the letter's name and the marks'; *uniXXXX* where those are taken. Each glyph gets its Unicode value.

## Where the marks go

- **Gaps**: from the font's own composites, per mark and case, where it has two or more (a composite counts where its character decomposes into its components: not a fraction, a guillemet, or *Ŀ*'s middle dot). Each mark counts as the character's own (a macron borrowed for a macron below is a mark below, not a macron above), measured from its letter as the composite places both (a letter component moved right moves its marks with it); a Greek capital's mark at its left counts on no side, and a composite with a component scaled, flipped, rotated, or slanted is not counted. Otherwise, from the corpus: *diacritics/train/marks.py* measures how the open-licensed fonts at hand (250 families) build their accented letters as composites (the mark fonts draw, where it is not Unicode's: Latvian's comma below is measured as a comma below, not as a cedilla), into *diacritics/mark_model.json*. A font whose own gaps run larger or smaller than the corpus's has the corpus's gaps scaled by the same amount.
- **Across**: each mark is centered on the center of the letter's top fifth (bottom fifth for marks below; the ogonek hangs from the right end of it), offset by a share of the letter's width. That share is the font's habit on that side and case, all its marks pooled (so a breve and a caron agree on *i*), or the corpus's, plus how far the corpus puts marks on that very letter from its habit: over *b*'s bowl rather than its stem (44% of its width), *K* and *Y* almost centered. A slanted hand's lowercase habit does not carry over to capitals. The corpus's letters are cut into fifths as Diacritics cuts the font's in FontLab, their outlines, curves and all (not their points, whose off-curve handles reach past the ink: measured that way, a top fifth's center ran 2 to 3% of the letter's width left of FontLab's on *c*, *C*, *G*, and *Æ*, and up to 7% off either way on *o* in a tenth of the fonts). Against 120 fonts' own composites, a mark above lands a median 17 units across from theirs (18 before), 18 on *c* (22), 22 on *C* (28), 16 on *G* (19).
- **Stacks**: marks stack the way the corpus stacks each pair. An acute or a hook above sits beside a circumflex, to its right and a little lower than its top, as Vietnamese is set; a tilde over a circumflex, or a macron over a dieresis, sits on it, about 0.05 em apart.
- **Greek capitals** carry their tonos (and breathings) at their left, the mark's top level with the capital's, 0.03 em away; the glyph is made wider by as much, so the mark keeps the letter's left sidebearing.
- **Polytonic Greek**: a breathing and its accent are one mark, the font's own (*psilioxia*, *dasiavaria*) or its two marks side by side, the breathing first, 0.025 em apart; a perispomeni sits on its breathing (*psiliperispomeni*) or dialytika; the dialytika takes its tonos or varia between its dots. The iota subscript goes under a small letter (*ᾳ*), and after a capital as an adscript (*ᾼ*: the font's *prosgegrammeni*, else its iota), on the baseline, the glyph made wider. A capital's breathings and accents sit at its left, as one.

## Room above and below

A glyph must stay clear of the lines around it at a 120% line spacing (applications' automatic leading): its top below where the line above's descenders come down (1.2 em plus the descender), its bottom above where the line below's tallest letters (the ascender or cap height) come up. A glyph that would not fit is made compact: the gaps of its stack closed to 40%, then its marks made smaller, 5% at a time, down to 70%; the list says what it took ("A + dieresis + macron (gaps closed to fit a 120% line)"). One that still does not fit is listed unchecked: it needs a shorter letter or marks drawn for it.

*Compact glyphs to fit the line*, beside *Also list existing glyphs*, switches that off (it is on at first, and the choice is remembered in *%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\diacritics.json*). Off, every mark keeps its own gap and its full size, a glyph too tall says by how much ("A + dieresis + macron (62 units too tall for a 120% line)") and stays checked, and the top line counts those ("42 too tall for a 120% line as placed") in place of the compacted ones: the compacting is yours to do. Switching it either way changes only the glyphs still placed as suggested. A glyph whose marks you moved, scaled, lined up, or took from a related letter keeps what you set: the hand's changes are measured from the suggestion it was placed under, so that glyph keeps that suggestion until its marks are put back (*Reset diacritics*, *Restore suggestions*), and then follows the box again. Checks follow the suggestion too (a glyph too tall even compact is unchecked, as before), but for the checks you changed yourself. Nothing changes in the font until you build: a glyph the font has (*Also list existing glyphs*) is only compared with the other suggestion, and is rebuilt as the list shows it only if you check it.

## Placing marks by hand

Under the list, the selected glyph is drawn large between its letter and a letter the font has with the same base, with the baseline and, as faint dashed lines named at one end, the font's lines, as Font Check's *Vertical metrics* reads them. They are of two kinds:

- **Limits**, past which something real happens, each saying what in parentheses: *winAscent* and *winDescent* ("winAscent 1160 (Windows clips above)"), the hhea ascender and descender ("hhea descender -288 (macOS clips below)": macOS lays lines out by them, and applications there may clip past them), and the room a 120% line leaves ("120% line 1080 (meets the line above)"). A limit a mark crosses is drawn in amber.
- **Reference lines**: the font's ascender and descender and the typo ascender and descender. They are drawn and never tinted: every accented capital rises over the ascender, and a mark below comes under the descender, with nothing clipped and no line met.

Lines at the same height share one line and one label ("typo and font ascender 880"); where a reference line and a limit coincide, the line is the limit's ("hhea descender, winDescent -288 (Windows and macOS clip below)"). A value the font does not set draws none. The row's notes about a glyph too tall stay as they are. The *Mark anchors* tab's editor draws the same lines.

The glyph is drawn as a renderer would fill it with its components kept: one outline, each component transformed as it would be written, filled by nonzero winding. A hole shown there (a mirrored component over another part) is a hole the font would have. The rows' pictures are drawn the same way.

- Click a mark to select it (it is drawn in the highlight color, in a frame with handles). Drag it, or press the arrow keys (Shift: 10 units), to move it; Shift while dragging keeps it on one axis. While dragged, a mark above or below its letter snaps, within 5 pixels, its center to the letter's: the center of its ink's box, and, where it differs (*a*, *r*, *G*), the center of its topmost ink (lowest, for a mark below); a thin line, named, shows the center while it holds. Alt held drags it freely.
- A drag ends when the button is let go, wherever the pointer is then, outside the editor or the window too; the picture keeps its scale while a mark is dragged and is fitted again afterward. A mark can be moved up to 16,000 units either way, and scaled between 10% and 1,000%: TrueType keeps a component's offset and every coordinate in 16 bits (−32,768 to 32,767), and a mark anywhere in the largest em OpenType allows (16,384 units), moved that far, still lands on coordinates the format can hold. A move within that stays exactly as you made it. The line under the editor says how far, in whole units ("moved 1,250 right, 40 up"). The picture opens to hold a moved mark up to an em past its lines; a mark moved farther is outside it: an arrow at the nearest edge points to it (a click on the arrow selects the mark), the line says so ("outside the view"), and Backspace or *Reset diacritic* brings it back.
- Double-click the glyph (or its row) to open it in a Glyph window; one not built yet opens its letter.
- Click the letter with diacritics drawn after the glyph (*ò* beside a new *ớ*) to go to its row and see how its own marks sit. It is a glyph the font has: its row is listed, unchecked, while *Also list existing glyphs* stays off, and *Check all* leaves it alone (the log says so). Tick the box to rebuild the font's own glyphs.
- Drag a corner of the frame to scale the mark both ways (Shift: in proportion), or a side to scale it one way; the opposite corner or side stays put.
- Tab selects the next mark, Backspace puts the selected one back, Escape deselects; *Reset diacritics* (*Reset diacritic* for a glyph with one) puts all of the glyph's marks back.
- *Apply to related letters* gives the diacritics moved or scaled by hand on the selected letter to the related ones: the same mark, on the same side and at the same place in its stack, over letters of the same case (a circumflex raised on C: every capital with a circumflex). Raised and scaled as much; across, a mark the hand centered on its letter (snapped to a center) is centered on each letter, on the same center, and otherwise its offset from its letter's center goes over in units, not the hand's move: centered stays centered on a wider letter (*ǽ* after *ó*), each letter keeping where the fonts set marks on it (an acute right of *ĺ*'s stem). A stacked mark, a mark beside its letter, and an ogonek take the hand's move as it is. The button is grayed once applied, until the mark is moved again. It stays clickable while a related letter holds a placement made by hand for a mark that is back at the suggestion here: after *Reset diacritic* on a letter whose bad placement went to all, *Apply to related letters* puts theirs back too, and the log says so. Each letter's mark is then measured against its letter and its other marks, outlines and all: one that overlaps or comes within 12 units (per 1000) is named in the log and in its row, to look at; *Reset diacritics* on its row puts it back. A mark drawn joined to its letter (an ogonek, a true cedilla, a horn) is judged the other way: touching its letter is how it should sit, and it is named when it does not touch it. The vertical caron stands beside its letter, like any other mark.
- *Line up marks above* (or *below*) sets every mark of the selected one's group at its height: the bottom of each mark above (the top of each mark below) level with the selected mark's. A group is the same side, the same case, and letters of the same height (ink tops within 6% of the em for marks above, bottoms for marks below), so *h*'s circumflex is not set as low as *e*'s. Stacked marks, conventions, and taller or shorter letters are left as they are, each reason named in the log with its letters (click one to select it). The moved marks count as moved by hand. Marks joined to their letter line up with their own kind only, by the edge fonts keep level (measured on the corpus's 140 Latin fonts): ogoneks by their bottoms (level across *A*, *E*, *I*, *U* in half the fonts exactly, three in four within 6/1000 of the em), true cedillas (*Ç*, *Ş*, *Ţ*: within 5) by their bottoms, horns (*Ơ*, *Ư*) and vertical carons (*ď*, *ľ*, *ť*; the capital *Ľ*'s sits 37/1000 lower, its own group by case) by their tops. Select one and the button reads *Line up ogoneks* (cedillas, horns, vertical carons).
- Under the list, *Same diacritic* shows the letters with one of the selected glyph's marks on the same side, both cases: click one to select it and see how the mark sits on it.
- *Also list existing glyphs* adds the letters with diacritics the font already has (Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic) in a section of their own, unchecked, each drawn beside its glyph as the font has it (*In the font*, *FreeLab*). Placements applied to related letters and lined-up marks reach them, so a font can be made consistent; only the checked ones are rebuilt, their outlines in each master replaced by the letter and marks as components. A rebuilt glyph keeps its own advance width where that is wider than its letter's (the room *ď*, *ľ*, and *ť* often keep for the vertical caron). Once built, it is listed unchecked again, so a later build does not rebuild it over edits made since. Placement already follows the font's own accented glyphs where it has them.
- The changes stay with the glyph as you go down the list, its row's picture follows, and they are built. A mark moved by hand moves by as much in every master, scaled about its own center.

*Restore suggestions* puts every check and every mark moved by hand back as the list first came.

## Reference

Left of the editor, the selected glyph's character is drawn in another font, to compare. At first that is *FreeLang*, the font FreeLab bundles for Font Check's lists (Noto subsets, with their mark features; it has 844 of the 846 letters with diacritics of the *All* set): Diacritics registers it with Qt through Font Check's own function, so it is there without Font Check having been opened. It is the first entry of the list, *FreeLang (default)*, above a line that sets it apart from the installed fonts (among which it is not listed a second time), as applications set recently used fonts over the others; each font's name is written in that font, the default's too (a font with no Latin letters, a symbol font, keeps the dialog's font: its name would not read in its own). Pick another font and the choice is remembered (*%LOCALAPPDATA%\FreeLab\diacritics.json*), pick the first entry again and the pick is taken back. The family's name is read from Font Check, never written in Diacritics. A pick saved by an earlier version is kept. The *Mark anchors* tab has the same list, and a pick on either tab is the choice of both. Where the bundled font cannot be registered, the reference starts as it did before: on the installed font, among families known for wide coverage (Noto Sans, Gentium Plus, Charis SIL, DejaVu Sans, Segoe UI, Arial, and others), with the most of the listed characters. A font that lacks the character (as QRawFont reads the font itself: Windows would otherwise draw another font's glyph under its name; a font QRawFont cannot read counts as lacking it) gives way to the font picked before it, or to the next of those families, and the note says so ("Fette Mikado lacks ĭ: shown in Noto Sans.").

The reference is filled as text is, by nonzero winding. It was filled by Qt's default even-odd rule before, which punched a hole wherever two glyphs of the text overlapped (a mark on a letter, a mark on a mark): that hole was the picture's, not the font's.

## The list, the log, and the History

- Double-click a row (or select it and click *Show in Glyph window*) to open its glyph between H's, n's, and o's in a Glyph window; a glyph not built yet opens its letter. The rows that cannot be built are selectable too: their character is drawn in the reference font.
- *Find a glyph*, over the list, shows only the rows whose glyph name or *Built from* text holds what is typed (A to Z in any case), whose character it is, or whose Unicode value it is (*U+0105*, *0105*, *105*), as it is typed, with the count beside it ("12 of 341 glyphs shown"). Rows not shown keep their checks, and *Build checked glyphs* builds every checked glyph, shown or not: while a search hides checked rows, the button says so ("Build 12 checked glyphs (5 not shown)"). The *Mark anchors* tab has the same field over its list (name, anchors, note, character, Unicode value).
- *Check all* and *Check none* act on the rows shown. *Check only high-value glyphs* checks, among the rows shown, the glyphs Font Check lists under *High-value missing glyphs* for this font (what the languages already within reach miss), and unchecks the others: gaps are closed without new ones being opened. The set comes from Font Check's own functions, with its window's settings where it is open and the settings it starts with otherwise; it is read once the list is shown, and again after a build. The button is grayed while it is read, when Font Check's data cannot be read (the log says why), and when no row shown is in the set or the checks are so already.
- Glyphs handed over by Font Check (*From Font Check* in *Character set:*) are each accounted for: a glyph is a row, or the top line and the log say how many are not listed and why, each named (the font has it already, which is the case once it is built; its script has no letters in the font; it is not built from a letter and marks; it is not one character). In the log, a glyph the font has is a link.
- The *Built from* cells and the notes of both lists are written as short notes: no period, and data in parentheses ("A + gravecomb (suggested: mark 25 right, 4 up)", "A + dieresis + macron (gaps closed to fit a 120% line)"). A test walks every row a made-up font of some ninety glyphs produces, in both lists, and fails on a cell that ends with a period or holds a dash or an interpunct.
- *Correct mirrored components* sets right the mirrored components of the glyphs listed under *Also list existing glyphs* (those shown), on a click, as one step of the History (see *Limits* for what a mirrored component does). A component cannot be reversed where it stands: its contours are its glyph's. So the mark is mirrored into a glyph of its own, with its contours reversed, named after it (*caron.flipped* for a mark turned upside down, *comma.mirrored* for one mirrored left to right; a number is added where that name is another glyph's, and a numbered glyph an earlier run made is used again when it still matches), made once, in every master, and used by every letter that needs it; each letter's component then points to it with what is left of its transform, which mirrors nothing. The letters stay glyphs of components and look as they did. (Decomposing the mark into each letter would fill the hole too, but would leave as many copies to keep alike as there are letters, and no composites: that way was not taken.) The corrected glyph is a copy: redrawing the mark later does not redraw it. Everything is read back: the corrected glyph's nodes against the mirror of the mark's, each contour's direction against the mark's, the letter's picture against what it was; where anything differs, the glyph is left as it was, the log says why, and the note stays in its row. A letter is set right in every master or in none: every master is planned first, and when one cannot be done (the mark has no outline there, or its read-back differs), the masters already written are put back, so the masters keep matching. A *Build* follows the same rule for a mirrored part, and takes out the corrected glyphs it made that no built glyph ended up using. A mark that is itself made of components is left (decompose it first), and only the current master is looked at to tell which glyphs have mirrored components.
- *Check kerning of these glyphs* opens Kerning Check on the pairs with the glyphs built since the dialog opened (their marks may collide with their neighbors').
- The log under the editor says what each build did: done in green, warnings in amber, errors in red. A letter named in it to look at is a link: a click selects its row. A glyph built since has left the list: the click then lists its row, unchecked, without switching *Also list existing glyphs* on, and selects it there, or, where that would not list it (another character set chosen since), opens it in a Glyph window.
- Building is one step of FontLab's History, the font's list of glyphs included: *Edit > Undo* takes the new glyphs back. The dialog reads the font again after an undo, a redo, or an edit made in FontLab, keeping its checks (but for the glyphs the font has, built or rebuilt ones included, which come back unchecked) and the marks moved by hand. Another font is read afresh, nothing carried over.
- New glyphs are added at the end of the font, and FontLab's Font window shows them at once.

On the owner's test font (no anchors; 106 composites, most of them with marks placed by hand), 269 glyphs can be built, 93 of them with a mark below borrowed from above and 12 with a mark drafted from punctuation, and 59 need a mark the font lacks.

## Limits

- Ligature-like letters (*ǆ*, *ĳ*) and letters with no decomposition (*ŧ*, *ø*) are not built: they are drawn.
- Marks are made smaller, not redrawn: a stack that needs flatter marks (as fonts draw for Vietnamese capitals) is better drawn by hand.
- A mark that always attaches (cedilla, ogonek) attaches as the corpus's do; a drafted cedilla (a comma) is only a placeholder.
- A mark that joins its letter (a horn, an ogonek, a cedilla) overlaps it, as two components, and Diacritics does not merge them: that is the export's matter. FontLab 7's export profiles have *Remove Overlaps*, which its manual says is needed for the non-variable final formats and not for variable ones. An OpenType PS font has no components (its glyphs are exported as outlines), so that option merges the mark and its letter. An OpenType TT font keeps them as components by the profile's *TTF: use composites*: components kept are not merged, TrueType allows them to overlap, and rasterizers fill the overlap as long as both are wound the same way, though text set outlined or stroked shows where they cross; decomposing the composites lets them be merged. A variable font keeps its overlaps, and the `glyf` flags `OVERLAP_SIMPLE` and `OVERLAP_COMPOUND` tell the rasterizers that need telling (Apple's) to fill them: check the exported font for them.
- Overlapping parts fill only while they are wound the same way. A mirrored component (scaled by a negative number one way, not both) has its contours reversed, and where it overlaps another part a renderer filling by nonzero winding leaves a hole. Diacritics writes none. What it turns upside down, it turns by a half turn. Where a mirror would still come from the font (a glyph a mark is drafted from that is itself a single mirrored component: a `^` made of a flipped caron, which the build replaces by the glyph it points to, the two transforms combined), the build puts the mark's corrected glyph in its place, mirrored with its contours reversed, as *Correct mirrored components* does (see *The list, the log, and the History*), and the log says which glyph was added; only where that glyph cannot be made is the mirrored component written, and named in the log with what to do. A glyph the font has whose own components are mirrored is named in its row when *Also list existing glyphs* is on, and *Correct mirrored components* sets it right when you click it; a glyph you leave keeps its note.

  When the hole shows was measured, on 2026-11-13, on small fonts built by script (fontTools 4.65): a square base, a bar-shaped mark laid over its top edge, as a composite glyph and as one glyph of outlines (contours copied in, overlaps kept), in TrueType and (outlines only, CFF having no components) in CFF. Each was rendered large by FreeType 2.14.3 (through PIL), drawn through HarfBuzz 11.2.0's outlines, and drawn by Windows GDI into a bitmap, and the overlap looked at:

  | The mark | TrueType, a component | TrueType, outlines | CFF, outlines |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | as it is | filled | filled | filled |
  | mirrored one way (across or up) | **hole** | **hole** | **hole** |
  | turned by a half turn | filled | filled | filled |
  | mirrored, its contours reversed | filled | filled | filled |

  FreeType, HarfBuzz, and GDI (antialiased or ClearType) agree on every cell. So the hole shows exactly where two overlapping contours run opposite ways, in either format, and none of the three sets a mirrored component's direction right by itself: a composite shows the hole just as its outlines do. One more case stands apart: GDI without antialiasing draws a CFF font with a hole at every overlap, whichever way its contours run (all four rows), which no direction can mend; in a CFF font the overlaps have to be removed. The FreeType part is kept as a test (`HoleRenderingTests`, run where fontTools and PIL are installed); HarfBuzz and GDI were run once, by the same script with two more renderers.

  What FontLab does at export was not measured: FontLab cannot be run from the tests. The owner's report is that fonts exported from FontLab showed these holes and that *Remove Overlaps* mended them. That agrees with the table: once the overlap is removed, no two contours are left to run opposite ways over each other, whichever way they ran. It does not tell whether FontLab keeps a mirrored component as a component in a TrueType export (kept components are not merged, as the item above says) or decomposes it, nor whether it sets its direction right when it does; so a font with mirrored components is best corrected in the font, and an exported font looked at where a mark meets its letter.
- The corpus model is measured on Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic composites (250 families); a Greek capital's left-hand marks are left out of it, as they are not above it.

## Mark anchors

Scripts whose marks are not precomposed (Khmer, Thai, Lao, Myanmar, Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, Bengali, Gujarati, Tamil, and the combining marks of any script) are set by the font's mark features: each mark's anchor meets its base's. The *Mark anchors* tab places them, for the script you pick among those the font has marks of:

- **Bases** (the script's letters that take marks, and their unencoded forms, *beh-ar.init* or *i.2*, by the name before the period) get *top* and *bottom* anchors, every one of them (a combining mark goes on letters Unicode has no precomposed form for: a dot below *p* or *x*); in Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic, the side anchors only where their precomposed letters need them (*a*: *ogonek*; *o* and *u*: *horn* too), and the list sorts the letters by that (*Marks above and below*, *With side marks too*). Marks stack on each other by the marks' own anchors, so a letter whose precomposed forms stack marks (Vietnamese's *ế*) takes no other anchors and has no section of its own. Which letters take marks: in Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic, those Unicode composes letters with marks on (*a*, *w*, *ø*, *ł*, the dotless *ı*), not those it has precomposed already (*ẅ*) nor those it composes nothing on (*ª*, *ƒ*); in every script, those at least half the corpus's fonts having them attach marks to (Thai's 46 consonants, not its vowels). A script the corpus says nothing of (Myanmar) takes every letter. The top line counts the letters left out; **marks** (Unicode's nonspacing marks) get the anchor they attach by (*_top* or *_bottom*, by where their ink sits: above or below half the x-height) and the one the next mark attaches to (*top* or *bottom*), for mark-to-mark. Anchors a glyph has already are kept; its row names those the marks attach by (*top*, *bottom*, a font's own *above* where its marks have *_above*), not the others it may carry, which say nothing of where marks go. A mark attached by an anchor of its own (a horn's *_topright*, an ogonek's *_bottom*) takes no other: in two mark classes, fontTools' compiler would refuse it.
- **Hebrew's points** attach where most of the corpus's Hebrew fonts attach them (27 of its 30 Hebrew families attach their points by anchors): the dagesh (and mapiq) by *_center*, inside the letter, every base getting a *center* anchor where the fonts land the dagesh in that letter (in its counter: 31% across and halfway up, as the median of its letters); the holam and the sin dot by *_topleft*, at the letter's top left; the shin dot by *_topright*, which shin alone gets, at its right head. Of the cantillation marks (10 families have them, and all 10 attach them), pashta and telisha qetana go at the top left too, yetiv and dehi at the bottom right (*_bottomright*), the others over or under the letter like the vowel points. Nothing stacks on these marks, so they get no anchor of their own for the next mark. Letters get these anchors only when the font has marks that use them.
- **Ligatures** (Arabic's *lam with alef* and the like: an encoded presentation form such as U+FEFB, a *uniXXXXYYYY* name, or a name of joined letters, *lam_alef-ar*) get *top_1*, *bottom_1*, *top_2*, *bottom_2* (a pair per letter they join; the first letter, read first, is on the right), and the code attaches marks to each part (`pos ligature`). Two-part ligatures take their parts' anchors where the corpus's fonts set theirs: 72% and 22% across, from 19 fonts' mark-to-ligature anchors; longer ones are split evenly.
- **The reph** (the ra over the consonant it comes before, in Devanagari, Bengali, and Gujarati: *rephdeva*, *Reph.dv*, *dvReph*, *Reph.bn*, *bn_reph*, *gjReph*, *rephgujr*, *reph-gujarati*, a ra and virama by their code points such as *uni09B009CD*, and the like, not Marathi's eyelash ra; a reph whose name names another script is left to that script) gets *_reph*, every base a *reph* anchor where the corpus's fonts of that script land it, level with its top: Devanagari's 78% across the consonant (32 fonts), Bengali's 89% (11), Gujarati's 84% (11).
- **Where**: a base's anchor where the corpus's fonts land a mark on that very letter, shaped through HarfBuzz with all their means at once (anchors, forms they substitute, contextual positioning), from at least 3 fonts: on Thai's *po pla*, *fo fa*, and *fo fan*, a mark above sits left of the ascender, not over it. A letter the corpus has too little of takes its script's rule: over (under) the center of its ink's top (bottom) fifth. Latin takes the letter's placement across only, and its script's rule for the height, which landed marks closer. A mark's anchor sits under (over) the center of its ink. Both by how the corpus's fonts of that script place theirs: *diacritics/train/anchors.py* reads the *mark*, *abvm*, and *blwm* features of 260 open-licensed fonts (Noto Sans and Serif Khmer, Thai, Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, Bengali, Gujarati, Tamil, Battambang, Hanuman, Sarabun, Kanit, Amiri, Cairo, Frank Ruhl Libre, Heebo, Hind and its Bengali, Gujarati, and Tamil kin, Mukta, Poppins, and others) into *diacritics/anchor_model.json*. Thai and Devanagari set their marks right of center (Thai's top anchor at 43% of the base's width right of center, its marks' at their right edge), and Bengali and Gujarati too; Arabic 0.10 em above the ink, Khmer 0.07 em. A letter's placement is taken from the fonts attaching that script's marks only: a font that leaves them where they are drawn says nothing of where they go. Where a script's marks are set off their center (Thai's), a base's anchor is set so that the mark sampled (Thai's *mai ek*) lands where the fonts land it, and the others sit as their own anchors say.
- **Reference**: beside it, the selected glyph's letter carrying the sample marks at once, one below and one above, and, where marks stack on it (a letter whose precomposed forms stack them, or any letter of a script that composes none, such as Thai), a second mark on the one above (on *e*, for instance, a dot below, a circumflex, and an acute), then the letter with each side mark; a mark is shown on the sample letter, alone and with another mark on it. All in the reference font (the tab has the first tab's list of fonts, one choice for both), as that font's own features place them, falling back as the first tab's does, then to fonts of that script (Noto Sans Thai, Noto Serif Hebrew, Noto Sans Bengali, Hind Vadodara, Leelawadee UI, Nirmala UI, and so on). An example is drawn only as the font can draw it: a letter and marks Unicode has one character for is drawn as that character (*Ấ* for *A* with a circumflex and an acute); what stays a letter with marks is kept when the font sets the marks on the letter, which Diacritics tells from what Qt would draw (the example's ink against its letter's alone, and its advance against the letter's: FontLab has no shaper to ask); else it is tried with one mark fewer, and left out when the font sets not even one. No mark is ever shown standing beside its letter, and the line under the picture says what was left off.
- **By hand**: the selected glyph is drawn with a mark attached; drag the mark, or press the arrow keys (Shift: 10 units), to move the glyph's anchor (a base's where the mark should sit, a mark's own so that it sits as shown). A mark is drawn with another mark on it too: drag that one to move the anchor the next mark attaches to (mark to mark). A letter where marks stack is drawn with a second mark on its mark above, which follows the first.
- **Moving anchors**: drag a sample mark (or press the arrow keys) to move the anchor it hangs on, one to add or one the glyph has already (after adding, for instance); *Reset anchors* puts the selected glyph's back, in the list and the picture. A moved anchor of the glyph's own is written as a move. Checks and moves stay when the list is read again (another script, after writing), for the same font only; a move once written leaves the list (the font has it), and one an error stopped stays. A mark attached by an anchor of its own of another side (an ogonek kept on its *_bottom*) is drawn hanging on that anchor.
- **The code**: *Add checked anchors* adds them (and moves the glyph's own, where moved by hand) in every master, each measured on its own; a glyph with anchors to add only in another master (Bold lacking what Regular has) is listed checked, its row saying so, and written there, the current master left as it is; and, for a font with one master, writes the *mark* and *mkmk* features between FreeLab's marker lines (`markClass` per mark, `pos base` per base, `pos ligature` per ligature, `pos mark` per mark), and the checked substitutions (*ccmp*, *pres*), checked by fontTools' feature parser first. With several masters, the code is left to FontLab's automatic features, which read each master's anchors.

A script's anchor heights come from at least 3 fonts of it; fewer (Greek's and Cyrillic's marks below, and Tamil's, which has none below) give way to Latin's. With one script, the *Script:* choice is a plain name.

Tried on fonts of the corpus, their own anchors taken away, the marks land a median 31 units (Noto Sans Thai) to 47 units (Sarabun) across and 27 to 35 up from where the fonts put them; Arabic is roughest (36 across, 117 up in Amiri, whose marks keep heights of their own). The anchors are a start, to adjust by eye.

Each font of the corpus left out in turn of the per-letter placements, the sample mark lands this far from where that font puts it (median over the letters of the fonts attaching that script's marks, in units of a 1000-unit em; across, then up; by the script's rule alone in parentheses): Thai 11 and 15 above (19 and 27), Devanagari 18 and 19 (45 and 24), Khmer 11 and 5 (23 and 19), Arabic 44 and 61 (78 and 59), Latin 37 and 29 (53 and 29); Hebrew 18 and 14 below (16 and 17), 23 and 20 above (27 and 17), 31 and 25 for the dagesh (55 and 32), 28 and 27 for the holam (40 and 30), 16 and 27 for the shin dot (50 and 31); Bengali 42 and 26 above (83 and 45), 51 and 28 below (86 and 23); Gujarati 49 and 21 above (89 and 54), 51 and 32 below (89 and 29); Tamil's pulli 26 and 25 (78 and 219). The scripts measured before land as they did (Latin closer across, 37 against 41, now that fonts not attaching Latin marks are left out of its letters).

**Substitutions**: the forms some scripts take beside their marks, written when the font has them (a list section of their own, each checkable):

- *Thai* (`ccmp`), by the convention 25 of the corpus's 27 Thai families follow: a tone mark after a mark above takes its smaller form (*.small*); *yo ying* and *tho than* their forms without the tail a vowel below would meet (*.less*), *do chada*, *to patak*, *ru*, and *lu* their shorter forms (*.short*); *lo chula* its shorter form under a mark above. The forms are found by the letter's name in the font, Glyphs' name for it (*yoYing-thai*), or *uni0E0D*, case and hyphens aside. The narrower marks some fonts keep for *po pla*'s ascender are not needed: the anchors set marks beside it.
- *Devanagari*, *Gujarati*, and *Bengali* (`pres`): before each consonant, the i-matra whose hook reaches into it as far as the corpus's fonts with several i-matras reach into that consonant (Devanagari's 56% of *ka*'s advance, 88% of *kha*'s, from 31 fonts; Gujarati's 63% and 85%, from 10 or 11), save before the consonants most of those fonts keep their nominal i-matra before (Bengali's, but for *tta*, *la*, and a few others). Its reach is how far its ink goes past its advance. The i-matras are the font's glyphs named after its own (*uni093F.01*, *iMatra-deva.a03*, *gjmI.alt02*, *uni09BF.long01*), Glyphs' name for it with any common tag of the script (*iMatra-gujarati*, *iMatra-gujr*), or its name with other digits (*MatraI3.gj* beside *MatraI.gj*, *ivowelsign2gujr* beside *ivowelsigngujr*). Most of the corpus's Gujarati families draw several (11 of 12), against 6 of its 11 Bengali ones, those for their longer consonants only.
- *Hebrew*: none. Only 6 of the 29 Hebrew families with precomposed letters (*U+FB1D* to *U+FB4E*) put them in for a letter and its point; the others attach the point, as the anchors do.
- *Tamil*: none; its vowel signs that join a consonant (*u*, *uu*) are forms the font substitutes, not marks.

Tried offline on corpus fonts (FreeLab's mark features compiled with fontTools in place of the font's own positioning, its own substitutions kept, and shaped with HarfBuzz), the sample marks land a median 4 to 11 units across and 0 to 25 up from where Sarabun's, Noto Sans Devanagari's, and Noto Sans Khmer's own features put them; Hebrew's points 9 to 25 units across and 13 to 34 up (Noto Serif Hebrew, Frank Ruhl Libre, Heebo, David Libre: the median of their medians), Bengali's 16 to 34 and 6 to 22 (Noto Sans Bengali, Hind Siliguri, Tiro Bangla), Gujarati's 17 to 52 and 5 to 9 (Noto Sans Gujarati, Hind Vadodara, Rasa), and Tamil's pulli 18 and 21 in Catamaran (Noto Sans Tamil and Mukta Malar join their consonants and pulli in one glyph). The reph lands 8 to 74 units across from where Noto Sans Devanagari's, the Bengali, and the Gujarati fonts' own does: one share across for each script, where fonts differ. The i-matras picked reach within a median 0 to 10 units of the fonts' own picks in Noto Sans Devanagari, Noto Sans Bengali, Hind Siliguri, Tiro Bangla, and Hind Vadodara (the nominal i-matra alone: up to 71); in Noto Sans Gujarati and Rasa, whose lengths follow their own design, 81 and 53 (88 and 26). *diacritics/train/shaped.py* does the shaping for *anchors.py*; the corpus took 73 more open-licensed Thai, Arabic, and Devanagari families from Google Fonts for it (Kanit, Prompt, Sarabun's kin, Cairo, Tajawal, Vazirmatn, Scheherazade New, Poppins, Mukta, Teko, and others), then 65 Hebrew, Bengali, Gujarati, and Tamil ones: every such family Google Fonts has with an OFL or Apache name table (29 Hebrew, 10 Bengali, 11 Gujarati, 15 Tamil; Alef, whose name table names no license, was left out), the Rubik display variants aside.
